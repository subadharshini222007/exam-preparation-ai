
## Unit 1: India — Location, Size and Boundaries

### 1.1 Location of India

India lies entirely in the **Northern Hemisphere**. Its mainland extends roughly from **8°4′ N to 37°6′ N latitude** and from **68°7′ E to 97°25′ E longitude**. [toppersnotes-main.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws](https://toppersnotes-main.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/08124021/Volume-2-Geography-of-India-TNPSC-ENG-08-07.pdf)

- India is located in **South Asia**.
- The Tropic of Cancer, at **23°30′ N**, passes through eight Indian states.
- These states are Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura and Mizoram.
- India’s southernmost mainland point is **Kanyakumari** in Tamil Nadu.
- The southernmost point of India including island territories is **Indira Point** in the Great Nicobar Island.
- The northernmost point is near **Indira Col** in the Ladakh region.
- The westernmost point is near **Guhar Moti** in Gujarat.
- The easternmost point is near **Kibithu** in Arunachal Pradesh.

### 1.2 Size of India

- Total geographical area: about **32.87 lakh sq. km** or **3.287 million sq. km**.
- India is the **seventh largest country in the world** by area.
- India accounts for about **2.4%** of the world’s total geographical area.
- India is the **second most populous country** in the world.
- India’s north-south extent is about **3,214 km**.
- India’s east-west extent is about **2,933 km**.
- The north-south extent is greater than the east-west extent.
- The latitudinal and longitudinal extent of India is approximately **30 degrees**.
- Though the angular extent is similar, the north-south distance is longer because lines of longitude converge toward the poles.

### 1.3 Indian Standard Time

- India follows one standard time zone: **Indian Standard Time (IST)**.
- IST is based on the **82°30′ E longitude**.
- This meridian passes through **Mirzapur in Uttar Pradesh**.
- IST is **5 hours 30 minutes ahead of Greenwich Mean Time (GMT)**.
- India has a longitudinal extent of nearly 30 degrees, which equals a time difference of about two hours between eastern and western parts.
- To avoid confusion, one standard meridian is used for the entire country.

### 1.4 Land Boundaries

India shares land boundaries with **six countries**:

| Country | Bordering Indian states |
|---|---|
| Pakistan | Jammu & Kashmir, Ladakh, Punjab, Rajasthan, Gujarat |
| Afghanistan | Ladakh |
| China | Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh |
| Nepal | Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Sikkim |
| Bhutan | Sikkim, West Bengal, Assam, Arunachal Pradesh |
| Bangladesh | West Bengal, Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram |
| Myanmar | Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram |

- India has the longest land border with **Bangladesh**.
- India’s shortest international land border is with **Afghanistan**.
- Sri Lanka is separated from India by the **Palk Strait** and the **Gulf of Mannar**.
- Maldives lies to the south-west of India in the Indian Ocean.

### 1.5 Important Exam Points

- India is called a **subcontinent** because it has diverse physical features, climate, vegetation, soils and cultures.
- The Himalayas protect India from cold Central Asian winds.
- The Indian Ocean provides India with a central location for trade between East Asia, Africa and Europe.
- India’s location between West Asia and East Asia gives it major strategic and commercial importance.

## Unit 2: Physical Divisions of India

India is divided into **six major physical divisions**. [toppersnotes-main.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws](https://toppersnotes-main.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/08124021/Volume-2-Geography-of-India-TNPSC-ENG-08-07.pdf)

1. The Himalayan Mountains  
2. The Great Northern Plains  
3. The Peninsular Plateau  
4. The Indian Desert  
5. The Coastal Plains  
6. The Islands  

### 2.1 Himalayan Mountains

- Located in the north.
- Young fold mountains formed by the collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates.
- Extend from Jammu & Kashmir to Arunachal Pradesh.
- Act as a climatic barrier and source of many perennial rivers.

### 2.2 Northern Plains

- Formed by alluvial deposits brought by the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra river systems.
- Highly fertile and densely populated.
- Extend from Punjab to Assam.

### 2.3 Peninsular Plateau

- Oldest physical division of India.
- Made mainly of hard crystalline, igneous and metamorphic rocks.
- Triangular in shape, with its base along the northern edge and apex near Kanyakumari.

### 2.4 Indian Desert

- Located in western Rajasthan and adjoining parts of Gujarat, Haryana and Punjab.
- Characterised by arid climate, sand dunes and low rainfall.

### 2.5 Coastal Plains

- Narrow plains along the Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal.
- Important for ports, agriculture, fishing and trade.

### 2.6 Islands

- Two major island groups: **Andaman & Nicobar Islands** and **Lakshadweep Islands**.

## Unit 3: The Himalayan Mountains

### 3.1 Formation and Extent

The Himalayas are young fold mountains formed due to the collision between the Indian Plate and the Eurasian Plate. They extend about **2,400 km** from the Indus gorge in the west to the Brahmaputra gorge in the east. [toppersnotes-main.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws](https://toppersnotes-main.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/08124021/Volume-2-Geography-of-India-TNPSC-ENG-08-07.pdf)

- The Himalayas are the highest mountain range in the world.
- Mount Everest, the world’s highest peak, lies in the Himalayas.
- Its height is **8,848.86 m**.
- The Himalayas are geologically young and structurally unstable.
- Earthquakes are relatively common in this region.

### 3.2 Three Parallel Ranges

| Range | Other name | Average height | Important peaks |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Greater Himalayas | Himadri | Above 6,000 m | Everest, Kanchenjunga, Nanda Devi |
| Lesser Himalayas | Himachal | 3,700–4,500 m | Dhauladhar, Pir Panjal, Mahabharat |
| Outer Himalayas | Shiwaliks | 900–1,100 m | Dundwa, Churia hills |

### 3.3 Greater Himalayas

- Also called **Himadri**.
- Northernmost and highest range.
- Contains most of the world’s highest peaks.
- Mostly covered with snow throughout the year.
- Important passes include Nathu La, Shipki La, Bomdi La, Khardung La and Zoji La.

### 3.4 Lesser Himalayas

- Also called **Himachal**.
- Located south of the Greater Himalayas.
- Important hill stations include Shimla, Mussoorie, Nainital, Darjeeling and Almora.
- Famous valleys include Kashmir Valley, Kangra Valley and Kullu Valley.

### 3.5 Shiwaliks

- Outermost range of the Himalayas.
- Composed of unconsolidated river deposits.
- Broad valleys between Shiwaliks and Lesser Himalayas are called **Duns**.
- Examples: Dehra Dun, Kotli Dun and Patli Dun.

### 3.6 Regional Divisions

| Division | Extent |
|---|---|
| Punjab Himalayas | Between Indus and Satluj rivers |
| Kumaon Himalayas | Between Satluj and Kali rivers |
| Nepal Himalayas | Between Kali and Teesta rivers |
| Assam Himalayas | Between Teesta and Dihang rivers |

## Unit 4: Northern Plains of India

The Northern Plains were formed by the alluvial deposits of the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra rivers. They extend over about **2,400 km** from east to west and are among the most fertile regions of the world. [tncareerguidance](https://tncareerguidance.in/Indian-Geography--Syllabus)

### 4.1 Formation

- Rivers originating in the Himalayas carry large quantities of sediments.
- These sediments were deposited over millions of years.
- The plain is extremely fertile because of alluvial soil.
- It supports intensive agriculture and a high population density.

### 4.2 Regional Divisions

| Division | States / region |
|---|---|
| Punjab Plain | Punjab, Haryana, parts of Rajasthan |
| Ganga Plain | Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal |
| Brahmaputra Plain | Assam |

### 4.3 Relief Features

- **Bhabar:** Narrow belt at the foothills where streams disappear into porous boulder deposits.
- **Terai:** Marshy region where streams reappear.
- **Bhangar:** Older alluvium, containing calcareous deposits called kankar.
- **Khadar:** Newer alluvium, renewed almost every year by floods; very fertile.

### 4.4 Importance

- Major food-producing region of India.
- Wheat, rice, sugarcane, pulses and oilseeds are widely cultivated.
- Dense transport network and major cities are located here.

## Unit 5: Peninsular Plateau

The Peninsular Plateau is the oldest and most stable landmass of India. It is a tableland composed of old crystalline, igneous and metamorphic rocks. [toppersnotes-main.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws](https://toppersnotes-main.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/08124021/Volume-2-Geography-of-India-TNPSC-ENG-08-07.pdf)

### 5.1 Extent

- Bounded by the Aravalli Range in the north-west.
- Bounded by the Western Ghats in the west.
- Bounded by the Eastern Ghats in the east.
- Tapers toward the south and ends near Kanyakumari.

### 5.2 Major Divisions

| Division | Description |
|---|---|
| Central Highlands | North of Narmada river |
| Deccan Plateau | South of Narmada river |
| Chota Nagpur Plateau | Rich in minerals, especially coal and iron |
| Malwa Plateau | Located in Madhya Pradesh |
| Deccan Trap | Formed by volcanic lava flows |

### 5.3 Western Ghats

- Also called **Sahyadri**.
- Continuous range along the western edge of the Deccan Plateau.
- Higher than the Eastern Ghats.
- Important peaks: Anamudi, Doddabetta, Mahabaleshwar.
- Anamudi in Kerala is the highest peak in South India.

### 5.4 Eastern Ghats

- Discontinuous range along the eastern side of the peninsula.
- Cut by rivers such as Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri.
- Important hills: Nallamala, Jindhagada, Velikonda and Palkonda.

## Unit 6: Indian Desert

The Indian Desert lies mainly in western Rajasthan. It is part of the Thar Desert and extends into Gujarat, Haryana and Punjab. [toppersnotes-main.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws](https://toppersnotes-main.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/08124021/Volume-2-Geography-of-India-TNPSC-ENG-08-07.pdf)

- Rainfall is very low, generally below 150 mm annually.
- The Aravalli Range lies to its east.
- Luni is the only major river flowing through this region.
- Sand dunes, saline lakes and dry river beds are common.
- Important lakes include Sambhar and Didwana.

## Unit 7: Coastal Plains

India has two broad coastal plains: the **Western Coastal Plain** along the Arabian Sea and the **Eastern Coastal Plain** along the Bay of Bengal. [toppersnotes-main.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws](https://toppersnotes-main.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/08124021/Volume-2-Geography-of-India-TNPSC-ENG-08-07.pdf)

| Feature | Western Coastal Plain | Eastern Coastal Plain |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Between Western Ghats and Arabian Sea | Between Eastern Ghats and Bay of Bengal |
| Width | Narrow | Wider |
| Rivers | Short and fast-flowing | Longer and form deltas |
| Major divisions | Konkan, Kanara, Malabar | Utkal, Andhra, Coromandel |
| Ports | Mumbai, Kochi, Goa | Chennai, Visakhapatnam, Kolkata |

- The western coast is suitable for ports because it is narrow and steep.
- The eastern coast is suitable for agriculture because it is broad and has fertile deltas.

## Unit 8: Islands of India

India has two major island groups:

- **Andaman and Nicobar Islands** in the Bay of Bengal.
- **Lakshadweep Islands** in the Arabian Sea.

### 8.1 Andaman and Nicobar Islands

- Comprise about **572 islands**.
- Located in the Bay of Bengal.
- Barren Island is India’s only active volcano.
- Narcondam is a dormant volcano.
- The southernmost point of India, Indira Point, is in Great Nicobar.

### 8.2 Lakshadweep Islands

- Coral islands located in the Arabian Sea.
- Formerly called Laccadive, Minicoy and Amindivi Islands.
- Kavaratti is the capital.
- Coral reefs are an important ecological feature.

## Unit 9: Drainage System and River Basins

A drainage system refers to the pattern formed by a river, its tributaries and sub-tributaries. Indian rivers are broadly divided into **Himalayan rivers** and **Peninsular rivers**. [toppersnotes-main.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws](https://toppersnotes-main.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/08124021/Volume-2-Geography-of-India-TNPSC-ENG-08-07.pdf)

### 9.1 Himalayan vs Peninsular Rivers

| Feature | Himalayan Rivers | Peninsular Rivers |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Himalayas | Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats, plateau |
| Nature of flow | Perennial | Seasonal |
| Water source | Snow and rainfall | Mainly rainfall |
| Valleys | Deep gorges and V-shaped valleys | Broad and shallow valleys |
| Deltas | Large deltas | Deltas or estuaries |
| Examples | Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra | Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Narmada |

### 9.2 Drainage Patterns

- **Dendritic pattern:** Tree-like branching pattern.
- **Trellis pattern:** Streams follow parallel valleys.
- **Radial pattern:** Rivers flow outward from a central highland.
- **Centripetal pattern:** Rivers flow toward a central lake or depression.

## Unit 10: Himalayan Rivers

### 10.1 Indus River System

- Originates near Lake Mansarovar in Tibet.
- Enters India through Ladakh.
- Major tributaries: Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas and Satluj.
- The Indus Water Treaty allocates the Indus system largely between India and Pakistan.

### 10.2 Ganga River System

- Originates as **Bhagirathi** from the Gangotri Glacier.
- Alaknanda joins Bhagirathi at Devprayag to form the Ganga.
- Major tributaries: Yamuna, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi, Son and Chambal.
- The Ganga forms the world’s largest delta with the Brahmaputra—the **Sundarbans Delta**.

### 10.3 Brahmaputra River System

- Originates in Tibet as **Tsangpo**.
- Enters India as **Siang/Dihang** in Arunachal Pradesh.
- Known as **Jamuna** in Bangladesh.
- Causes frequent floods in Assam due to heavy rainfall and sediment deposition.

## Unit 13: Climate, Monsoon and Rainfall

India has a tropical monsoon type of climate. The word **monsoon** is derived from the Arabic word *mausim*, meaning season. [tnpsc.gov](https://tnpsc.gov.in/static_pdf/syllabus/final_g2_2a_syll_english.pdf)

### 13.1 Factors Affecting Climate

- Latitude
- Altitude
- Distance from the sea
- Western disturbances
- Himalayan mountains
- El Nino and La Nina

### 13.2 Seasons

1. Cold weather season: December to February  
2. Hot weather season: March to May  
3. Southwest monsoon season: June to September  
4. Retreating monsoon season: October to November  

### 13.3 Southwest Monsoon

- Begins in Kerala around **1 June**.
- Divides into the **Arabian Sea branch** and the **Bay of Bengal branch**.
- The Arabian Sea branch strikes the Western Ghats and causes heavy rainfall in Kerala, coastal Karnataka and Konkan.
- The Bay of Bengal branch moves toward north-east India and the Gangetic plain.
- Mawsynram in Meghalaya receives the highest rainfall in India.

### 13.4 Retreating Monsoon

- Occurs during October and November.
- Tamil Nadu receives most of its rainfall during this season.
- This rainfall is called **north-east monsoon rainfall**.
- Cyclones are common in the Bay of Bengal during this period.
