
PART B - UNIT 6: SOIL FORMATION AND CLASSIFICATION

1. INTRODUCTION

Soil is the uppermost layer of the earth's crust.
It supports plant growth and agriculture.

Soil formation is a long process influenced by climate, parent rock, relief, organisms
and time.

2. FACTORS OF SOIL FORMATION

1. Parent rock
2. Climate
3. Relief
4. Organisms
5. Time

2.1 Parent Rock

- Determines mineral content
- Example: Black soil from Deccan lava
- Example: Red soil from crystalline rocks

2.2 Climate

- Temperature and rainfall affect weathering
- High rainfall causes leaching
- High temperature speeds up chemical weathering

2.3 Relief

- Slope affects erosion
- Flat areas allow soil accumulation
- Steep slopes cause soil erosion

2.4 Organisms

- Plants add organic matter
- Microorganisms decompose organic matter
- Earthworms improve soil structure

2.5 Time

- Soil formation takes hundreds to thousands of years
- Mature soils have well developed horizons

3. CLASSIFICATION OF INDIAN SOILS

ICAR classifies Indian soils into eight major types:

1. Alluvial soil
2. Black soil
3. Red and yellow soil
4. Laterite soil
5. Arid soil
6. Forest and mountain soil
7. Peaty and marshy soil
8. Saline and alkaline soil

4. ALLUVIAL SOIL

- Most widespread and fertile soil
- Found in Northern Plains and river deltas
- Formed by river deposits
- Rich in potash and lime
- Deficient in nitrogen and humus
- Suitable for rice, wheat, sugarcane
- Khadar is newer alluvium
- Bhangar is older alluvium

5. BLACK SOIL

- Also called regur soil
- Formed from Deccan lava
- Rich in iron, lime, magnesium and alumina
- Retains moisture
- Best for cotton cultivation
- Found in Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Karnataka and parts of Tamil Nadu

6. RED AND YELLOW SOIL

- Formed from crystalline and metamorphic rocks
- Red colour due to iron oxide
- Yellow colour due to hydration of iron
- Deficient in nitrogen, phosphorus and humus
- Found in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Odisha

7. LATERITE SOIL

- Formed in areas of high rainfall and high temperature
- Intense leaching removes silica and bases
- Rich in iron and aluminium oxides
- Poor in fertility
- Suitable for tea, coffee and cashew
- Found in Western Ghats, Assam and Odisha hills

8. ARID SOIL

- Found in Rajasthan and adjoining areas
- Sandy and saline
- Low humus content
- Suitable for drought-resistant crops
- Example: bajra and pulses

9. FOREST AND MOUNTAIN SOIL

- Found in Himalayan slopes and forest areas
- Rich in humus and organic matter
- Thin and less fertile in higher altitudes
- Suitable for tea, coffee, spices and fruits

10. PEATY AND MARSHY SOIL

- Found in Kerala, coastal Odisha and parts of West Bengal
- High organic content
- Black, heavy and acidic
- Suitable for paddy cultivation after reclamation

11. SALINE AND ALKALINE SOIL

- Found in Rajasthan, Gujarat, Punjab and Haryana
- High salt content
- Poor fertility
- Caused by poor drainage and over-irrigation

12. SOIL EROSION AND CONSERVATION

Soil erosion is the removal of topsoil by wind and water.

Causes:
- Deforestation
- Overgrazing
- Faulty farming
- Heavy rainfall
- Wind

Conservation methods:
- Afforestation
- Contour ploughing
- Terrace farming
- Strip cropping
- Shelter belts
- Crop rotation

13. IMPORTANT FACTS

- Alluvial soil is most fertile and widespread
- Black soil is best for cotton
- Red soil is rich in iron
- Laterite soil is formed by leaching
- Arid soil is sandy and saline
- Forest soil is rich in humus
- Peaty soil is rich in organic matter
- Saline soil has high salt content
- ICAR classifies Indian soils into eight types
- Koppen classification is used for climatic regions

PART A - UNIT 7: MAJOR SOIL TYPES OF INDIA

1. INTRODUCTION

India has diverse soil types due to variations in climate, parent rock, relief and
vegetation.

ICAR classifies Indian soils into eight major types:

1. Alluvial soil
2. Black soil
3. Red and yellow soil
4. Laterite soil
5. Arid soil
6. Forest and mountain soil
7. Peaty and marshy soil
8. Saline and alkaline soil

2. ALLUVIAL SOIL

2.1 Formation

- Formed by deposits of rivers
- Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra river systems
- Also formed in coastal deltas

2.2 Distribution

- Northern Plains from Punjab to Assam
- River valleys
- Coastal deltas
- Eastern coastal plains

2.3 Characteristics

- Most fertile and widespread soil
- Rich in potash and lime
- Deficient in nitrogen and humus
- Loamy to clayey texture
- Highly suitable for agriculture

2.4 Types

- Khadar: Newer alluvium, renewed by floods
- Bhangar: Older alluvium, contains kankar

2.5 Crops

- Rice
- Wheat
- Sugarcane
- Jute
- Pulses
- Oilseeds

3. BLACK SOIL

3.1 Formation

- Formed from weathering of Deccan lava (basalt)

3.2 Distribution

- Deccan Plateau
- Maharashtra
- Madhya Pradesh
- Gujarat
- Parts of Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh

3.3 Characteristics

- Rich in iron, lime, magnesium and alumina
- Retains moisture
- Develops cracks in dry season
- Poor in nitrogen, phosphorus and humus

3.4 Crops

- Cotton
- Sugarcane
- Tobacco
- Oilseeds
- Pulses

4. RED AND YELLOW SOIL

4.1 Formation

- Formed from crystalline and metamorphic rocks
- Red colour due to iron oxide
- Yellow colour due to hydration of iron

4.2 Distribution

- Tamil Nadu
- Karnataka
- Andhra Pradesh
- Odisha
- Chhattisgarh
- Parts of Maharashtra

4.3 Characteristics

- Coarse in upper slopes

4.4 Crops

- Millets
- Pulses
- Groundnut
- Potato
- Cotton

5. LATERITE SOIL

5.1 Formation

- Formed in areas of high rainfall and high temperature
- Intense leaching removes silica and bases
- Rich in iron and aluminium oxides

5.2 Distribution

- Western Ghats
- Karnataka
- Kerala
- Tamil Nadu
- Madhya Pradesh
- Assam hills
- Odisha hills

5.3 Characteristics

- Poor in fertility
- Rich in iron and aluminium
- Reddish colour
- Suitable for plantation crops after manuring

5.4 Crops

- Tea
- Coffee
- Cashew
- Rubber

6. ARID SOIL

6.1 Formation

- Formed in dry areas
- Wind deposited sand
- High salt content due to evaporation

6.2 Distribution

- Western Rajasthan
- Northern Gujarat
- Southern Punjab

6.3 Characteristics

- Sandy and saline
- Low humus and moisture
- High salt content
- Poor fertility

6.4 Crops

- Bajra
- Pulses
- Drought-resistant crops with irrigation

7. FOREST AND MOUNTAIN SOIL

7.1 Formation

- Formed from forest litter and organic matter
- Found in hilly and mountainous areas

7.2 Distribution

- Himalayan slopes
- Western Ghats
- Eastern Ghats

7.3 Characteristics

- Rich in humus and organic matter
- Thin and less fertile in higher altitudes
- Loamy to silty texture

7.4 Crops

- Tea
- Coffee
- Spices
- Fruits

8. PEATY AND MARSHY SOIL

8.1 Formation

- Formed in areas of high rainfall and poor drainage
- Accumulation of organic matter

8.2 Distribution

- Kerala
- Coastal Odisha
- Parts of West Bengal

8.3 Characteristics

- High organic content
- Black, heavy and acidic
- Suitable for paddy after reclamation

9. SALINE AND ALKALINE SOIL

9.1 Formation

- Caused by poor drainage and over-irrigation
- High salt content due to evaporation

9.2 Distribution

- Rajasthan
- Gujarat
- Punjab
- Haryana

9.3 Characteristics

- High salt content
- Poor fertility
- White crust on surface

10. IMPORTANT FACTS

- Red soil is rich in iron
- Laterite soil is formed by leaching
- Arid soil is sandy and saline
- Forest soil is rich in humus
- Peaty soil is rich in organic matter
- Saline soil has high salt content
- ICAR classifies Indian soils into eight types

PART B - UNIT 8: SOIL EROSION AND CONSERVATION

1. INTRODUCTION

Soil erosion is the removal of topsoil by wind and water.

Topsoil is the most fertile layer and its loss reduces soil productivity.

2. CAUSES OF SOIL EROSION

1. Deforestation
2. Overgrazing
3. Faulty farming methods
4. Heavy rainfall
5. Wind
6. Construction activities

3. TYPES OF SOIL EROSION

3.1 Water Erosion

- Caused by running water
- Includes sheet erosion, rill erosion and gully erosion

3.2 Wind Erosion

- Caused by strong winds
- Common in arid and semi-arid areas
- Example: Rajasthan desert

4. CONSERVATION METHODS

4.1 Afforestation

- Planting trees to reduce soil erosion
- Roots bind soil

4.2 Contour Ploughing

- Ploughing along contour lines
- Reduces speed of water flow
- Suitable for hilly areas
- Can reduce erosion by up to 50% on moderate slopes [81]

4.3 Terrace Farming

- Cutting steps on slopes
- Reduces runoff
- Suitable for Himalayan region [80][83]

4.4 Strip Cropping

- Growing grass between crop strips
- Reduces wind force
- Suitable for plains [83][84]

4.5 Shelter Belts

- Planting rows of trees
- Reduces wind speed
- Suitable for desert areas [83]

4.6 Crop Rotation

- Changing crops seasonally
- Maintains soil fertility

4.7 Controlled Grazing

- Prevents overgrazing
- Protects grass cover

5. IMPORTANT FACTS

- Soil erosion removes topsoil
- Water and wind are main agents
- Contour ploughing reduces water flow
- Terrace farming reduces runoff
- Strip cropping reduces wind force
- Shelter belts reduce wind speed
- Afforestation binds soil
- Crop rotation maintains fertility

PART A - UNIT 9: NATURAL VEGETATION - INTRODUCTION

1. INTRODUCTION

Natural vegetation refers to plant communities that grow without human interference.
It develops according to climate, soil, relief and rainfall.

India has diverse vegetation due to variations in:
- Rainfall
- Temperature
- Altitude
- Soil
- Relief

2. FACTORS AFFECTING NATURAL VEGETATION

2.1 Rainfall

- High rainfall supports dense forests
- Low rainfall supports thorn forests
- Very low rainfall supports desert vegetation

2.2 Temperature

- High temperature supports tropical forests
- Low temperature supports montane forests
- Very low temperature supports tundra vegetation

2.3 Altitude

- Vegetation changes with altitude
- Himalayas show clear altitudinal zonation

2.4 Soil

- Alluvial soil supports agriculture
- Laterite soil supports tea and coffee
- Black soil supports cotton

3. TYPES OF NATURAL VEGETATION IN INDIA

India has five major types of natural vegetation:

1. Tropical Evergreen Forests
2. Tropical Deciduous Forests
3. Tropical Thorn Forests and Scrubs
4. Montane Forests
5. Mangrove Forests

4. IMPORTANCE OF FORESTS

- Provide timber and fuel
- Regulate climate
- Prevent soil erosion
- Maintain water cycle
- Provide habitat for wildlife
- Support biodiversity

5. FOREST CONSERVATION

- Afforestation

Indian forests are classified based on:

1. Tropical Evergreen Forests
2. Tropical Deciduous Forests
3. Tropical Thorn Forests

2.1 Location

- Western Ghats
- North-East India

2.2 Rainfall

- Above 200 cm

- Dense and multi-layered

3.1 Location

- Most widespread forests in India
- Found in plains and lower hills

3.2 Rainfall

- Between 70 and 200 cm

3.3 Characteristics

- Trees shed leaves in dry season
- Also called monsoon forests
- Economically important

3.4 Types

- Moist Deciduous Forests: 100-200 cm rainfall
- Dry Deciduous Forests: 70-100 cm rainfall

- Teak

- Haryana

- Parts of Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh

4.2 Rainfall

- Below 50 cm

4.3 Characteristics

- Deep roots
- Small leaves
- Thorny plants

4.4 Important Species

- Babool
- Ber
- Khair
- Neem
- Khejri
- Cactus

5. MONTANE FORESTS

5.1 Location

- Himalayan region
- Western Ghats
- Nilgiris
- Vindhyas

5.2 Characteristics

5.3 Important Species

6. MANGROVE FORESTS

6.1 Location

- Coastal areas
- River deltas
- Sundarbans

- Mahanadi delta

6.2 Characteristics

- Have breathing roots (pneumatophores)
- Protect coast from cyclones and erosion

6.3 Important Species

- Sundari

- Keora
- Gewa

- Montane forests change with altitude
- Mangroves grow in saline coastal areas

PART A - UNIT 11: WILDLIFE OF INDIA

India is one of the world's megadiversity countries.
It has rich wildlife due to varied climate, vegetation and habitats.

India has:
- More than 90,000 animal species
- More than 45,000 plant species
- Rich biodiversity in forests, wetlands, deserts, mountains and coasts

- Found only in Gir National Park, Gujarat

- National heritage animal of India

- Conservation programme: Project Elephant
- Project Elephant launched in 1992

2.4 One-Horned Rhinoceros

- Found in Kaziranga National Park, Assam
- Endangered species
- Major conservation area: Kaziranga

2.5 Snow Leopard

- Found in Himalayan region
- Endangered species
- Conservation programme: Project Snow Leopard

2.6 Great Indian Bustard

- Found in Rajasthan and Gujarat
- Critically endangered species
- Major conservation programme: Project Great Indian Bustard

2.7 Nilgiri Tahr

- Found in Nilgiri Hills
- Endemic to Western Ghats
- Endangered species

3. THREATS TO WILDLIFE

1. Habitat loss
2. Deforestation
3. Poaching
4. Climate change
5. Human-wildlife conflict
