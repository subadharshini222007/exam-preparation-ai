
PART A - AGRICULTURE

1. INTRODUCTION

Agriculture is a primary activity.
It includes growing crops, fruits, vegetables, flowers and rearing livestock.

Indian agriculture is divided into three cropping seasons:

1. Kharif - June to September
2. Rabi - October to March
3. Zaid - March to June

2. TYPES OF FARMING

1. Primitive subsistence farming
2. Intensive subsistence farming
3. Commercial farming

3. IMPORTANT FACTS

- Agriculture is the main occupation of India
- Kharif crops are grown with monsoon rainfall
- Rabi crops are grown in winter
- Zaid crops are grown in summer
- India is a major producer of rice, wheat, sugarcane, cotton, jute and tea

PART B - MAJOR CROPS

1. RICE

- Kharif crop
- Requires high temperature and high rainfall
- Major states: West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu
- Staple food of majority of Indians

2. WHEAT

- Rabi crop
- Requires cool growing season
- Major states: Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana, Madhya Pradesh

3. MILLETS

- Kharif crop
- Includes jowar, bajra, ragi
- Grown in dry areas
- Major states: Maharashtra, Karnataka, Rajasthan

4. MAIZE

- Kharif crop
- Used as food and fodder
- Major states: Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar

5. PULSES

- Rabi crop
- Includes gram, tur, moong
- Rich in protein
- Major states: Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan

6. SUGARCANE

- Kharif crop
- Requires hot and humid climate
- Major states: Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu

7. COTTON

- Kharif crop
- Requires black soil
- Major states: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Telangana, Karnataka

8. JUTE

- Kharif crop
- Requires alluvial soil
- Major states: West Bengal, Bihar, Assam

9. TEA

- Plantation crop
- Requires high rainfall and well-drained soil
- Major states: Assam, West Bengal, Kerala, Tamil Nadu

10. COFFEE

- Plantation crop
- Requires high rainfall and shade
- Major states: Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu

PART C - IRRIGATION

1. INTRODUCTION

Irrigation is the supply of water to crops.
It is essential for agriculture in India.

2. TYPES OF IRRIGATION

1. Wells and tube wells
2. Canals
3. Tanks
4. Drip irrigation
5. Sprinkler irrigation

3. WELLS AND TUBE WELLS

- Most common type of irrigation
- Used in plains and deltas
- Suitable where groundwater is available

4. CANALS

- Used in areas near rivers
- Suitable for plains
- Example: Ganga canal, Indira Gandhi canal

5. TANKS

- Used in Deccan Plateau
- Store rainwater
- Common in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh

6. DRIP IRRIGATION

- Water is supplied drop by drop
- Saves water
- Suitable for dry areas

7. SPRINKLER IRRIGATION

- Water is sprayed like rain
- Suitable for uneven land
- Saves water

8. IMPORTANT FACTS

- Irrigation is essential for agriculture
- Wells and tube wells are most common
- Canals are used near rivers
- Tanks are used in Deccan Plateau
- Drip irrigation saves water

PART A - TRANSPORT

1. INTRODUCTION

Transport is the movement of goods, services and people from one place to another.

Transport is divided into:

1. Land transport
2. Water transport
3. Air transport

2. LAND TRANSPORT

2.1 Roadways

- India has the second largest road network in the world
- Total length: about 62.16 lakh km
- Roads are cheap to build
- Suitable for short distances
- Provide door-to-door service
- Act as feeder to railways, ports and airports

Types of roads:
1. National Highways
2. State Highways
3. District Roads
4. Rural Roads
5. Border Roads
6. International Highways

Important road projects:
- Golden Quadrilateral
- North-South Corridor
- East-West Corridor
- Bharatmala Pariyojana

Golden Quadrilateral:
- Connects Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata
- Length: 5,846 km
- Four/six-lane expressway
- Completed largely by 2012

North-South Corridor:
- Connects Srinagar to Kanyakumari
- Length: about 4,016 km

East-West Corridor:
- Connects Silchar (Assam) to Porbandar (Gujarat)
- Length: about 3,640 km

Bharatmala Pariyojana:
- Major highway development programme
- Aims to build economic corridors and feeder routes

2.2 Railways

- Principal mode for long-distance freight and passengers
- Economical for bulky goods
- Promotes national integration
- Indian Railways is divided into zones

Types of gauges:
1. Broad gauge
2. Metre gauge
3. Narrow gauge

Important railway projects:
- Konkan Railway
- Dedicated Freight Corridors
- Metro Rail

2.3 Pipelines

- Used for transport of oil, gas and slurry
- Low running cost
- No trans-shipment losses

3. WATER TRANSPORT

3.1 Inland Waterways

- Cheapest mode for heavy and bulky goods
- Fuel-efficient and environment friendly
- National Waterways run on Ganga, Brahmaputra and other rivers

3.2 Ocean Routes

- Carry about 95% of India's trade volume
- Major ports handle international trade

Major ports:
1. Mumbai Port
2. Kandla Port
3. Marmagao Port
4. Kochi Port
5. Chennai Port
6. Visakhapatnam Port
7. Kolkata Port
8. Paradip Port

Sagarmala Programme:
- Aims to modernise ports
- Improve port connectivity
- Promote coastal shipping

4. AIR TRANSPORT

- Fastest mode of transport
- Suitable for difficult terrain
- Preferred in North-East India
- Carries high-value, light freight

Important airports:
1. Indira Gandhi International Airport - Delhi
2. Chhatrapati Shivaji International Airport - Mumbai
3. Chennai International Airport - Chennai
4. Kempegowda International Airport - Bengaluru

PART A - POPULATION

1. INTRODUCTION

Population means the total number of people living in a particular area at a given time.

Population is an important resource because people use resources, produce goods,
provide services and contribute to economic development.

The Census of India is the main source of population data.

2. CENSUS OF INDIA

- Census is the official enumeration of population.
- It is conducted once every ten years.
- The first complete Census in India was conducted in 1881.
- The Census of 2011 was the fifteenth Census of India.
- It was the seventh Census after Independence.

Census provides information about:
- Population size
- Population density
- Sex ratio
- Literacy
- Occupation
- Rural and urban population
- Migration
- Age composition

3. POPULATION DISTRIBUTION

Population distribution means the pattern in which people are spread over an area.

Population is unevenly distributed in India.

Areas of high population density:
- Indo-Gangetic Plains
- Coastal plains
- Industrial regions
- River valleys
- Urban and metropolitan regions

Areas of low population density:
- Himalayas
- Thar Desert
- Dense forest regions
- North-Eastern hilly areas
- Ladakh region

4. FACTORS AFFECTING POPULATION DISTRIBUTION

1. Relief
- Plains support dense population.
- Mountains and plateaus have lower population density.

2. Climate
- Moderate climate supports high population.
- Extreme heat, cold and aridity reduce population density.

3. Soil
- Fertile alluvial soil supports agriculture and high population density.

4. Water Availability
- River valleys and coastal areas attract population.

5. Minerals and Industries
- Industrial regions attract workers and migrants.
- Example: Bengaluru, Chennai, Pune, Ahmedabad and Mumbai.

6. Transport and Communication
- Better connectivity supports settlement and economic activity.

5. POPULATION DENSITY

Population density means the number of persons living per square kilometre.

Formula:
Population Density = Total Population / Total Area

High density indicates greater pressure on land and resources.
Low density may result from difficult terrain, harsh climate or lack of economic activity.

6. POPULATION GROWTH

Population growth means the change in population over a period of time.

Growth may be:
- Positive growth: Birth rate is higher than death rate.
- Negative growth: Death rate is higher than birth rate.
- Zero growth: Birth rate and death rate are equal.

7. PHASES OF POPULATION GROWTH IN INDIA

1. 1901-1921: Period of stagnant or negative growth
2. 1921-1951: Period of steady growth
3. 1951-1981: Period of rapid high growth
4. 1981 onwards: Period of slowing but positive growth

1921 is called the Great Divide in Indian demographic history because population growth
was negative before this period and positive after it.

8. POPULATION COMPOSITION

Population composition includes:
- Age structure
- Sex composition
- Literacy
- Occupation
- Rural-urban composition
- Religion
- Language

8.1 Age Structure

Population is generally classified into:

1. Children: Below 14 years
2. Working age population: 15 to 59 years
3. Aged population: 60 years and above

A high proportion of working-age population can create a demographic dividend if people
have education, skills and employment opportunities.

8.2 Sex Ratio

Sex ratio means the number of females per 1,000 males.

Sex ratio indicates the status of women in society.

8.3 Literacy

Literacy improves employment, health, awareness and social development.

According to Census 2011:
- Literacy rate in India: 74.04%
- Male literacy rate: 82.14%
- Female literacy rate: 65.46%

8.4 Occupational Structure

India’s population works in:
- Primary activities: Agriculture, fishing, forestry and mining
- Secondary activities: Manufacturing and construction
- Tertiary activities: Trade, transport, education, health and communication

PART B - URBANISATION

1. INTRODUCTION

Urbanisation is the process by which the proportion of population living in towns and
cities increases.

It occurs due to:
- Industrialisation
- Growth of services
- Better education and health facilities
- Rural-to-urban migration
- Expansion of existing towns

2. URBAN AREA - CENSUS CRITERIA

A place is treated as urban if it has:

1. A municipality, corporation, cantonment board or notified town area committee;
OR

2. All of the following conditions:
- Population of at least 5,000
- Population density of at least 400 persons per square kilometre
- At least 75% of male main workers engaged in non-agricultural activities

3. URBANISATION IN INDIA

- Urban population in 1951: about 17.29%
- Urban population in 2011: 31.16%
- Urban population in 2011: about 377.1 million
- Rural population in 2011: 68.84%

The urban population has increased due to industrial development, service-sector growth,
better health and sanitation, and rural-to-urban migration. [294][300][303]

4. TYPES OF URBAN SETTLEMENTS

1. Town
- A smaller urban settlement.

2. City
- A large urban settlement with more economic functions.

3. Metropolitan City
- A city with a population of more than 10 lakh.

4. Mega City
- A city with a population of more than 1 crore.

5. Urban Agglomeration
- A continuous urban spread consisting of a town and adjoining outgrowths.

5. CLASSIFICATION OF TOWNS

Towns are classified according to population size:

Class I Town:
- Population of 1 lakh and above

Class II Town:
- Population between 50,000 and 99,999

Class III Town:
- Population between 20,000 and 49,999

Class IV Town:
- Population between 10,000 and 19,999

Class V Town:
- Population between 5,000 and 9,999

Class VI Town:
- Population below 5,000

6. FUNCTIONS OF TOWNS

Towns may perform different functions:

1. Administrative towns
- State capitals and district headquarters

2. Industrial towns
- Jamshedpur, Bhilai, Rourkela

3. Commercial towns
- Mumbai, Delhi, Chennai, Kolkata

4. Transport towns
- Railway junctions and port cities

5. Educational towns
- Universities and educational centres

6. Religious towns
- Varanasi, Rameswaram, Tirupati

7. Tourist towns
- Ooty, Kodaikanal, Shimla

7. CAUSES OF URBANISATION

- Industrialisation
- Expansion of service sector
- Better employment opportunities
- Improved educational facilities
- Improved medical facilities
- Transport and communication development
- Rural poverty and unemployment
- Rural-to-urban migration

8. PROBLEMS OF URBANISATION

1. Housing shortage
2. Growth of slums
3. Traffic congestion
4. Water scarcity
5. Solid waste disposal problems
6. Air and water pollution
7. Unemployment
8. Urban poverty
9. Pressure on public services
10. Loss of green cover

9. URBAN PLANNING AND SOLUTIONS

- Planned urban development
- Affordable housing
- Mass public transport
- Waste segregation and recycling
- Rainwater harvesting
- Green belts and urban forests
- Smart city planning
- Slum improvement programmes
- Decentralisation of industries and services

10. IMPORTANT EXAM FACTS

- First complete Census in India: 1881
- Census is conducted every 10 years
- 1921 is called the Great Divide
- Urban population in 2011: 31.16%
- Rural population in 2011: 68.84%
- Urban population in 2011: about 377.1 million
- Census urban threshold population: 5,000
- Census urban density criterion: 400 persons per sq. km
- Male non-agricultural worker criterion: 75%
- Metropolitan city population: above 10 lakh
- Class I town population: 1 lakh and above
- Demographic dividend: high working-age population
