UNIT 1: INDIA - LOCATION, SIZE AND EXTENT
==================================================

1.1 LOCATION
India lies in the Northern and Eastern Hemispheres, in the southern part of Asia. The mainland extends from 8 degrees 4 minutes North latitude (Kanyakumari) to 37 degrees 6 minutes North latitude (Indira Col in the north), and from 68 degrees 7 minutes East longitude (western Gujarat) to 97 degrees 25 minutes East longitude (Arunachal Pradesh). Including the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, the southernmost point is Indira Point (6 degrees 45 minutes North) on Great Nicobar Island. Indira Point was earlier called Pygmalion Point or Parson's Point. It was submerged in part by the 2004 tsunami.
The east-west extent is about 2,933 km and the north-south extent is about 3,214 km. The difference in time between the easternmost and the westernmost points is about two hours. The Tropic of Cancer (23 degrees 30 minutes North) divides India into almost two equal parts, the northern sub-tropical and the southern tropical part. 

1.2 STANDARD MERIDIAN
The 82 degrees 30 minutes East longitude is the Standard Meridian of India. Indian Standard Time (IST) is 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of Greenwich Mean Time (GMT). The Standard Meridian passes through Mirzapur in Uttar Pradesh and through the states of Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Odisha and Andhra Pradesh.

1.3 TROPIC OF CANCER
It passes through eight states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura and Mizoram. 

1.4 SIZE
India has an area of about 3.287 million square kilometres, which is about 2.4 percent of the world's land area. India is the seventh largest country in the world in area (after Russia, Canada, China, USA, Brazil and Australia). In population India is now the most populous country. The largest state by area is Rajasthan and the smallest state is Goa. Among Union Territories, Andaman and Nicobar Islands is the largest and Lakshadweep is the smallest. India has 28 states and 8 Union Territories (after the reorganisation of Jammu and Kashmir in 2019).

1.5 BOUNDARIES
- India has a land frontier of about 15,200 km and a coastline of about 7,516 km (including islands; the mainland coastline is about 6,100 km).
- India shares land boundaries with seven countries: Pakistan, Afghanistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh and Myanmar. Sri Lanka (across the Palk Strait and Gulf of Mannar) and Maldives (across the Eight Degree Channel) are neighbours across the sea.
- The longest border is shared with Bangladesh. The shortest land border is with Afghanistan.
- Border lines: The Radcliffe Line (India-Pakistan), the McMahon Line (India-China, eastern sector), and the Durand Line (Afghanistan-Pakistan).
- Nine states have a coastline: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha and West Bengal. Gujarat has the longest coastline in India, and Tamil Nadu has about 1,076 km of coastline.
- Straits: The Palk Strait separates Tamil Nadu from Sri Lanka. The Ten Degree Channel separates the Andaman Islands from the Nicobar Islands. The Nine Degree Channel separates Lakshadweep (Kavaratti) from Minicoy. The Eight Degree Channel separates Minicoy from the Maldives.
- India's neighbours at sea: Sri Lanka, Maldives, Indonesia (close to Great Nicobar) and Thailand.

1.6 SIGNIFICANCE OF INDIA'S LOCATION
India's central location in the Indian Ocean between West and East Asia gives it a strategic position. The Indian Ocean (the only ocean named after a country) is crossed by key sea routes. The Suez Canal (opened in 1869) shortened the sea route between India and Europe. The Himalayas protect India from the cold winds of Central Asia.

UNIT 2: GEOLOGICAL HISTORY AND FORMATION
==================================================

2.1 ORIGIN
About 250 million years ago the earth had one supercontinent called Pangaea, which split into Laurasia (north) and Gondwanaland (south). Peninsular India was part of Gondwanaland along with Africa, Australia, South America and Antarctica. About 200 million years ago Gondwanaland broke up and the Indian plate began drifting northward at a fast speed. About 50 million years ago it collided with the Eurasian plate, and the Tethys Sea (a shallow sea between them) was folded and uplifted to form the Himalayas. The Himalayas are therefore young fold mountains and are still rising slightly. The theory of continental drift was given by Alfred Wegener (1912), and the plate tectonic theory explains the movement of plates.

2.2 GEOLOGICAL DIVISIONS OF INDIA
Indian rocks belong to four major systems: 
1. Archaean System (the oldest rocks, gneiss and granite; found in the Peninsular Plateau). 
2. Purana System (Cuddapah and Vindhyan systems; sedimentary rocks). 
3. Dravidian System (Carboniferous period to the Eocene; includes Gondwana coal). 
4. Aryan System (from the Carboniferous period to the present; includes the Deccan Traps and Himalayan rocks). 
The Deccan Trap (basalt lava) was formed by volcanic eruptions at the end of the Cretaceous period and covers parts of Maharashtra, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh and Karnataka.

2.3 THREE MAIN PHASES OF HIMALAYAN FORMATION
- The Greater Himalaya (Himadri) was formed first (about 50 million years ago).
- The Lesser Himalaya (Himachal) was formed next.
- The Outer Himalaya (Shiwaliks) was the youngest (about 5 million years ago).

UNIT 3: PHYSIOGRAPHIC DIVISIONS - OVERVIEW
==================================================

India is divided into six physiographic regions:
1. The Northern Mountains (Himalayas and Trans-Himalayas)
2. The Northern Plains (Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra Plains)
3. The Peninsular Plateau
4. The Indian Desert (Thar)
5. The Coastal Plains
6. The Islands
(Some books also add the Eastern Hills or Purvanchal as part of the Northern Mountains.)

UNIT 4: THE NORTHERN MOUNTAINS (THE HIMALAYAS)
==================================================

4.1 INTRODUCTION
The Himalayas (Himalaya means "abode of snow") are the highest and youngest mountain range of the world. They form an arc about 2,400 km long from the Indus gorge in the west to the Brahmaputra gorge in the east. The width varies from about 400 km in Kashmir to about 150 km in Arunachal Pradesh. The Himalayas are made of three parallel ranges, often described as the Greater (Himadri), Lesser (Himachal) and Outer (Shiwalik) Himalayas. They are fold mountains, and the area has frequent earthquakes and landslides. Trans-Himalayan ranges (Karakoram, Ladakh, Zaskar) lie north of the Himalayas.

4.2 TRANS-HIMALAYAS (TIBETAN HIMALAYAS)
Lies north of the Great Himalayas, about 40 km wide, 1000 km long: the Karakoram range, Ladakh range and Zaskar range. 
- The Karakoram range has K2 (Godwin Austen) at 8,611 m, the second highest peak in the world, and the Siachen Glacier (about 70 km long; the longest glacier in the Karakoram and one of the longest outside the polar regions). Other peaks: Gasherbrum, Broad Peak, Masherbrum.
- The Ladakh range lies between the Indus and Shyok rivers. Pangong Tso lake is in Ladakh.
- The Zaskar range lies south of the Indus.

4.3 THE GREATER HIMALAYA (HIMADRI)
- The northernmost and highest range of the Himalayas, with an average height of about 6,000 m. It is made up of granite and gneiss and is permanently snow-bound. Many glaciers originate here.
- Important peaks: Mount Everest (8,848.86 m in Nepal-China; highest in the world), Kanchenjunga (8,586 m; the highest peak in India, third highest in the world; in Sikkim-Nepal border), Makalu, Dhaulagiri, Nanga Parbat (8,126 m), Annapurna, Nanda Devi (7,816 m; the highest peak located entirely within India, in Uttarakhand), Kamet, Namcha Barwa.
- Important glaciers: Gangotri (the source of the Bhagirathi), Yamunotri, Pindari, Milam, Zemu (Sikkim; the largest glacier in the Eastern Himalaya), Siachen.

4.4 THE LESSER HIMALAYA (HIMACHAL)
- South of the Himadri, with altitudes between 3,700 and 4,500 m and a width of about 50 km. Made of highly compressed and altered rocks. 
- Important ranges: Pir Panjal (the longest and most important range in the Lesser Himalaya; Banihal Pass lies here), Dhauladhar, Nag Tibba, Mahabharat range, and Mussoorie range.
- Famous valleys: the Kashmir Valley, Kangra Valley, Kullu Valley. 
- Hill stations: Shimla, Mussoorie, Nainital, Ranikhet, Almora, Darjeeling, Dalhousie, Chail. 
- The "Marg" (meadows) such as Gulmarg and Sonamarg are high-altitude grassy slopes in Kashmir; the "Bugyals" of Uttarakhand are similar meadows. 

4.5 THE SHIWALIKS (OUTER HIMALAYA)
- The outermost and youngest range, with altitudes between 900 and 1,100 m and a width of 10-50 km. Made of unconsolidated sediments brought down by rivers from the main Himalayas.
- Longitudinal valleys between the Lesser Himalayas and Shiwaliks are called Duns in the west and Duars in the east: Dehradun (the largest Dun), Kotli Dun, Patli Dun, and Dun valleys of Uttarakhand.
- Two foothill features: Bhabar (a narrow, porous pebble belt at the foot of the Shiwaliks where streams disappear) and Terai (a marshy, thickly forested zone south of the Bhabar where the streams reappear; rich in wildlife, e.g. Jim Corbett National Park).

4.6 REGIONAL DIVISION OF THE HIMALAYAS (BY RIVER VALLEYS)
Based on the river valleys, Sir Sidney Burrard divided the Himalayas:
1. Punjab Himalaya: between the Indus and the Satluj (about 560 km). 
2. Kumaon Himalaya: between the Satluj and the Kali (about 320 km). Includes Nanda Devi, Kamet.
3. Nepal Himalaya: between the Kali and the Tista (about 800 km). Includes Everest, Kanchenjunga, Dhaulagiri.
4. Assam Himalaya: between the Tista and the Dihang (about 720 km). Includes Namcha Barwa.

4.7 THE EASTERN HILLS (PURVANCHAL)
Beyond the Dihang gorge, the Himalayas bend sharply to the south and form a series of hills in the north-eastern states, known as the Purvanchal: Patkai Bum, Naga Hills, Manipur Hills, Mizo (Lushai) Hills. The Patkai range in Arunachal Pradesh and Myanmar border is made up of low hills. Mizoram's Blue Mountain (Phawngpui) is the highest peak of Mizoram.

4.8 IMPORTANT PASSES OF THE HIMALAYAS
- Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh: Banihal Pass (links Jammu and Srinagar; the Jawahar Tunnel is here), Zoji La (links Srinagar and Leh), Khardung La (Ladakh; one of the highest motorable roads), Karakoram Pass (between Ladakh and Xinjiang), Burzil Pass (Srinagar-Gilgit).
- Himachal Pradesh: Rohtang Pass (links Manali and Lahul-Spiti; Atal Tunnel was opened beneath it in 2020), Bara Lacha La, Shipki La (on the Satluj; India-China border; Kailash Mansarovar route in some seasons).
- Uttarakhand: Lipulekh Pass (Kailash Mansarovar route), Mana Pass, Niti Pass.
- Sikkim: Nathu La (a trade route, Indo-China; Kailash Mansarovar route), Jelep La.
- Arunachal Pradesh: Bomdi La, Diphu Pass, Yonggyap, Tuting.
- Outside India: Khyber and Bolan passes in Pakistan (the old gateways to India from the north-west).

4.9 HIMALAYAN RIVERS AND LAKES (BRIEF)
The Himalayan rivers are perennial (they get water from snow melt and rain): the Indus, the Ganga and the Brahmaputra systems. They carry large amounts of silt. 
Glacial lakes: Gurudongmar and Tsomgo (Sikkim), Pangong Tso (Ladakh), Tso Moriri (Ladakh), Dal and Wular (Kashmir), Nainital, Bhimtal. Wular is the largest freshwater lake in India (Jammu and Kashmir). Loktak Lake (Manipur) is the largest freshwater lake in the North East and has the floating islands known as phumdis (Keibul Lamjao National Park, the only floating national park).

4.10 SIGNIFICANCE OF THE HIMALAYAS
- Climatic barrier: stops cold, dry winds from Central Asia; causes monsoon rain in India. 
- Source of perennial rivers and fertile alluvial soil.
- Rich forests, herbs and biodiversity.
- Hydroelectric power potential and tourism (Shimla, Manali, Darjeeling, Gangtok).
- Pilgrimage centres: Badrinath, Kedarnath, Gangotri, Yamunotri (Char Dham of Uttarakhand), Amarnath, Vaishno Devi.
- Natural defence boundary.

UNIT 5: THE NORTHERN PLAINS
==================================================

5.1 FORMATION AND EXTENT
The Northern Plains lie between the Himalayas in the north and the Peninsular Plateau in the south. They were formed by the deposition of alluvium brought by the rivers Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra and their tributaries over millions of years in a depression (a trough) formed in front of the rising Himalayas. The plains are about 2,400 km long and 240 to 320 km wide, covering an area of about 7 lakh square kilometres. These are among the most fertile and most densely populated regions in the world. The thickness of alluvium is up to several thousand metres in some places.

5.2 DIVISIONS ACCORDING TO RIVERS
1. Western Plains (Punjab-Haryana Plains): formed by the Indus and its tributaries (Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas and Satluj). The area between two rivers is called a Doab ("two waters"): Bist Doab (Beas-Satluj), Bari Doab (Ravi-Beas), Rechna Doab (Chenab-Ravi), Chaj Doab (Jhelum-Chenab) and Sind Sagar Doab (Indus-Jhelum). Punjab means "land of five rivers".
2. Ganga Plain: from Haryana (Yamuna) to Bangladesh, passing through Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Jharkhand (parts) and West Bengal. The Ganga Plain is divided into Upper (Ganga-Yamuna Doab), Middle (Ghaghara-Gandak-Kosi plains in Bihar) and Lower (Bengal) plains. 
3. Brahmaputra Plain (Assam Valley): in Assam; is long (about 700 km) and narrow. The Brahmaputra deposits silt and floods the plain frequently. Majuli (in the Brahmaputra) is the largest river island in the world.

5.3 DIVISIONS ACCORDING TO RELIEF (PHYSIOGRAPHY OF THE PLAIN)
1. Bhabar: a narrow belt (8-16 km wide) at the foot of the Shiwaliks; pebbles and boulders; streams disappear underground.
2. Terai: a marshy, damp belt (15-30 km wide) south of the Bhabar; thick forest; the Terai region has been cleared for agriculture and settlements.
3. Bhangar: older alluvium, found above the flood level; the terraces are rich in calcareous deposits known as "kankar". It is less fertile.
4. Khadar: newer alluvium found in the floodplains; renewed every year by floods; very fertile.
5. Delta: the Ganga-Brahmaputra delta (the Sundarbans delta) is the largest delta in the world.

5.4 THE RAJASTHAN PLAIN
Part of the Great Plain towards the west includes the Marusthali (desert) and Bagar (semi-arid) areas, which are described in the desert unit.

5.5 SIGNIFICANCE
The plains have deep and fertile soil, a flat surface suitable for irrigation and transport. They are the granary of India, producing wheat, rice, sugarcane and jute. They support a large number of towns and have dense road and rail networks.

UNIT 6: THE PENINSULAR PLATEAU
==================================================

6.1 INTRODUCTION
The Peninsular Plateau is a table land composed of old crystalline, igneous and metamorphic rocks. It is the oldest and most stable landmass of India (a part of the ancient Gondwana land). It is roughly triangular in shape with its base along the Delhi ridge (Aravalli) in the north-west and an apex at Kanyakumari. The width and length: about 16 lakh sq km area; average height 600-900 m above sea level. It is bounded by the Aravalli, Vindhya, Satpura; the Western Ghats in the west and the Eastern Ghats in the east. It slopes generally from west to east (and rivers like the Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery flow east), except for the Narmada, Tapi and rivers of the Western Ghats that flow west.

6.2 TWO MAIN DIVISIONS
1. The Central Highlands (Malwa Plateau and surroundings), north of the Narmada.
2. The Deccan Plateau, south of the Narmada.

6.3 THE CENTRAL HIGHLANDS
- The Aravalli Range: one of the oldest fold mountain ranges in the world (running about 800 km from Delhi to Gujarat through Haryana, Rajasthan). It has been eroded to low, rounded ridges. The highest peak is Guru Shikhar (1,722 m) on Mount Abu in Rajasthan. Mount Abu has the Dilwara Jain temples. The Aravalli is a water divide between the Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea drainage systems. 
- The Malwa Plateau: between the Aravalli and the Vindhya; the rivers Chambal, Betwa and Narmada flow through it. Mostly black soil (regur) is found here.
- The Bundelkhand Upland: between the Betwa and Ken, of Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh; made up of granite and gneiss.
- The Baghelkhand plateau: east of Bundelkhand, in Madhya Pradesh. 
- The Chotanagpur Plateau: in Jharkhand, West Bengal, Odisha, Chhattisgarh; also called the "Ruhr of India" because of its rich coal and iron ore. The Damodar river flows through it. Parasnath hills (Jharkhand) is the highest peak. 
- The Vindhya Range: runs from Gujarat to Bihar along the northern edge of the Narmada valley; the highest peak is Kalumar. 
- The Satpura Range: south of the Narmada, between the Narmada and Tapi. The highest peak is Dhupgarh (1,350 m, near Pachmarhi). Other ranges: Mahadeo Hills, Maikal Range (Amarkantak, the source of the Narmada and Son rivers). Pachmarhi is a hill station.
- The Rajmahal Hills: in Jharkhand, near the Ganga.
- The Narmada and Tapi flow through rift valleys (faults) in the Vindhya-Satpura region towards the west. 

6.4 THE DECCAN PLATEAU
The Deccan Plateau lies south of the Narmada. It is triangular and covers Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu and Kerala. Sub-divisions: 
- The Maharashtra Plateau (Deccan Trap), with black soil and basalt rocks.
- The Karnataka Plateau (Mysore Plateau), with an average height of 600-900 m, and highest part in Mullayanagiri (in the Baba Budan hills, 1,930 m) which is the highest in Karnataka. Also covers Malnad (hilly) and Maidan (plain) areas.
- The Telangana Plateau and Andhra Plateau.
- The Chhattisgarh Plain (the "Rice Bowl of Madhya Pradesh/Chhattisgarh") drains by the Mahanadi.
- The Dandakaranya region.

6.5 THE MEGHALAYA (SHILLONG) PLATEAU AND KARBI ANGLONG
The Meghalaya Plateau in the North-East is a detached portion of the Peninsular Plateau, separated from the main plateau by the Garo-Rajmahal Gap (Malda Gap), which was formed by a fault and filled with sediments. It has the Garo, Khasi and Jaintia hills. Shillong Peak (1,961 m) is the highest. Mawsynram and Cherrapunji receive the highest rainfall in the world (Mawsynram being the wettest place). 

6.6 THE WESTERN GHATS (SAHYADRI)
- A continuous escarpment running parallel to the west coast from the Tapi valley (Gujarat-Maharashtra border) to Kanyakumari for about 1,600 km, with an average height of 900-1,600 m. They are not true mountains but the faulted edge of the plateau (the sea-facing slope is steep; the plateau-facing slope is gentle).
- They are continuous and can be crossed only through the passes. They cause orographic rainfall (heavy rain on the windward side).
- They are the source of rivers: the Godavari, Krishna and Cauvery (flowing east).
- Important peaks: Anaimudi (Anamudi, 2,695 m) in Kerala (the Anamalai hills) is the highest peak in the Western Ghats and in South India. Doddabetta (2,637 m) in the Nilgiris of Tamil Nadu is the second highest in the Western Ghats and the highest in Tamil Nadu. Mahendragiri (1,647 m in Kanyakumari district? - see TN section).
- Passes: Thal Ghat (links Mumbai and Nashik), Bhor Ghat (links Mumbai and Pune), Pal Ghat (Palakkad Gap; links Kerala and Tamil Nadu; about 30 km wide), Shencottah (Senkottai) Pass (links Kollam in Kerala with Tenkasi/Shencottah in Tamil Nadu), Amba Ghat, Agumbe Ghat, Charmadi Ghat.
- Subdivisions: Northern Sahyadri (Maharashtra), Central Sahyadri (Karnataka), and Southern Sahyadri (Kerala-Tamil Nadu).
- The Western Ghats are a UNESCO World Heritage Site (2012) and one of the 36 biodiversity hotspots. 
- Hill stations: Mahabaleshwar, Lonavala, Khandala (Maharashtra), Coorg/Madikeri (Karnataka), Ooty/Udhagamandalam, Coonoor, Kotagiri, Kodaikanal, Munnar (Kerala), Valparai, Yercaud (Eastern Ghats).
- Western Ghat hills in Tamil Nadu: the Nilgiris, Anamalai (Anaimalai), Palani, Cardamom hills (Elaimalai), Varushanad and Agasthiyamalai.

6.7 THE EASTERN GHATS
- Discontinuous and irregular range of hills along the east coast, from the Mahanadi valley (Odisha) to the Nilgiris in the south. They have an average height of 600 m; the highest peak is Jindhagada (Arma Konda; about 1,690 m) in Andhra Pradesh. Mahendragiri (1,501 m) in Odisha is also an important peak.
- The Eastern Ghats are broken by rivers (the Godavari, Mahanadi, Krishna and Cauvery) flowing into the Bay of Bengal. 
- They meet the Western Ghats at the Nilgiri hills.
- Hills in Tamil Nadu (the southern end): the Javadi hills, Shevaroy hills (Yercaud, Servarayan peak), Kalrayan hills, Pachaimalai hills, Kolli hills, Chitteri hills and Palamalai.
- Eastern Ghats are older than Western Ghats and are made up of charnockite, khondalite and gneiss. They have rich minerals (bauxite, manganese).

6.8 DIFFERENCES - WESTERN GHATS VS EASTERN GHATS
- Western Ghats: continuous, higher (average 1,200 m), steeper on the west, heavy rainfall, run parallel to the Arabian Sea, form a water divide, difficult to cross except by passes.
- Eastern Ghats: discontinuous, lower (average 600 m), broken by rivers, less rainfall, run parallel to the Bay of Bengal.

6.9 THE NILGIRIS
The Nilgiri hills ("Blue Mountains") are where the Eastern and Western Ghats meet. Doddabetta (2,637 m) is the highest peak. Udhagamandalam (Ooty), known as the "Queen of Hill Stations", is the summer capital. Coonoor, Kotagiri and Wellington are other places. The first Biosphere Reserve of India (1986) is the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve. Tea, coffee and cardamom plantations are found there. Mudumalai and Mukurthi National Parks are here.

6.10 PALAKKAD (PALGHAT) GAP
The Palakkad Gap is a low pass in the Western Ghats between the Nilgiri hills and the Anamalai hills (about 30-40 km wide; 300 m height) linking Palakkad in Kerala and Coimbatore in Tamil Nadu. It allows the south-west monsoon winds to reach the interior of Tamil Nadu (Coimbatore). It is used by the railway and road. The rivers Bharathapuzha and Bhavani flow through nearby areas.

UNIT 7: THE INDIAN DESERT (THAR)
==================================================

7.1 LOCATION
The Great Indian Desert (Thar Desert) lies west of the Aravalli range, covering western Rajasthan and extending into Haryana, Punjab, Gujarat and into Pakistan. About 2 lakh sq km of it is in India (the ninth largest hot desert in the world). Rainfall is less than 150 mm annually.

7.2 FEATURES
- The region is mostly covered with sand dunes. Barchans (crescent-shaped dunes) are common. Dune movement is driven by wind.
- The Luni is the only significant river in the desert (a seasonal river in Rajasthan, rises in the Aravalli (Ajmer), flows into the Rann of Kutch). Streams disappear in the sand (inland drainage). 
- Salt lakes: Sambhar (the largest inland salt lake in India), Didwana, Pachpadra and Lunkaransar. Salt lake basins are called Playas (known as "Rann" locally in Kutch and "dhands" in the desert). 
- The Marusthali is the desert proper (west of the 25 cm rainfall line) and the Bagar is the semi-arid eastern part.
- The Rajasthan Canal (Indira Gandhi Canal) brings water to the desert from the Satluj-Beas.
- Vegetation: thorny scrub, cactus, khejri, babool. Fauna: camel, blackbuck, Great Indian Bustard (critically endangered). Desert National Park, Jaisalmer.
- Pokhran in the Thar is the site of India's nuclear tests (1974 and 1998).

7.3 THE RANN OF KUTCH
A vast salt marsh in Gujarat (Great Rann and Little Rann) that is flooded during the monsoon and dry in other seasons. The Little Rann of Kutch is home to the Indian Wild Ass (Asiatic wild ass).

UNIT 8: THE COASTAL PLAINS
==================================================

8.1 INTRODUCTION
The Peninsular Plateau is flanked by narrow coastal strips, the Western Coastal Plain and the Eastern Coastal Plain, running along the Arabian Sea and the Bay of Bengal respectively. India has a coastline of about 7,516 km including islands.

8.2 THE WESTERN COASTAL PLAIN
- A narrow strip (10-25 km wide) between the Western Ghats and the Arabian Sea; extends from Gujarat to Kanyakumari.
- Because of the submergence of the coast, there are no large deltas but there are natural harbours and lagoons (backwaters).
- Divisions: 
  1. Gujarat Coast: Kathiawar (Saurashtra) and Kutch peninsulas, Gulf of Khambhat and Gulf of Kutch.
  2. Konkan Coast: from Daman to Goa. 
  3. Kanara (Karnataka) Coast: from Goa to Mangalore.
  4. Malabar Coast: from Mangalore to Kanyakumari (Kerala). The Malabar coast has lagoons called "Kayals" (backwaters) such as the Vembanad (the largest, a Ramsar site; Alappuzha and Kochi), Ashtamudi and Kayamkulam. 
- Ports: Kandla (tidal port), Mumbai (the biggest natural harbour; Jawaharlal Nehru Port at Nhava Sheva is the largest container port), Marmagao (Goa), New Mangalore, Kochi (a natural harbour), Tuticorin (V.O. Chidambaranar port in Tamil Nadu in the south-east coast).
- Rivers on the western coast (the Narmada and Tapi) form estuaries rather than deltas.

8.3 THE EASTERN COASTAL PLAIN
- A wider plain (80-100 km wide) between the Eastern Ghats and the Bay of Bengal; extends from West Bengal to Kanyakumari. It has deltas of major east-flowing rivers: the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Cauvery. These deltas are very fertile. It is an emergent coast.
- Divisions: 
  1. Utkal Plain (Odisha): Mahanadi delta, Chilika Lake (the largest brackish water lagoon in India and Asia; a Ramsar site).
  2. Northern Circars: between the Mahanadi and the Krishna, in Odisha and Andhra Pradesh; the Godavari-Krishna delta. 
  3. Coromandel Coast: between the Krishna delta and Kanyakumari (Tamil Nadu). Pulicat Lake (the second largest lagoon in India; a barrier to the sea by Sriharikota island). Cauvery delta (the Granary of South India).
- Ports: Kolkata (Haldia), Paradip, Visakhapatnam (the oldest and deepest port; natural harbour), Chennai (artificial harbour), Ennore (Kamarajar port), Tuticorin. Chennai Port is one of the oldest ports in India; Visakhapatnam has the deepest.
- The east coast gets rainfall in October-December from the retreating north-east monsoon (especially the Coromandel coast of Tamil Nadu).

8.4 TAMIL NADU COAST
Tamil Nadu has a coastline of about 1,076 km along the Bay of Bengal, Indian Ocean (Gulf of Mannar) and Palk Bay. Features: Pulicat Lake (on the border with Andhra), Marina Beach (Chennai; second longest urban beach in the world, about 13 km), Mahabalipuram, Pichavaram mangroves (near Chidambaram), Cauvery delta (Nagapattinam), Point Calimere (Kodikarai) bird sanctuary, Palk Strait (between Tamil Nadu and Sri Lanka), Gulf of Mannar (coral reefs, pearl fisheries; Biosphere Reserve), Rameswaram and Dhanushkodi (on Pamban Island), Kanyakumari (the southern tip, the meeting point of the Arabian Sea, Bay of Bengal and Indian Ocean), Tuticorin (V.O. Chidambaranar Port, the second major port of Tamil Nadu), Ennore.

UNIT 9: THE ISLANDS
==================================================

9.1 THE ANDAMAN AND NICOBAR ISLANDS (BAY OF BENGAL)
- A group of about 572 islands (some count 836) lying in the Bay of Bengal, extending from about 6 degrees to 14 degrees North latitude, stretching over about 700 km. They are an extension of the Arakan Yoma mountain range of Myanmar. They are made of tertiary sandstone, limestone and shale. They are covered by dense tropical rain forests.
- The Andaman group is in the north and the Nicobar group is in the south; they are separated by the Ten Degree Channel. The Andaman consists of North, Middle and South Andaman (Great Andaman), Little Andaman and others. 
- The capital is Port Blair (South Andaman).
- Highest peak: Saddle Peak (737 m) in North Andaman.
- Barren Island (near Port Blair) has India's only active volcano; Narcondam is a dormant volcano.
- Indira Point (Great Nicobar) is the southernmost point of India. 
- Important: Great Nicobar Biosphere Reserve; Cellular Jail at Port Blair (the "Kalapani", used by the British to imprison freedom fighters). 
- Tribes: Jarawa, Onge, Sentinelese, Great Andamanese, Nicobarese, Shompen.
- The 2004 Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami (26 December 2004) affected these islands badly.

9.2 LAKSHADWEEP ISLANDS (ARABIAN SEA)
- A group of 36 small coral islands (atolls) lying about 200-440 km off the Kerala coast; the total area is only about 32 sq km. They are the smallest Union Territory by area. Capital: Kavaratti. 
- The coral islands are formed by tiny marine organisms called coral polyps. The Pitti Island is a bird sanctuary. The main islands are Kavaratti, Agatti, Minicoy, Amini, Andrott, Kalpeni. 
- Minicoy is the southernmost island and is separated from the Maldives by the Eight Degree Channel and from the Lakshadweep group by the Nine Degree Channel.
- The language of Minicoy is Mahl (Dhivehi). The main occupation is fishing and coconut cultivation. 
- Coral: atolls, fringing reefs and barrier reefs.

9.3 OTHER IMPORTANT ISLANDS
- Majuli: the largest river island in the world (Brahmaputra, Assam).
- Pamban Island: lies between the Indian mainland and Sri Lanka (Rameswaram is on it); Pamban Bridge connects it with the mainland. Adam's Bridge (Rama Setu) is a chain of shoals running from Pamban to Mannar Island in Sri Lanka.
- Sriharikota: the spaceport island of ISRO (Andhra Pradesh), separating Pulicat Lake from the sea.
- Salsette Island: where Mumbai is located. Elephanta Island (Gharapuri) near Mumbai has the cave temples.
- Diu: an island off the Gujarat coast. Shiyal Bet, Bet Dwarka (Gujarat).
- Sagar Island: where the Ganga meets the Bay of Bengal (West Bengal).
- Wheeler Island (now Abdul Kalam Island): a missile test range off Odisha.
- Kachchatheevu: a small island in the Palk Strait, ceded to Sri Lanka (1974 agreement).
- Hare Island, Sunderbans islands, and Divar (Goa).

UNIT 10: PHYSIOGRAPHY OF TAMIL NADU
==================================================

10.1 PHYSICAL DIVISIONS OF TAMIL NADU
Tamil Nadu lies between 8 degrees 5 minutes and 13 degrees 35 minutes North latitude and 76 degrees 15 minutes and 80 degrees 20 minutes East longitude, with an area of 1,30,058 sq km (about 4 percent of India). The physical divisions are:
1. The Western Ghats: the Nilgiris, Anamalai, Palani, Cardamom (Elaimalai), Varushanad and Agasthiyamalai hills; Doddabetta (2,637 m) is the highest peak in the Nilgiris and in Tamil Nadu. Anaimudi (2,695 m, in Kerala) lies on the Anamalai range near the border. Agasthiyakoodam (1,868 m) is the southern-most major peak, near Tirunelveli; Kolaryum? (see note).
2. The Eastern Ghats: Javadi hills, Shevaroy hills, Kalrayan hills, Pachaimalai hills, Kolli hills, Chitteri hills, Palamalai: they are low, broken hills.
3. The Central Plateau (Interior Plateau): Coimbatore Plateau and the Dharmapuri-Salem plateau (Mysore plateau region), and Madurai plains.
4. The Eastern Coastal Plain (Coromandel Coast): includes the fertile Cauvery delta, the Palar and Vaigai basins.
5. The Western Plain (Kanyakumari): the Nanjil Nadu (land of ploughs) plains. Kanyakumari district has features of the west coast.
6. The Palghat Gap in the Western Ghats.

10.2 SOIL AND RELIEF
Red soil covers the largest part of Tamil Nadu. Black soil occurs in Coimbatore, Madurai and Tirunelveli; alluvial soil in the Cauvery delta (Thanjavur, Tiruvarur, Nagapattinam); laterite soil in hills (Nilgiris, Kanyakumari); coastal sandy soil along the coast.

10.3 TAMIL NADU RIVERS (BRIEF)
Cauvery (the largest and most important), Vaigai, Tamiraparani (the only perennial river rising and flowing entirely in Tamil Nadu), Palar, Pennaiyar, Vellar, Bhavani, Noyyal, Amaravathi and Chittar. Most rivers flow east into the Bay of Bengal; Kodayar and Pazhayar flow in the west and south of Kanyakumari.

UNIT 11: QUICK REVISION - "HIGHEST, LONGEST, LARGEST" FACTS
==================================================

- Highest peak in the world: Mount Everest (8,848.86 m). 
- Highest peak in India: Kanchenjunga (8,586 m); highest peak entirely in India: Nanda Devi (7,816 m).
- Second highest peak in the world: K2 (Godwin Austen), 8,611 m (Karakoram).
- Highest peak of the Peninsular India: Anaimudi (2,695 m), Kerala.
- Highest peak in Tamil Nadu: Doddabetta (2,637 m), Nilgiris.
- Highest peak of the Aravalli: Guru Shikhar (1,722 m), Mount Abu.
- Highest peak of Satpura: Dhupgarh (1,350 m). Highest peak of Eastern Ghats: Jindhagada (1,690 m).
- Highest peak of Vindhya: Kalumar. Highest peak of Chotanagpur plateau: Parasnath Hill (1,365 m).
- Oldest mountain range of India: Aravalli. Youngest: Himalayas (especially Shiwaliks).
- Oldest landmass: Peninsular Plateau.
- Longest glacier in India: Siachen (Karakoram, about 70 km).
- Largest freshwater lake in India: Wular (Jammu and Kashmir). Largest inland salt lake: Sambhar. Largest brackish water lagoon: Chilika. Largest artificial lake: Govind Sagar (Bhakra). 
- Largest delta: Sundarbans (Ganga-Brahmaputra delta). 
- Largest river island: Majuli. 
- Southernmost point of mainland India: Kanyakumari; southernmost point of India: Indira Point.
- Longest coastline: Gujarat.
- Largest desert: Thar. Only active volcano in India: Barren Island (Andaman). 
- Wettest place: Mawsynram (Meghalaya).
- Highest motorable pass: Khardung La (claimed) / Umling La (claimed, Ladakh; check the latest records).
- Longest road tunnel in India: Atal Tunnel (Rohtang; 9.02 km) is among the longest highway tunnels above 10,000 ft. The Banihal-Qazigund tunnel (Jawahar Tunnel) is on the Jammu-Srinagar highway.
- India's longest river: Ganga (about 2,525 km); the longest river flowing wholly in India is the Ganga. 
- Longest river in Peninsular India: Godavari. 
- Mount Abu: Rajasthan's only hill station.
