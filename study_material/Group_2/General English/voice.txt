==================================================
UNIT 1: INTRODUCTION TO VOICE
==================================================

1.1 MEANING OF VOICE
Voice is the form of a verb which shows whether the subject performs the action or receives the action.

There are two voices in English:
1. Active Voice
2. Passive Voice

1.2 ACTIVE VOICE
In active voice, the subject does the action.

Structure:
Subject + Verb + Object

Examples:
- Ravi writes a letter.
- The teacher teaches English.
- The boy broke the window.
- The chef cooked dinner.
- The government launched a new scheme.

In the sentence "Ravi writes a letter":
- Ravi is the subject.
- writes is the verb.
- a letter is the object.
- Ravi performs the action.

1.3 PASSIVE VOICE
In passive voice, the subject receives the action. The object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence.

Structure:
Object + helping verb + past participle of the main verb + by + subject

Examples:
- A letter is written by Ravi.
- English is taught by the teacher.
- The window was broken by the boy.
- Dinner was cooked by the chef.
- A new scheme was launched by the government.

In the sentence "A letter is written by Ravi":
- A letter is the new subject.
- is written is the passive verb.
- by Ravi shows the doer of the action.

1.4 BASIC RULE FOR CHANGING ACTIVE TO PASSIVE
Step 1: Identify the subject, verb and object in the active sentence.
Step 2: Make the object of the active sentence the subject of the passive sentence.
Step 3: Use the correct form of the verb "be" according to the tense.
Step 4: Use the past participle (third form) of the main verb.
Step 5: Use "by" before the original subject.
Step 6: Change pronouns according to their function.

Pronoun change:
- I becomes me.
- We becomes us.
- He becomes him.
- She becomes her.
- They becomes them.
- You remains you.
- It remains it.

Examples:
- Active: "He helps me."
  Passive: "I am helped by him."
- Active: "They invited us."
  Passive: "We were invited by them."
- Active: "She loves him."
  Passive: "He is loved by her."

1.5 FORMS OF "BE" USED IN PASSIVE VOICE
The verb "be" is used according to the tense of the active sentence.

Tense - Active verb form - Passive verb form:
1. Simple Present - write / writes - am/is/are + written.
2. Present Continuous - am/is/are writing - am/is/are + being + written.
3. Present Perfect - has/have written - has/have + been + written.
4. Simple Past - wrote - was/were + written.
5. Past Continuous - was/were writing - was/were + being + written.
6. Past Perfect - had written - had + been + written.
7. Simple Future - will write - will + be + written.
8. Future Perfect - will have written - will + have + been + written.
9. Can/Could - can write - can + be + written.
10. May/Might - may write - may + be + written.
11. Must - must write - must + be + written.
12. Should - should write - should + be + written.

Passive structure:

   Passive: "A letter is written by Ravi."

   Passive: "English is taught by the teacher."

3. Active: "Farmers grow paddy."
   Passive: "Paddy is grown by farmers."

UNIT 3: PASSIVE VOICE WITH MODAL VERBS
==================================================

3.1 BASIC RULE
Active structure:
Subject + modal + base verb + object.

Passive structure:
Object + modal + be + past participle + by + subject.

Common modals:
can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would, ought to.

Examples:
1. Active: "You can solve this problem."
   Passive: "This problem can be solved by you."

2. Active: "The teacher may explain the lesson."
   Passive: "The lesson may be explained by the teacher."

3. Active: "We must obey the rules."
   Passive: "The rules must be obeyed by us."

4. Active: "You should complete the work."
   Passive: "The work should be completed by you."

5. Active: "They will announce the results."
   Passive: "The results will be announced by them."

6. Active: "The government must implement the scheme."
   Passive: "The scheme must be implemented by the government."

7. Active: "One should respect elders."
   Passive: "Elders should be respected."

8. Active: "The company can produce more vehicles."
   Passive: "More vehicles can be produced by the company."

9. Active: "Students should follow the instructions."
   Passive: "The instructions should be followed by students."

10. Active: "The committee might approve the proposal."
    Passive: "The proposal might be approved by the committee."

UNIT 4: PASSIVE VOICE OF INTERROGATIVE SENTENCES
==================================================

4.1 YES/NO TYPE QUESTIONS
Active structure:
Auxiliary verb + subject + main verb + object?

Passive structure:
Auxiliary verb + object + past participle + by + subject?

Examples:
1. Active: "Does Ravi write a letter?"
   Passive: "Is a letter written by Ravi?"

2. Active: "Is she cleaning the room?"
   Passive: "Is the room being cleaned by her?"

3. Active: "Have they completed the work?"
   Passive: "Has the work been completed by them?"

4. Active: "Did the boy break the window?"
   Passive: "Was the window broken by the boy?"

5. Active: "Will they announce the results?"
   Passive: "Will the results be announced by them?"

6. Active: "Can you solve this problem?"
   Passive: "Can this problem be solved by you?"

7. Active: "Must we obey the rules?"
   Passive: "Must the rules be obeyed by us?"

8. Active: "Should students respect teachers?"
   Passive: "Should teachers be respected by students?"

9. Active: "Did the chef cook dinner?"
   Passive: "Was dinner cooked by the chef?"

10. Active: "Has the government launched the scheme?"
    Passive: "Has the scheme been launched by the government?"

4.2 WH-TYPE QUESTIONS
When the question word is the object, it remains at the beginning in the passive sentence.

Examples:
1. Active: "Who wrote this letter?"
   Passive: "By whom was this letter written?"

2. Active: "Who broke the window?"
   Passive: "By whom was the window broken?"

3. Active: "Whom did you invite?"
   Passive: "Who was invited by you?"

4. Active: "What did he buy?"
   Passive: "What was bought by him?"

5. Active: "Which book did she read?"
   Passive: "Which book was read by her?"

6. Active: "When will they announce the results?"
   Passive: "When will the results be announced?"

7. Active: "Where did they build the bridge?"
   Passive: "Where was the bridge built?"

8. Active: "Why did the government launch the scheme?"
   Passive: "Why was the scheme launched by the government?"

9. Active: "How did the workers repair the road?"
   Passive: "How was the road repaired by the workers?"

10. Active: "Who has stolen my pen?"
    Passive: "By whom has my pen been stolen?"

UNIT 6: SPECIAL CASES IN PASSIVE VOICE
==================================================

6.1 VERBS WITH TWO OBJECTS
Some verbs take two objects: a direct object and an indirect object.
Common verbs: give, send, offer, teach, tell, show, promise, award.

Either object can become the subject in the passive sentence.

Examples:
1. Active: "The teacher gave the students a book."
   Passive: "The students were given a book by the teacher."
   Passive: "A book was given to the students by the teacher."

2. Active: "He sent me a letter."
   Passive: "I was sent a letter by him."
   Passive: "A letter was sent to me by him."

3. Active: "She offered him a job."
   Passive: "He was offered a job by her."
   Passive: "A job was offered to him by her."

4. Active: "The principal awarded the student a prize."
   Passive: "The student was awarded a prize by the principal."
   Passive: "A prize was awarded to the student by the principal."

5. Active: "They told us a story."
   Passive: "We were told a story by them."
   Passive: "A story was told to us by them."

6. Active: "The manager showed the visitors the office."
   Passive: "The visitors were shown the office by the manager."
   Passive: "The office was shown to the visitors by the manager."

7. Active: "The teacher taught us English."
   Passive: "We were taught English by the teacher."
   Passive: "English was taught to us by the teacher."

8. Active: "He promised her a gift."
   Passive: "She was promised a gift by him."
   Passive: "A gift was promised to her by him."

9. Active: "The company gave the employees bonuses."
   Passive: "The employees were given bonuses by the company."
   Passive: "Bonuses were given to the employees by the company."

10. Active: "The government provided the farmers seeds."
    Passive: "The farmers were provided seeds by the government."
    Passive: "Seeds were provided to the farmers by the government."

6.2 VERBS FOLLOWED BY PREPOSITIONS
When a verb is followed by a preposition, the preposition is retained in the passive voice.

Examples:
1. Active: "They laughed at him."
   Passive: "He was laughed at by them."

2. Active: "The teacher praised her for her work."
   Passive: "She was praised for her work by the teacher."

3. Active: "People speak well of him."
   Passive: "He is well spoken of by people."

4. Active: "They looked into the matter."
   Passive: "The matter was looked into by them."

5. Active: "The committee called for an explanation."
   Passive: "An explanation was called for by the committee."

6. Active: "Everyone laughed at the clown."
   Passive: "The clown was laughed at by everyone."

7. Active: "They sent for the doctor."
   Passive: "The doctor was sent for."

8. Active: "The students complained about the noise."
   Passive: "The noise was complained about by the students."

9. Active: "The police looked for the thief."
   Passive: "The thief was looked for by the police."

10. Active: "We must abide by the law."
    Passive: "The law must be abided by."

6.3 VERBS OF PERCEPTION
Verbs such as see, hear, watch, feel, help, make are used in passive voice with "to" before the bare infinitive.

Examples:
1. Active: "The teacher made the students write the answer."
   Passive: "The students were made to write the answer by the teacher."

2. Active: "They saw him cross the road."
   Passive: "He was seen to cross the road."

3. Active: "We heard her sing a song."
   Passive: "She was heard to sing a song."

4. Active: "The mother made the child drink milk."
   Passive: "The child was made to drink milk by the mother."

5. Active: "They helped him complete the work."
   Passive: "He was helped to complete the work."

6. Active: "The coach made the players practise daily."
   Passive: "The players were made to practise daily by the coach."

7. Active: "People saw the thief enter the house."
   Passive: "The thief was seen to enter the house."

8. Active: "We watched the birds fly."
   Passive: "The birds were watched to fly."

9. Active: "The officer made the clerk sign the register."
   Passive: "The clerk was made to sign the register by the officer."

10. Active: "They heard him shout for help."
    Passive: "He was heard to shout for help."

6.4 QUASI-PASSIVE VERBS
Some verbs such as read, write, sell, wash, wear, open, shut, burn can be used in an active form with a passive meaning.

Examples:
1. "This book reads well." - means "This book is read well."
2. "The pen writes smoothly." - means "The pen writes smoothly when used."
3. "These clothes sell quickly." - means "These clothes are sold quickly."
4. "The floor washes easily." - means "The floor can be washed easily."
5. "The door opens easily." - means "The door can be opened easily."

6.5 PASSIVE WITHOUT "BY"
"By + agent" is omitted when the doer is unknown, unimportant or obvious.

Examples:
1. Active: "Someone has stolen my bicycle."
   Passive: "My bicycle has been stolen."

2. Active: "They speak Tamil in Tamil Nadu."
   Passive: "Tamil is spoken in Tamil Nadu."

3. Active: "People grow paddy in Thanjavur."
   Passive: "Paddy is grown in Thanjavur."

4. Active: "The authorities have cancelled the examination."
   Passive: "The examination has been cancelled."

5. Active: "Someone has broken the chair."
   Passive: "The chair has been broken."

UNIT 7: COMMON ERRORS IN PASSIVE VOICE
==================================================

1. Always use the past participle in passive voice.
   Incorrect: "A letter is write by Ravi."
   Correct: "A letter is written by Ravi."

2. Use the correct form of "be" according to tense.
   Incorrect: "The work is complete by them."
   Correct: "The work is completed by them."

3. Do not use passive voice when the verb has no object.
   Incorrect: "The baby was slept by the mother."
   Correct: "The baby slept."

4. Intransitive verbs cannot normally be changed into passive voice.
   Examples: go, come, sleep, laugh, die, arrive.
   - "He laughed." cannot become "He was laughed."

5. Use "by" only when the doer is mentioned.
   Correct: "The road was repaired."
   Correct: "The road was repaired by the workers."

6. In imperative sentences, use "Let + object + be + past participle".
   Correct: "Let the door be closed."

7. After verbs of perception, use "to" in passive voice.
   Correct: "He was made to write the answer."

UNIT 9: INTRODUCTION TO NARRATION
==================================================

9.1 MEANING
Direct Speech reports the exact words spoken by a person. The words are placed within quotation marks.

Example:
- Direct: Ravi said, "I am busy."

Indirect Speech reports the meaning of the words spoken, without using the exact words or quotation marks.

Example:
- Indirect: Ravi said that he was busy.

9.2 PARTS OF DIRECT SPEECH
A direct speech sentence has two parts:
1. Reporting verb: said, told, asked, exclaimed, ordered, requested.
2. Reported speech: the exact words within quotation marks.

Example:
- He said, "I am tired."
- He said = reporting clause.
- "I am tired." = reported speech.

9.3 BASIC CHANGES FROM DIRECT TO INDIRECT
1. Remove quotation marks.
2. Use "that" after reporting verbs such as "said" and "told" in statements.
3. Change pronouns according to the speaker and listener.
4. Change tense of the reported speech when the reporting verb is in the past tense.
5. Change words showing nearness in time and place.

UNIT 10: CHANGES IN PRONOUNS
==================================================

10.1 FIRST PERSON PRONOUNS
First person pronouns in reported speech change according to the subject of the reporting verb.

Rule:
First person changes according to the subject.

Examples:
1. Direct: He said, "I am ill."
   Indirect: He said that he was ill.

10.2 SECOND PERSON PRONOUNS
Second person pronouns change according to the object of the reporting verb.

Second person changes according to the object.

Third person pronouns do not change.

   Indirect: Kumar said that it was raining.

UNIT 11: CHANGES IN TENSES
==================================================

11.1 GENERAL RULE
If the reporting verb is in the present or future tense, the tense of the reported speech does not change.

Examples:
1. Direct: He says, "I am busy."
   Indirect: He says that he is busy.

2. Direct: She says, "I will come."
   Indirect: She says that she will come.

3. Direct: He will say, "I have finished the work."
   Indirect: He will say that he has finished the work.

If the reporting verb is in the past tense, the tense of the reported speech usually changes.

11.2 TENSE CHANGE CHART
Direct speech tense - Indirect speech tense:
1. Simple Present - Simple Past.
2. Present Continuous - Past Continuous.
3. Present Perfect - Past Perfect.
4. Simple Past - Past Perfect.
5. Past Continuous - Past Perfect Continuous.
6. Past Perfect - Past Perfect (no change).
7. Simple Future - Conditional Future; will becomes would.
8. Can - Could.
9. May - Might.
10. Must - Had to / Must.
11. Shall - Should / Would.

11.3 TENSE CHANGE EXAMPLES
1. Direct: He said, "I write a letter."
   Indirect: He said that he wrote a letter.

2. Direct: She said, "I am reading a book."
   Indirect: She said that she was reading a book.

3. Direct: They said, "We have completed the work."
   Indirect: They said that they had completed the work.

4. Direct: He said, "I wrote a letter."
   Indirect: He said that he had written a letter.

5. Direct: She said, "I was waiting for you."
   Indirect: She said that she had been waiting for me.

6. Direct: He said, "I had finished the work."
   Indirect: He said that he had finished the work.

7. Direct: She said, "I will help you."
   Indirect: She said that she would help me.

8. Direct: He said, "I can swim."
   Indirect: He said that he could swim.

9. Direct: She said, "I may come tomorrow."
   Indirect: She said that she might come the next day.

10. Direct: The teacher said, "You must submit the form."
    Indirect: The teacher said that I had to submit the form.

11.4 EXCEPTIONS TO TENSE CHANGE
The tense does not change in the following cases:

1. Universal truth:
   Direct: The teacher said, "The earth revolves round the sun."
   Indirect: The teacher said that the earth revolves round the sun.

2. Habitual fact:
   Direct: He said, "The sun rises in the east."
   Indirect: He said that the sun rises in the east.

3. Historical fact:
   Direct: The teacher said, "India became independent in 1947."
   Indirect: The teacher said that India became independent in 1947.

4. If the reporting verb is in present or future tense:
   Direct: He says, "I am tired."
   Indirect: He says that he is tired.

UNIT 19: COMMON ERRORS IN NARRATION
==================================================

1. Do not retain quotation marks in indirect speech.
   Incorrect: He said that "he was tired."
   Correct: He said that he was tired.

2. Use "that" in indirect statements.
   Correct: He said that he was tired.

3. Use "if" or "whether" for yes/no questions.
   Correct: He asked whether I was ready.

4. Do not use "if" or "whether" for wh-questions.
   Correct: He asked where I lived.
   Incorrect: He asked if where I lived.

5. Change the tense when the reporting verb is in the past tense.
   Incorrect: He said that he is tired.
   Correct: He said that he was tired.

6. Do not change the tense of universal truths.
   Correct: The teacher said that the earth revolves round the sun.

7. Use "not to" for negative commands.
   Correct: He advised me not to go out.

8. Change "said to" into "told" in indirect statements.
   Correct: He told me that he was busy.

9. Change question order into statement order in indirect speech.
   Correct: He asked me what my name was.
   Incorrect: He asked me what was my name.

10. Change near words into distant words.
   Correct: She said that she would come the next day.

==================================================
UNIT 21: INTRODUCTION TO SENTENCES
==================================================

21.1 MEANING OF A SENTENCE
A sentence is a group of words that makes complete sense. It begins with a capital letter and ends with a full stop, question mark or exclamation mark.

Examples:
- The sun rises in the east.
- Where are you going?
- What a beautiful flower!
- Please close the door.

21.2 CLASSIFICATION OF SENTENCES
Sentences are classified in two ways:
1. According to function or meaning.
2. According to structure or clause pattern.

According to function, there are five types:
1. Assertive or Declarative sentence.
2. Interrogative sentence.
3. Imperative sentence.
4. Exclamatory sentence.
5. Optative sentence.

According to structure, there are three types:
1. Simple sentence.
2. Compound sentence.
3. Complex sentence.

==================================================
UNIT 22: ASSERTIVE OR DECLARATIVE SENTENCES
==================================================

22.1 DEFINITION
An assertive or declarative sentence makes a statement or expresses an opinion. It ends with a full stop.

Examples:
- Chennai is the capital of Tamil Nadu.
- The earth revolves round the sun.
- She is a doctor.
- They have completed the work.
- India is a democratic country.

22.2 TYPES OF ASSERTIVE SENTENCES
1. Affirmative sentence - agrees or states something positively.
   Examples:
   - He is honest.
   - She has come.
   - They are playing.

2. Negative sentence - denies or states something negatively.
   Examples:
   - He is not honest.
   - She has not come.
   - They are not playing.

22.3 AFFIRMATIVE TO NEGATIVE WITHOUT CHANGING MEANING
1. Use "not" with the opposite adjective.
   - Affirmative: "He is brave."
   - Negative: "He is not cowardly."

2. Use "not" with the opposite word.
   - Affirmative: "She is always punctual."
   - Negative: "She is never late."

3. Use "not" with a negative word.
   - Affirmative: "Everyone was present."
   - Negative: "No one was absent."

4. Use "not less than" for "more than".
   - Affirmative: "He is more than sixty years old."
   - Negative: "He is not less than sixty years old."

5. Use "not unsuccessful" for "successful".
   - Affirmative: "The attempt was successful."
   - Negative: "The attempt was not unsuccessful."

==================================================
UNIT 23: INTERROGATIVE SENTENCES
==================================================

23.1 DEFINITION
An interrogative sentence asks a question. It ends with a question mark.

Examples:
- Where do you live?
- Have you finished the work?
- Who is your class teacher?
- Why are you late?
- Can you swim?

23.2 TYPES OF INTERROGATIVE SENTENCES
1. Yes/No type questions - answered by yes or no.
   Examples:
   - Are you ready?
   - Do you know him?
   - Has she come?

2. Wh-type questions - begin with question words.
   Examples:
   - What is your name?
   - Where do you live?
   - When will you come?
   - Why did he leave?
   - How did you solve it?

3. Alternative questions - offer a choice.
   Examples:
   - Do you like tea or coffee?
   - Will you come today or tomorrow?

4. Tag questions - a short question added to a statement.
   Examples:
   - You are coming, aren't you?
   - She has finished, hasn't she?
   - He can swim, can't he?

23.3 ASSERTIVE TO INTERROGATIVE WITHOUT CHANGING MEANING
1. Affirmative assertive becomes negative interrogative.
   - Assertive: "He is honest."
   - Interrogative: "Is he not honest?"

2. Negative assertive becomes affirmative interrogative.
   - Assertive: "He is not honest."
   - Interrogative: "Is he honest?"

3. Use "who" for "nobody".
   - Assertive: "Nobody can do this."
   - Interrogative: "Who can do this?"

4. Use "what" for "nothing".
   - Assertive: "Nothing was done."
   - Interrogative: "What was done?"

5. Use "why" for "no reason".
   - Assertive: "There is no reason to doubt him."
   - Interrogative: "Why should we doubt him?"

==================================================
UNIT 24: IMPERATIVE SENTENCES
==================================================

24.1 DEFINITION
An imperative sentence expresses an order, command, request, advice, suggestion or prohibition. The subject "you" is usually understood but not written.

Examples:
- Close the door.
- Please help me.
- Do not make noise.
- Take rest.
- Let us go now.

24.2 TYPES OF IMPERATIVE SENTENCES
1. Command or order:
   - Stand up.
   - Leave the room.
   - Submit the report.

2. Request:
   - Please help me.
   - Please give me a pen.
   - Kindly wait here.

3. Advice:
   - Work hard.
   - Take exercise daily.
   - Do not waste time.

4. Prohibition:
   - Do not smoke here.
   - Do not touch the wire.
   - Do not tell lies.

5. Suggestion:
   - Let us go for a walk.
   - Let us start early.
   - Let us discuss the matter.

24.3 ASSERTIVE TO IMPERATIVE
An order can be expressed as an imperative sentence.

Examples:
1. Assertive: "You should obey your parents."
   Imperative: "Obey your parents."

2. Assertive: "You must not waste water."
   Imperative: "Do not waste water."

3. Assertive: "You should respect elders."
   Imperative: "Respect elders."

4. Assertive: "You should keep the room clean."
   Imperative: "Keep the room clean."

5. Assertive: "You should not tell lies."
   Imperative: "Do not tell lies."

==================================================
UNIT 25: EXCLAMATORY SENTENCES
==================================================

25.1 DEFINITION
An exclamatory sentence expresses strong emotion such as joy, sorrow, surprise, anger, wonder or approval. It ends with an exclamation mark.

Examples:
- What a beautiful flower!
- How clever she is!
- Hurrah! We have won.
- Alas! He has failed.
- Bravo! Well done.

25.2 STRUCTURE
1. What + a/an + adjective + noun + subject + verb!
   - What a beautiful sight it is!
   - What an intelligent boy he is!

2. How + adjective/adverb + subject + verb!
   - How beautiful the flower is!
   - How quickly he runs!

25.3 ASSERTIVE TO EXCLAMATORY
1. Use "What a" or "How" according to the sentence.
2. Retain the meaning.
3. Use an exclamation mark.

Examples:
1. Assertive: "It is a very beautiful flower."
   Exclamatory: "What a beautiful flower it is!"

2. Assertive: "She is very intelligent."
   Exclamatory: "How intelligent she is!"

3. Assertive: "The boy is very brave."
   Exclamatory: "How brave the boy is!"

4. Assertive: "It is a very difficult question."
   Exclamatory: "What a difficult question it is!"

5. Assertive: "The scenery is very beautiful."
   Exclamatory: "How beautiful the scenery is!"

6. Assertive: "He runs very fast."
   Exclamatory: "How fast he runs!"

7. Assertive: "It is a very good idea."
   Exclamatory: "What a good idea it is!"

8. Assertive: "The child is very cute."
   Exclamatory: "How cute the child is!"

9. Assertive: "It was a very pleasant surprise."
   Exclamatory: "What a pleasant surprise it was!"

10. Assertive: "The mountain is very high."
    Exclamatory: "How high the mountain is!"

UNIT 26: OPTATIVE SENTENCES
==================================================

26.1 DEFINITION
An optative sentence expresses a wish, prayer, blessing or curse. It usually begins with "may" and ends with an exclamation mark.

Examples:
- May you succeed!
- May God bless you!
- May you live long!
- May you recover soon!
- Long live our nation!

26.2 ASSERTIVE TO OPTATIVE
Examples:

   Optative: "May you succeed!"

2. Assertive: "I wish you a long life."
   Optative: "May you live long!"

3. Assertive: "I pray that God may bless you."
   Optative: "May God bless you!"

4. Assertive: "I wish you a speedy recovery."

UNIT 27: SIMPLE, COMPOUND AND COMPLEX SENTENCES
==================================================

27.1 SIMPLE SENTENCE
A simple sentence has only one subject-verb combination and expresses one complete idea. It has one main clause and no subordinate clause.

Examples:
- The boy is reading a book.
- She went to Chennai.
- They play cricket every evening.
- The sun rises in the east.
- He works in a bank.

27.2 COMPOUND SENTENCE
A compound sentence has two or more main clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction such as and, but, or, so, yet, for, nor.

Examples:
- The boy is reading a book, and his sister is writing a letter.
- He was ill, but he attended the class.
- You can take tea or coffee.
- He worked hard, so he passed.
- She was tired, yet she continued working.

27.3 COMPLEX SENTENCE
A complex sentence has one main clause and one or more subordinate clauses. Subordinate clauses are introduced by words such as because, although, when, while, if, since, unless, who, which, that.

Examples:
- When he came, I was reading.
- Because he was ill, he did not attend the class.
- The book that you gave me is interesting.
- If you work hard, you will pass.
- Although he is poor, he is honest.

27.4 SIMPLE TO COMPOUND SENTENCE
Use a coordinating conjunction to join two main clauses.

Examples:
1. Simple: "Being ill, he did not come."
   Compound: "He was ill, so he did not come."

2. Simple: "Besides being intelligent, he is hardworking."
   Compound: "He is intelligent, and he is hardworking."

3. Simple: "In spite of poverty, he is honest."
   Compound: "He is poor, but he is honest."

4. Simple: "On seeing the police, the thief ran away."
   Compound: "The thief saw the police, and he ran away."

5. Simple: "He must work hard to succeed."
   Compound: "He must work hard, or he will not succeed."

6. Simple: "The sun having risen, we started our journey."
   Compound: "The sun rose, and we started our journey."

7. Simple: "Notwithstanding his illness, he attended the meeting."
   Compound: "He was ill, yet he attended the meeting."

8. Simple: "He is too weak to walk."
   Compound: "He is very weak, so he cannot walk."

9. Simple: "Owing to rain, the match was cancelled."
   Compound: "It rained, so the match was cancelled."

10. Simple: "Besides teaching, she writes books."
    Compound: "She teaches, and she also writes books."

27.5 SIMPLE TO COMPLEX SENTENCE
Use a subordinate clause with words such as when, because, if, although, who, which, that.

Examples:
1. Simple: "Being ill, he did not come."
   Complex: "As he was ill, he did not come."

2. Simple: "He worked hard to pass."
   Complex: "He worked hard so that he could pass."

3. Simple: "The boy standing there is my brother."
   Complex: "The boy who is standing there is my brother."

4. Simple: "I saw a wounded bird."
   Complex: "I saw a bird which was wounded."

5. Simple: "On reaching Chennai, he called me."
   Complex: "When he reached Chennai, he called me."

   Complex: "Though he is poor, he is honest."

   Complex: "He is so weak that he cannot walk."
