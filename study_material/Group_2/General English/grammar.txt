PART A: VERBS AND AUXILIARY VERBS
==================================================

==================================================
UNIT 1: INTRODUCTION TO VERBS
==================================================

1.1 MEANING OF A VERB
A verb is a word that expresses an action, an occurrence or a state of being.

Examples of action verbs:
- run, write, eat, play, read, sing, jump, speak.

Examples of state verbs:
- is, am, are, was, were, seem, appear, become, belong.

Examples in sentences:
- Birds fly in the sky.
- She writes a letter.
- He is a teacher.
- The milk smells bad.
- The meeting will happen tomorrow.

1.2 FINITE VERBS
A finite verb changes according to the subject, number, person and tense.

Examples:
- He writes a letter.
- They write letters.
- She wrote a letter.
- I am happy.
- They are happy.

1.3 NON-FINITE VERBS
A non-finite verb does not change according to the subject or tense.

There are three types:
1. Infinitive - to write, to read, to go.
2. Gerund - writing, reading, swimming.
3. Participle - writing, written, broken.

Examples:
- He wants to learn English. (infinitive)
- Reading is a good habit. (gerund)
- The broken chair must be repaired. (participle)

==================================================
UNIT 2: KINDS OF VERBS
==================================================

2.1 MAIN VERBS OR PRINCIPAL VERBS
Main verbs express the main action or state in a sentence.

Examples:
- She writes a letter.
- They play football.
- He eats breakfast.
- We study English.
- The child sleeps early.

2.2 TRANSITIVE VERBS
A transitive verb needs an object to complete its meaning.

Structure:
Subject + Transitive Verb + Object

Examples:
- She wrote a letter.
- He bought a car.
- They built a house.
- The teacher taught the students.
- The boy kicked the ball.

2.3 INTRANSITIVE VERBS
An intransitive verb does not need an object.

Examples:
- Birds fly.
- The baby slept.
- He laughed loudly.
- The sun rises in the east.
- The train arrived late.

Some verbs can be both transitive and intransitive:
- She sings a song. (transitive)
- She sings well. (intransitive)
- He reads a book. (transitive)
- He reads slowly. (intransitive)

2.4 LINKING VERBS
Linking verbs connect the subject with a word that describes or identifies it.

Common linking verbs:
is, am, are, was, were, become, seem, appear, look, feel, taste, smell, sound.

Examples:
- She is intelligent.
- The milk smells bad.
- He became angry.
- The flower looks beautiful.
- The soup tastes good.

Important rule:
After a linking verb, use an adjective, not an adverb.
- Correct: "She looks beautiful."
- Incorrect: "She looks beautifully."
- Correct: "The food tastes good."
- Incorrect: "The food tastes well."

2.5 REGULAR AND IRREGULAR VERBS
Regular verbs form their past tense and past participle by adding -ed or -d.

Examples:
- work - worked - worked
- play - played - played
- walk - walked - walked
- clean - cleaned - cleaned
- help - helped - helped

Irregular verbs change their forms irregularly.

Important irregular verbs:
- go - went - gone
- eat - ate - eaten
- see - saw - seen
- write - wrote - written
- drink - drank - drunk
- sing - sang - sung
- take - took - taken
- give - gave - given
- buy - bought - bought
- bring - brought - brought
- teach - taught - taught
- catch - caught - caught
- begin - began - begun
- break - broke - broken
- choose - chose - chosen
- speak - spoke - spoken
- swim - swam - swum
- fly - flew - flown
- grow - grew - grown
- know - knew - known

UNIT 3: AUXILIARY VERBS
==================================================

3.1 MEANING OF AUXILIARY VERBS
Auxiliary verbs are helping verbs. They help the main verb to form tenses, questions, negatives, emphasis and passive voice.

Common auxiliary verbs:
be, am, is, are, was, were, being, been,
have, has, had,
do, does, did,
will, would, shall, should,
can, could, may, might, must, ought to.

3.2 PRIMARY AUXILIARY VERBS
The primary auxiliaries are be, do and have.

1. Be forms: am, is, are, was, were, being, been.
   Uses:
   - Continuous tenses: "She is writing."
   - Passive voice: "The letter was written."
   - Questions: "Is he a doctor?"
   - Negatives: "He is not a doctor."

2. Do forms: do, does, did.
   Uses:
   - Questions: "Do you know him?"
   - Negatives: "He does not know her."
   - Emphasis: "I do like coffee."
   - Short answers: "Yes, I do."

3. Have forms: have, has, had.
   Uses:
   - Perfect tenses: "She has finished the work."
   - Passive perfect: "The work has been finished."
   - Questions: "Have you eaten?"
   - Negatives: "He has not come."

3.3 MODAL AUXILIARY VERBS
Modal auxiliaries express ability, possibility, permission, obligation, advice, request, necessity and probability.

Common modals:
can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to.

Important rule:
After a modal verb, use the base form of the main verb.
- Correct: "He can swim."
- Incorrect: "He can swims."
- Correct: "You must go."
- Incorrect: "You must to go."

3.4 USES OF MODAL VERBS

1. Can - ability, permission or possibility.
   - She can speak Tamil.
   - Can I use your phone?
   - It can be dangerous.

2. Could - past ability, polite request or possibility.
   - When he was young, he could run fast.
   - Could you help me?
   - It could rain later.

3. May - permission or possibility.
   - May I come in?
   - It may rain today.

4. Might - slight possibility.
   - He might come late.
   - She might know the answer.

5. Must - strong necessity or obligation.
   - You must obey the law.
   - We must wear helmets.

6. Should - advice, duty or expectation.
   - You should study regularly.
   - Students should respect teachers.

7. Will - future action, promise or determination.
   - I will help you.
   - The train will arrive soon.

8. Would - polite request, past habit or imagined situation.
   - Would you please open the window?
   - He would go for a walk every morning.
   - If I were rich, I would help the poor.

9. Shall - suggestion, offer or future with I/we.
   - Shall we go now?
   - Shall I bring water?

10. Ought to - moral duty.
    - We ought to help the poor.
    - You ought to respect elders.

3.5 SEMI-MODALS
Semi-modals behave like modal verbs in some ways.
Examples: need, dare, used to, have to, be able to.

Examples:
- You need not worry.
- How dare you speak like that?
- He used to live in Madurai.
- I have to submit the form today.
- She is able to speak French.

3.6 QUESTION AND NEGATIVE FORMS
1. With be:
   - Statement: "She is a teacher."
   - Question: "Is she a teacher?"
   - Negative: "She is not a teacher."

2. With have:
   - Statement: "He has finished."
   - Question: "Has he finished?"
   - Negative: "He has not finished."

3. With do:
   - Statement: "He knows her."
   - Question: "Does he know her?"
   - Negative: "He does not know her."

4. With modals:
   - Statement: "He can swim."
   - Question: "Can he swim?"
   - Negative: "He cannot swim."

UNIT 4: SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT WITH VERBS
==================================================

4.1 BASIC RULE
A singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb.

Examples:
- The boy plays cricket.
- The boys play cricket.
- She writes a letter.
- They write letters.

4.2 IMPORTANT RULES
1. Two subjects joined by "and" take a plural verb.
   - Ravi and Kumar are friends.

2. One idea expressed by two nouns takes a singular verb.
   - Bread and butter is my breakfast.

3. Each, every, either and neither take singular verbs.
   - Each student has a book.
   - Every boy was present.

4. One of the + plural noun takes a singular verb.
   - One of the boys is absent.

5. A number of takes a plural verb.
   - A number of students were absent.

6. The number of takes a singular verb.
   - The number of students is small.

7. Collective nouns take singular verbs when the group acts as one unit.
   - The committee has decided.

8. Uncountable nouns take singular verbs.
   - The news is good.
   - The furniture is new.

9. Nouns such as police, people and cattle take plural verbs.
   - The police have arrested the thief.

10. Words such as mathematics, physics and news take singular verbs.
    - Mathematics is interesting.

PART B: INFINITIVES, GERUNDS AND PARTICIPLES

==================================================
UNIT 6: NON-FINITE VERBS
==================================================

6.1 MEANING
Non-finite verbs do not change according to the subject, number, person or tense.

There are three types:
1. Infinitive.
2. Gerund.
3. Participle.

Examples:
- To read is a good habit. (infinitive)
- Reading is a good habit. (gerund)
- The reading boy is my brother. (present participle)
- The broken chair is old. (past participle)

==================================================
UNIT 7: INFINITIVE
==================================================

7.1 DEFINITION
An infinitive is the base form of a verb, usually preceded by "to".

Examples:
- to read, to write, to go, to eat, to learn.

Examples in sentences:
- He wants to learn English.
- To err is human.
- She went to buy vegetables.
- I have a book to read.
- It is time to go.

7.2 USES OF INFINITIVE
1. As the subject of a sentence:
   - To help others is a virtue.
   - To tell lies is wrong.

2. As the object of a verb:
   - He wants to become a doctor.
   - She decided to resign.
   - They agreed to help us.

3. After certain adjectives:
   - It is easy to learn English.
   - She is happy to see you.
   - He is ready to go.

4. After certain nouns:
   - He has a desire to succeed.
   - She gave me a book to read.
   - It is time to leave.

5. To express purpose:
   - He went to Chennai to attend an interview.
   - She works hard to pass the examination.
   - We save money to buy a house.

7.3 BARE INFINITIVE
A bare infinitive is an infinitive without "to". It is used after certain verbs.

Common verbs followed by bare infinitive:
make, let, hear, see, watch, feel, help, bid, know.

Examples:
- The teacher made the students write the answer.
- Let him go.
- I saw him cross the road.
- We heard her sing.
- He helped me carry the bag.

But in the passive voice, "to" is used:
- The students were made to write the answer.
- He was seen to cross the road.
- She was heard to sing.

7.4 SPLIT INFINITIVE
A split infinitive occurs when a word, usually an adverb, comes between "to" and the verb.

Examples:
- to quickly run
- to carefully read

In formal English, it is better to avoid split infinitives.
- Better: "to run quickly"
- Better: "to read carefully"

==================================================
UNIT 8: GERUND
==================================================

8.1 DEFINITION
A gerund is a verb form ending in -ing which functions as a noun.

Examples:
- Reading is a good habit.
- Swimming is good exercise.
- I enjoy reading novels.
- He is fond of gardening.
- She is interested in painting.

8.2 USES OF GERUND
1. As the subject of a verb:
   - Smoking is injurious to health.
   - Walking keeps us healthy.
   - Collecting stamps is his hobby.

2. As the object of a verb:
   - I enjoy reading.
   - He avoided answering the question.
   - She likes cooking.

3. As the object of a preposition:
   - He is fond of music.
   - She is good at painting.
   - He insisted on going.
   - She is interested in learning French.

4. As a complement:
   - His hobby is gardening.
   - What I like most is reading.
   - Her favourite activity is swimming.

5. After certain expressions:
   - It is no use crying.
   - It is worth reading.
   - There is no harm in asking.
   - He is used to getting up early.

8.3 VERBS FOLLOWED BY GERUND
Common verbs followed by a gerund:
enjoy, avoid, mind, finish, stop, postpone, suggest, consider, practise, keep, deny, admit, risk, escape, imagine, miss, dislike.

Examples:
- I enjoy reading novels.
- He avoided meeting her.
- Would you mind opening the window?
- She finished writing the letter.
- They postponed holding the meeting.
- He suggested going by train.
- She practised speaking English.
- He denied committing the mistake.

8.4 GERUND VS PRESENT PARTICIPLE
Both gerunds and present participles end in -ing, but their functions differ.

Gerund functions as a noun:
- Swimming is good exercise.
- I like swimming.

Present participle functions as an adjective or forms continuous tense:
- The swimming boy is my brother.
- He is swimming in the river.

==================================================
UNIT 9: PARTICIPLE
==================================================

9.1 DEFINITION
A participle is a non-finite verb form which functions as an adjective or helps to form a tense.

There are two main participles:
1. Present participle - verb + ing.
2. Past participle - third form of the verb.

9.2 PRESENT PARTICIPLE
A present participle ends in -ing.

Uses:
1. To form continuous tenses:
   - He is reading.
   - They were playing.

2. As an adjective:
   - The crying child needs help.
   - The running boy won the race.
   - The barking dog frightened him.

3. To show two actions happening at the same time:
   - Walking along the road, he saw a snake.
   - Singing a song, she entered the room.

9.3 PAST PARTICIPLE
A past participle is usually the third form of the verb.

Examples:
- written, broken, taken, given, seen, done, made.

Uses:
1. To form perfect tenses:
   - He has written a letter.
   - They had finished the work.

2. To form passive voice:
   - The letter was written by him.
   - The window was broken by the boy.

3. As an adjective:
   - The broken window must be repaired.
   - This is a written statement.
   - The stolen bicycle was found.

9.4 PERFECT PARTICIPLE
A perfect participle is formed with "having + past participle".

Examples:
- Having finished the work, he went home.
- Having seen the police, the thief ran away.
- Having completed the course, she got a job.

9.5 DANGLING PARTICIPLE
A dangling participle does not correctly modify any word in the sentence.

Incorrect: "Walking in the garden, the flowers were beautiful."
Correct: "Walking in the garden, I saw beautiful flowers."

Incorrect: "Having finished the work, the room was cleaned."
Correct: "Having finished the work, he cleaned the room."

==================================================
UNIT 10: INFINITIVE VS GERUND
==================================================

10.1 VERBS FOLLOWED BY INFINITIVE
Common verbs followed by an infinitive:
want, hope, decide, agree, promise, plan, learn, refuse, offer, afford, manage, fail, expect, wish, attempt.

Examples:
- He wants to go.
- She decided to resign.
- They agreed to help.
- He promised to come.
- She learned to swim.
- He refused to answer.

10.2 VERBS FOLLOWED BY GERUND
Common verbs followed by a gerund:
enjoy, avoid, mind, finish, stop, postpone, suggest, consider, practise, keep, deny, admit, risk, escape, imagine, dislike.

Examples:
- I enjoy reading.
- He avoided meeting her.
- She finished cooking.
- They suggested going by train.
- He denied stealing the money.

10.3 VERBS FOLLOWED BY EITHER INFINITIVE OR GERUND
Some verbs can take either form with little or no change in meaning.
Examples: like, love, hate, prefer, begin, start, continue.

Examples:
- I like to read. / I like reading.
- She began to cry. / She began crying.
- They continued to work. / They continued working.

But some verbs change meaning:
- He stopped to smoke. (he stopped in order to smoke)
- He stopped smoking. (he gave up smoking)
- I remembered to post the letter. (I did not forget)
- I remembered posting the letter. (I recalled that I had posted it)

==================================================
UNIT 12: INTRODUCTION TO QUESTION TAGS
==================================================

12.1 MEANING
A question tag is a short question added at the end of a statement to seek confirmation or agreement.

Examples:
- You are coming, aren't you?
- She has finished, hasn't she?
- He can swim, can't he?
- They went home, didn't they?

12.2 BASIC RULES
1. A positive statement takes a negative question tag.
   - He is a doctor, isn't he?

2. A negative statement takes a positive question tag.
   - He is not a doctor, is he?

3. Use the same auxiliary verb in the tag as in the statement.
   - She has come, hasn't she?
   - They can swim, can't they?

4. If there is no auxiliary verb, use do, does or did.
   - He plays cricket, doesn't he?
   - They went home, didn't they?

5. The pronoun in the tag must match the subject.
   - Ravi is a teacher, isn't he?
   - The girls are dancing, aren't they?

6. The tag is usually followed by a question mark.

==================================================
UNIT 13: QUESTION TAGS WITH DIFFERENT VERBS
==================================================

13.1 WITH "BE"
- He is a teacher, isn't he?
- They are students, aren't they?
- She was present, wasn't she?
- They were absent, weren't they?
- I am late, aren't I?

Important:
"I am" takes the tag "aren't I", not "amn't I".

13.2 WITH "HAVE"
When "have" is a main verb meaning possession, both forms may be used in modern English:
- He has a car, hasn't he? / doesn't he?
- She had a pen, hadn't she? / didn't she?

When "have" is an auxiliary verb:
- He has finished, hasn't he?
- They have gone, haven't they?
- She had left, hadn't she?

13.3 WITH "DO"
When the statement has no auxiliary verb, use do, does or did.

- He plays cricket, doesn't he?
- She knows Tamil, doesn't she?
- They work hard, don't they?
- He came yesterday, didn't he?
- She finished the work, didn't she?

13.4 WITH MODAL VERBS
- He can swim, can't he?
- She could dance, couldn't she?
- You may go, mayn't you? / can you?
- He must come, mustn't he?
- We should help others, shouldn't we?
- They will come, won't they?
- He would help, wouldn't he?
- You ought to obey, oughtn't you? / shouldn't you?

13.5 WITH NEGATIVE WORDS
Words such as never, seldom, rarely, hardly, scarcely, barely and little are negative in meaning. Therefore, they take a positive tag.

Examples:
- He never tells lies, does he?
- She seldom comes late, does she?
- He hardly knows her, does he?
- They rarely visit us, do they?
- There is little water, is there?

UNIT 14: QUESTION TAGS WITH SPECIAL SUBJECTS
==================================================

14.1 THERE
Use "there" in the tag.
- There is a temple here, isn't there?
- There are many students, aren't there?
- There was a meeting, wasn't there?

14.2 THIS / THAT
Use "it" in the tag.
- This is your book, isn't it?
- That was a good film, wasn't it?

14.3 THESE / THOSE
Use "they" in the tag.
- These are your books, aren't they?
- Those were expensive shoes, weren't they?

14.4 EVERYONE, EVERYBODY, SOMEONE, SOMEBODY, NOBODY, NO ONE
Use "they" in the tag.
- Everyone is present, aren't they?
- Somebody has taken my pen, haven't they?
- Nobody came, did they?
- No one knows the answer, do they?

14.5 NOTHING, SOMETHING, EVERYTHING
Use "it" in the tag.
- Nothing happened, did it?
- Something is wrong, isn't it?
- Everything is ready, isn't it?

14.6 ONE
Use "one" in formal English.
- One should do one's duty, shouldn't one?

14.7 IMPERATIVE SENTENCES
For a request or command, use "will you" or "won't you".
- Open the door, will you?
- Please help me, will you?
- Do not make noise, will you?
- Come here, won't you?

14.8 LET'S
Use "shall we" after "let's".
- Let's go for a walk, shall we?
- Let's start early, shall we?

14.9 EXCLAMATORY SENTENCES
For a negative exclamatory sentence, use a positive tag.
- What a beautiful sight, isn't it?
- How clever she is, isn't she?

==================================================
UNIT 16: BASIC SENTENCE PATTERNS
==================================================

16.1 MEANING
A sentence pattern shows the arrangement of subject, verb, object, complement and other parts in a sentence.

The five basic elements are:
1. Subject (S)
2. Verb (V)
3. Object (O)
4. Complement (C)
5. Adverbial (A)

16.2 SUBJECT
The subject is the person or thing about which something is said.

Examples:
- Ravi plays cricket.
- The sun rises in the east.
- Honesty is the best policy.

16.3 VERB
The verb shows the action or state.

Examples:
- Ravi plays cricket.
- The sun rises in the east.
- She is a doctor.

16.4 OBJECT
The object receives the action of the verb.

There are two types:
1. Direct object - receives the direct action.
2. Indirect object - receives the direct object.

Examples:
- He gave me a book.
- He gave = verb.
- me = indirect object.
- a book = direct object.

- She sent her friend a letter.
- her friend = indirect object.
- a letter = direct object.

16.5 COMPLEMENT
A complement completes the meaning of the subject or object.

There are two types:
1. Subject complement - describes or identifies the subject.
2. Object complement - describes or identifies the object.

Examples:
- She is a doctor. (subject complement)
- He became angry. (subject complement)
- They elected him captain. (object complement)
- We found the movie interesting. (object complement)

16.6 ADVERBIAL
An adverbial tells when, where, how, why or how often something happens.

Examples:
- He came yesterday.
- She lives in Chennai.
- He speaks clearly.
- They go to the temple every week.

UNIT 17: FIVE BASIC SENTENCE PATTERNS
==================================================

17.1 SV PATTERN
Subject + Verb

Examples:
- Birds fly.
- The baby slept.
- The sun rises.
- He laughed.
- The train arrived.

17.2 SVO PATTERN
Subject + Verb + Object

Examples:
- Ravi plays cricket.
- She writes a letter.
- They built a house.
- The teacher taught English.
- The boy kicked the ball.

17.3 SVC PATTERN
Subject + Verb + Complement

Examples:
- She is a doctor.
- He became angry.
- The milk smells bad.
- They are students.
- The sky is blue.

17.4 SVA PATTERN
Subject + Verb + Adverbial

Examples:
- He lives in Chennai.
- She came yesterday.
- They play outside.
- The meeting begins at 10 a.m.
- He works hard.

17.5 SVOO PATTERN
Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object

Examples:
- He gave me a book.
- She sent her friend a letter.
- The teacher taught us English.
- They offered him a job.
- The principal awarded the student a prize.

17.6 SVOC PATTERN
Subject + Verb + Object + Complement

Examples:
- They elected him captain.
- We found the movie interesting.
- The news made her happy.
- He considered the plan useful.
- She painted the wall blue.

17.7 SVOA PATTERN
Subject + Verb + Object + Adverbial

Examples:
- He placed the book on the table.
- She kept the money in the bank.
- They built the house near the river.
- He put the pen in his bag.
- The gardener planted the trees in the garden.

=================================================
UNIT 20: INTRODUCTION TO PHRASES AND CLAUSES
==================================================

20.1 MEANING OF A PHRASE
A phrase is a group of words that makes sense but does not contain a subject-verb combination that forms a complete sentence.

Examples:
- in the morning
- on the table
- a beautiful flower
- very quickly
- to buy a book

Examples in sentences:
- He came in the morning.
- The book is on the table.
- She bought a beautiful flower.
- He walked very quickly.
- He went to buy a book.

20.2 MEANING OF A CLAUSE
A clause is a group of words containing a subject and a verb.

Examples:
- when he came
- because she was ill
- who lives in Chennai
- that he is honest
- if you work hard

Examples in sentences:
- When he came, I was reading.
- Because she was ill, she stayed at home.
- The man who lives in Chennai is my uncle.
- He said that he was honest.
- If you work hard, you will pass.

20.3 DIFFERENCE BETWEEN PHRASE AND CLAUSE
Phrase:
- Does not have both subject and finite verb.
- Cannot stand alone as a complete sentence.
Example: "in the morning".

Clause:
- Has both subject and finite verb.
- May or may not stand alone as a complete sentence.
Example: "when he came".

==================================================
UNIT 21: KINDS OF PHRASES
==================================================

21.1 NOUN PHRASE
A noun phrase is a group of words that functions as a noun.

Examples:
- The tall boy won the prize.
- A beautiful flower is in the vase.
- The man in the blue shirt is my uncle.
- Reading books is a good habit.
- To help others is a virtue.

21.2 VERB PHRASE
A verb phrase consists of the main verb and its helping verbs.

Examples:
- She is writing a letter.
- They have finished the work.
- He will go to Chennai.
- The road was repaired.
- She must be waiting.

21.3 ADJECTIVE PHRASE
An adjective phrase is a group of words that functions as an adjective.

Examples:
- She is a very intelligent girl.
- He is a man of great courage.
- The road is too narrow for lorries.
- The boy in the red shirt is my cousin.
- This is a book worth reading.

21.4 ADVERB PHRASE
An adverb phrase is a group of words that functions as an adverb.

Examples:
- He walked very slowly.
- She will come in the evening.
- They live near the railway station.
- He spoke in a loud voice.
- The child slept for two hours.

21.5 PREPOSITIONAL PHRASE
A prepositional phrase begins with a preposition and ends with a noun or pronoun.

Examples:
- The book is on the table.
- He came from Madurai.
- The house near the temple belongs to Ravi.
- She sat beside her mother.
- They walked along the beach.

21.6 INFINITIVE PHRASE
An infinitive phrase begins with "to" and includes the base verb and its objects or modifiers.

Examples:
- He went to buy vegetables.
- To help the poor is a virtue.
- She has a desire to become a doctor.
- He came to meet me.
- They work hard to succeed.

21.7 PARTICIPIAL PHRASE
A participial phrase begins with a present participle or past participle and functions as an adjective.

Examples:
- Walking along the road, he saw a snake.
- The boy standing near the gate is my cousin.
- The broken window must be repaired.
- Having finished the work, he went home.
- The car parked outside belongs to Ravi.

21.8 GERUND PHRASE
A gerund phrase begins with a gerund and functions as a noun.

Examples:
- Reading novels is her hobby.
- He is fond of playing cricket.
- She avoided answering the question.
- Collecting stamps is an interesting hobby.
- His habit is getting up early.

==================================================
UNIT 22: KINDS OF CLAUSES
==================================================

22.1 MAIN CLAUSE OR INDEPENDENT CLAUSE
A main clause can stand alone as a complete sentence.

Examples:
- He came home.
- She is reading.
- They play cricket.
- I met him yesterday.
- The train arrived late.

22.2 SUBORDINATE CLAUSE OR DEPENDENT CLAUSE
A subordinate clause cannot stand alone as a complete sentence. It depends on the main clause.

Examples:
- when he came
- because she was ill
- if you work hard
- who lives in Chennai
- that he is honest

22.3 KINDS OF SUBORDINATE CLAUSES
There are three kinds:
1. Noun clause.
2. Adjective clause.
3. Adverb clause.

==================================================
UNIT 23: NOUN CLAUSE
==================================================

23.1 DEFINITION
A noun clause functions as a noun in a sentence. It can be the subject, object or complement.

Common introductory words:
that, what, who, whom, whose, which, when, where, why, how, whether, if.

23.2 NOUN CLAUSE AS SUBJECT
- What he said is true.
- That he failed was unexpected.
- How the accident happened is unknown.
- Whether he will come is uncertain.
- Who broke the window is not known.

23.3 NOUN CLAUSE AS OBJECT
- He said that he was busy.
- I know what you want.
- She asked where I lived.
- Tell me why you came late.
- He does not know whether she will come.

23.4 NOUN CLAUSE AS COMPLEMENT
- The problem is that we have no money.
- My belief is that hard work pays.
- The question is whether he will help.
- This is what I wanted.
- The truth is that he was absent.

23.5 NOUN CLAUSE AFTER A PREPOSITION
- It depends on what he says.
- We are concerned about whether he will come.
- Pay attention to what the teacher says.

==================================================
UNIT 24: ADJECTIVE CLAUSE
==================================================

24.1 DEFINITION
An adjective clause describes or modifies a noun or pronoun. It is also called a relative clause.

Common relative pronouns:
who, whom, whose, which, that.

Relative adverbs:
when, where, why.

24.2 WITH "WHO"
Used for persons.
- The man who came here is my uncle.
- The students who work hard will pass.
- The woman who spoke to you is my aunt.

24.3 WITH "WHICH"
Used for animals and things.
- The book which I bought is interesting.
- The house which is near the temple belongs to Ravi.
- The dog which barked loudly is mine.

24.4 WITH "THAT"
Used for persons, animals and things.
- The boy that came here is my cousin.
- The book that you gave me is useful.
- The house that he built is beautiful.

24.5 WITH "WHOSE"
Shows possession.
- The girl whose father is a doctor is my friend.
- The house whose roof was damaged has been repaired.
- The student whose answer was correct got the prize.

24.6 WITH "WHOM"
Used as the object of a verb or preposition.
- The man whom I met is a doctor.
- The girl whom you helped is my sister.
- The person to whom I spoke was polite.

24.7 WITH RELATIVE ADVERBS
- I remember the day when we met.
- This is the place where he was born.
- Tell me the reason why you came late.

==================================================
UNIT 25: ADVERB CLAUSE
==================================================

25.1 DEFINITION
An adverb clause modifies a verb, an adjective or another adverb. It tells time, place, reason, purpose, result, condition, concession, comparison or manner.

25.2 ADVERB CLAUSE OF TIME
Introduced by: when, while, before, after, until, till, since, as soon as, whenever.
- When he came, I was reading.
- Wait until I return.
- As soon as he arrived, we started.
- While she was cooking, the phone rang.
- I have known him since 2010.

25.3 ADVERB CLAUSE OF PLACE
Introduced by: where, wherever.
- Sit where you like.
- He will go wherever he finds work.
- This is the place where the accident happened.

25.4 ADVERB CLAUSE OF REASON
Introduced by: because, since, as, now that.
- He stayed at home because he was ill.
- Since it was raining, we stayed indoors.
- As he was tired, he went to bed early.

25.5 ADVERB CLAUSE OF PURPOSE
Introduced by: so that, in order that, lest.
- Work hard so that you may pass.
- She spoke slowly in order that everyone could understand.
- Walk carefully lest you should fall.

25.6 ADVERB CLAUSE OF RESULT
Introduced by: so...that, such...that.
- He was so tired that he could not walk.
- It was such a good film that everyone liked it.
- The question was so difficult that nobody could answer it.

25.7 ADVERB CLAUSE OF CONDITION
Introduced by: if, unless, provided, in case, as long as.
- If you work hard, you will pass.
- Unless you apologise, he will not forgive you.
- You may go provided you return early.
- In case it rains, take an umbrella.

25.8 ADVERB CLAUSE OF CONCESSION
Introduced by: though, although, even though, while.
- Though he is poor, he is honest.
- Although it rained, we went out.
- Even though he was tired, he continued working.

25.9 ADVERB CLAUSE OF COMPARISON
Introduced by: than, as...as, so...as.
- She is taller than her sister.
- He is as brave as a lion.
- It is not so cold as yesterday.

25.10 ADVERB CLAUSE OF MANNER
Introduced by: as, as if, as though.
- Do as I say.
- He behaves as if he were a king.
- She sings as though she were a professional singer.

PART F: DEGREES OF COMPARISON

==================================================
UNIT 27: INTRODUCTION TO DEGREES OF COMPARISON
==================================================

27.1 MEANING
Adjectives and adverbs have three degrees of comparison:
1. Positive degree.
2. Comparative degree.
3. Superlative degree.

27.2 POSITIVE DEGREE
The positive degree describes one person, animal or thing without comparison.

Examples:
- Ram is tall.
- This flower is beautiful.
- He runs fast.
- She is intelligent.
- The problem is difficult.

27.3 COMPARATIVE DEGREE
The comparative degree compares two persons, animals or things.

Examples:
- Ram is taller than Kumar.
- This flower is more beautiful than that one.
- He runs faster than his brother.
- She is more intelligent than her friend.
- This problem is more difficult than that one.

27.4 SUPERLATIVE DEGREE
The superlative degree compares one person, animal or thing with many others.

Examples:
- Ram is the tallest boy in the class.
- This is the most beautiful flower in the garden.
- He runs the fastest of all.
- She is the most intelligent student.
- This is the most difficult problem.

==================================================
UNIT 28: FORMATION OF COMPARATIVE AND SUPERLATIVE
==================================================

28.1 ONE-SYLLABLE ADJECTIVES
Add -er and -est.

Examples:
- tall - taller - tallest
- small - smaller - smallest
- old - older - oldest
- strong - stronger - strongest
- short - shorter - shortest

28.2 ADJECTIVES ENDING IN -E
Add -r and -st.

Examples:
- large - larger - largest
- brave - braver - bravest
- wise - wiser - wisest
- nice - nicer - nicest
- safe - safer - safest

28.3 ADJECTIVES ENDING IN CONSONANT + Y
Change y into i and add -er/-est.

Examples:
- happy - happier - happiest
- easy - easier - easiest
- heavy - heavier - heaviest
- busy - busier - busiest
- early - earlier - earliest

28.4 ONE VOWEL + ONE CONSONANT
Double the final consonant and add -er/-est.

Examples:
- big - bigger - biggest
- hot - hotter - hottest
- thin - thinner - thinnest
- fat - fatter - fattest
- sad - sadder - saddest

28.5 ADJECTIVES OF THREE OR MORE SYLLABLES
Use "more" and "most".

Examples:
- beautiful - more beautiful - most beautiful
- intelligent - more intelligent - most intelligent
- important - more important - most important
- difficult - more difficult - most difficult
- expensive - more expensive - most expensive

28.6 SOME TWO-SYLLABLE ADJECTIVES
Some two-syllable adjectives can take either form.

Examples:
- clever - cleverer / more clever - cleverest / most clever
- simple - simpler / more simple - simplest / most simple
- narrow - narrower / more narrow - narrowest / most narrow
- gentle - gentler / more gentle - gentlest / most gentle
- quiet - quieter / more quiet - quietest / most quiet

==================================================
UNIT 29: IRREGULAR COMPARISON
==================================================

Positive - Comparative - Superlative:
- good - better - best
- well - better - best
- bad - worse - worst
- badly - worse - worst
- little - less - least
- much - more - most
- many - more - most
- far - farther / further - farthest / furthest
- old - older / elder - oldest / eldest
- late - later / latter - latest / last

Important differences:
1. Elder and eldest are used only for members of the same family.
   - My elder brother is a doctor.
   - She is my eldest sister.

2. Older and oldest can be used for persons and things.
   - This is the oldest temple in the town.
   - He is older than his friend.

3. Farther refers to physical distance.
   - Chennai is farther than Coimbatore.

4. Further means additional.
   - For further information, contact the office.

5. Later refers to time.
   - I will meet you later.

6. Latter refers to the second of two things already mentioned.
   - Of tea and coffee, I prefer the latter.

7. Latest means most recent.
   - This is the latest news.

8. Last means final.
   - This is the last chance.

==================================================
UNIT 30: RULES FOR USING DEGREES
==================================================

30.1 COMPARATIVE DEGREE
1. Comparative degree is followed by "than".
   - She is taller than her sister.
   - Chennai is bigger than Madurai.

2. Use "than any other" when comparing one member with all other members of the same group.
   - Chennai is bigger than any other city in Tamil Nadu.

3. Use "than any" when comparing a thing with a different group.
   - Chennai is bigger than any city in Kerala.

4. Do not use double comparatives.
   - Incorrect: "more better"
   - Correct: "better"

5. Use "much", "far", "a lot" or "rather" before a comparative degree.
   - She is much better today.
   - This book is far more useful.
   - He is a lot taller than his brother.

30.2 SUPERLATIVE DEGREE
1. Superlative degree is usually preceded by "the".
   - He is the best student.
   - This is the most beautiful garden.

2. Use "of all" or "in" after the superlative degree.
   - He is the tallest of all.
   - She is the best student in the class.

3. Do not use double superlatives.
   - Incorrect: "most best"
   - Correct: "best"

30.3 POSITIVE DEGREE
1. Use "as...as" for affirmative comparison.
   - He is as brave as a lion.
   - She is as intelligent as her sister.

2. Use "so...as" in negative comparison.
   - He is not so brave as a lion.
   - This book is not so useful as that one.

3. Use "the same as" to show equality.
   - My bag is the same as yours.

4. Use "different from" to show difference.
   - This book is different from that one.

UNIT 31: TRANSFORMATION OF DEGREES
==================================================

31.1 POSITIVE TO COMPARATIVE
Rule:
Use "as...as" in positive degree and "-er than" or "more...than" in comparative degree.

Examples:
1. Positive: "Ram is as tall as Kumar."
   Comparative: "Kumar is not taller than Ram."

2. Positive: "This book is as useful as that one."
   Comparative: "That book is not more useful than this book."

3. Positive: "He is as brave as a lion."
   Comparative: "A lion is not braver than he is."

4. Positive: "She is as intelligent as her sister."
   Comparative: "Her sister is not more intelligent than she is."

5. Positive: "This road is as wide as that road."
   Comparative: "That road is not wider than this road."

31.2 COMPARATIVE TO POSITIVE
Examples:
1. Comparative: "Ram is taller than Kumar."
   Positive: "Kumar is not as tall as Ram."

2. Comparative: "Chennai is bigger than Madurai."
   Positive: "Madurai is not as big as Chennai."

3. Comparative: "He is stronger than his brother."
   Positive: "His brother is not as strong as he is."

4. Comparative: "This problem is more difficult than that one."
   Positive: "That problem is not as difficult as this problem."

5. Comparative: "She sings better than her friend."
   Positive: "Her friend does not sing as well as she does."

31.3 COMPARATIVE TO SUPERLATIVE
Rule:
Use "than any other" in comparative and "the + superlative" in superlative degree.

Examples:
1. Comparative: "Chennai is bigger than any other city in Tamil Nadu."
   Superlative: "Chennai is the biggest city in Tamil Nadu."

2. Comparative: "He is better than any other student in the class."
   Superlative: "He is the best student in the class."

3. Comparative: "This is more useful than any other book."
   Superlative: "This is the most useful book."

4. Comparative: "She is more intelligent than any other girl in the class."
   Superlative: "She is the most intelligent girl in the class."

5. Comparative: "The Cauvery is longer than any other river in Tamil Nadu."
   Superlative: "The Cauvery is the longest river in Tamil Nadu."

31.4 SUPERLATIVE TO COMPARATIVE
Examples:
1. Superlative: "Chennai is the biggest city in Tamil Nadu."
   Comparative: "Chennai is bigger than any other city in Tamil Nadu."

2. Superlative: "He is the best student in the class."
   Comparative: "He is better than any other student in the class."

3. Superlative: "This is the most useful book."
   Comparative: "This is more useful than any other book."

4. Superlative: "She is the most intelligent girl in the class."
   Comparative: "She is more intelligent than any other girl in the class."

5. Superlative: "The Cauvery is the longest river in Tamil Nadu."
   Comparative: "The Cauvery is longer than any other river in Tamil Nadu."

31.5 SUPERLATIVE TO POSITIVE
Examples:
1. Superlative: "Chennai is the biggest city in Tamil Nadu."
   Positive: "No other city in Tamil Nadu is as big as Chennai."

2. Superlative: "He is the best student in the class."
   Positive: "No other student in the class is as good as he is."

3. Superlative: "This is the most useful book."
   Positive: "No other book is as useful as this book."

4. Superlative: "She is the most intelligent girl in the class."
   Positive: "No other girl in the class is as intelligent as she is."

5. Superlative: "The Cauvery is the longest river in Tamil Nadu."
   Positive: "No other river in Tamil Nadu is as long as the Cauvery."

32.1 THREE DEGREES

Examples:

- early - earlier - earliest
- well - better - best
- badly - worse - worst

UNIT 33: COMMON ERRORS IN DEGREES OF COMPARISON
==================================================

1. Do not use double comparatives.
   Incorrect: "He is more taller than I."
   Correct: "He is taller than I."

2. Do not use double superlatives.
   Incorrect: "She is the most cleverest girl."
   Correct: "She is the cleverest girl."

3. Use "than" after the comparative degree.
   Incorrect: "She is taller from her sister."
   Correct: "She is taller than her sister."

4. Use "the" before the superlative degree.
   Incorrect: "He is best student."
   Correct: "He is the best student."

5. Use "fewer" for countable nouns and "less" for uncountable nouns.
   Correct: "There are fewer students today."
   Correct: "There is less water in the tank."

6. Use "many" for countable nouns and "much" for uncountable nouns.
   Correct: "Many books were sold."
   Correct: "Much money was spent."

7. Do not use "elder" with "than".
   Incorrect: "He is elder than me."
   Correct: "He is elder to me." / "He is older than I am."

8. Use "senior to", "junior to", "superior to", "inferior to" and "preferable to", not "than".
   Correct: "He is senior to me."
   Correct: "This cloth is superior to that."

9. Use "prefer to", not "prefer than".
   Correct: "I prefer tea to coffee."

10. Use "different from", not "different than" in formal English.
    Correct: "This book is different from that one."
