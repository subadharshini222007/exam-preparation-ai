PART A: READING COMPREHENSION
==================================================

==================================================
UNIT 1: INTRODUCTION TO READING COMPREHENSION
==================================================

1.1 MEANING
Reading comprehension means understanding a written passage and answering questions based on it.

A comprehension passage may test:
1. Main idea.
2. Supporting details.
3. Meaning of words and phrases.
4. Inference.
5. Tone and attitude of the writer.
6. Purpose of the passage.
7. Logical conclusion.

1.2 TYPES OF COMPREHENSION QUESTIONS
1. Factual questions - answers are directly stated in the passage.
2. Vocabulary questions - ask the meaning of a word or phrase.
3. Inference questions - answers are not directly stated but can be understood.
4. Main idea questions - ask the central theme of the passage.
5. Title questions - ask the most suitable title.
6. Tone questions - ask whether the writer is critical, supportive, neutral, humorous or sarcastic.
7. Application questions - ask the candidate to apply the idea to a new situation.

==================================================
UNIT 2: STEPS TO ANSWER COMPREHENSION
==================================================

2.1 STEP 1: READ THE QUESTIONS FIRST
Read the questions before reading the passage. This helps to identify what information is required.

2.2 STEP 2: READ THE PASSAGE CAREFULLY
Read the passage once for general understanding. Read difficult parts again.

2.3 STEP 3: IDENTIFY THE MAIN IDEA
The main idea is usually found in:
1. The first sentence.
2. The last sentence.
3. The repeated idea.
4. The sentence that summarises the passage.

2.4 STEP 4: LOCATE KEYWORDS
Find words in the questions and locate the same or similar words in the passage.

2.5 STEP 5: ANSWER FROM THE PASSAGE
Do not answer based only on personal opinion. Use information given in the passage.

2.6 STEP 6: CHECK ALL OPTIONS
Eliminate options that are:
1. Not mentioned in the passage.
2. Opposite to the passage.
3. Too broad or too narrow.
4. Partly correct but incomplete.

==================================================
UNIT 3: MAIN IDEA AND TITLE
==================================================

3.1 MAIN IDEA
The main idea is the central point of the passage.

Example:
Passage:
"Trees give us oxygen, shade, fruits and wood. They prevent soil erosion and provide shelter to birds and animals. Therefore, planting trees is essential for a healthy environment."

Main idea:
"Trees are essential for human beings and the environment."

3.2 SUITABLE TITLE
A good title should be short, clear and cover the whole passage.

Examples:
1. Passage about the benefits of trees:
   Title: "Importance of Trees".

2. Passage about water pollution and its effects:
   Title: "Water Pollution and Its Effects".

3. Passage about the need to save electricity:
   Title: "Need for Energy Conservation".

4. Passage about the role of youth in nation-building:
   Title: "Role of Youth in Nation-Building".

5. Passage about the benefits of reading:
   Title: "Benefits of Reading".

==================================================
UNIT 4: FACTUAL AND INFERENCE QUESTIONS
==================================================

4.1 FACTUAL QUESTIONS
Factual questions have direct answers in the passage.

Example:
Passage:
"Ravi went to Chennai on Monday. He attended an interview and returned on Wednesday."

Question: "When did Ravi go to Chennai?"
Answer: "On Monday."

Question: "Why did he go to Chennai?"
Answer: "To attend an interview."

4.2 INFERENCE QUESTIONS
Inference questions require logical understanding of the passage.

Example:
Passage:
"Ravi reached the station five minutes after the train left. He looked disappointed."

Question: "What can be inferred?"
Answer: "Ravi missed the train."

Example:
Passage:
"The river was polluted, and many fish were found dead near the factory."

Question: "What can be inferred?"
Answer: "The factory may have caused pollution."

==================================================
UNIT 5: VOCABULARY IN COMPREHENSION
==================================================

5.1 FINDING WORD MEANING
The meaning of a difficult word can often be understood from:
1. The words before and after it.
2. Examples given in the passage.
3. The general sense of the sentence.

Example:
"The scientist made a significant discovery, which changed the way people understood the disease."

Here, "significant" means "important".

Example:
"The village was remote; it was far away from the city and difficult to reach."

Here, "remote" means "far away".

5.2 COMMON WORDS IN COMPREHENSION
1. Essential - necessary.
2. Significant - important.
3. Beneficial - helpful.
4. Harmful - damaging.
5. Decline - decrease.
6. Increase - grow.
7. Preserve - protect.
8. Conserve - save.
9. Promote - encourage.
10. Reduce - lessen.

==================================================
UNIT 6: TONE AND PURPOSE
==================================================

6.1 TONE
Tone is the writer's attitude.

Common tones:
1. Informative - gives facts.
2. Critical - finds fault.
3. Supportive - praises or encourages.
4. Neutral - does not take sides.
5. Humorous - funny.
6. Sarcastic - says the opposite of what is meant.
7. Persuasive - tries to convince.
8. Warning - alerts about danger.

Examples:
1. "Plastic waste is destroying our rivers and oceans." - Warning tone.
2. "The government has introduced several useful schemes." - Supportive tone.
3. "Some officials make tall promises but do little work." - Critical tone.
4. "The report presents data on rainfall from 2010 to 2020." - Informative tone.

6.2 PURPOSE
The purpose is the reason for writing the passage.

Common purposes:
1. To inform.
2. To explain.
3. To persuade.
4. To warn.
5. To describe.
6. To entertain.
7. To compare.
8. To analyse.

Example:
"Plant trees, save water and avoid plastic to protect the environment."
Purpose: To persuade readers to protect the environment.

UNIT 7: MODEL COMPREHENSION PASSAGE 1
==================================================

PASSAGE:
Water is essential for all living beings. However, freshwater is limited. Only about three percent of the world's water is freshwater, and most of it is stored in glaciers and ice caps. Due to population growth, urbanisation and climate change, the demand for water is increasing. Many regions face water scarcity. Therefore, rainwater harvesting, recycling of wastewater and efficient irrigation methods are necessary. Every individual should use water carefully and avoid wastage.

QUESTIONS:
1. What is the main idea of the passage?
   Answer: Water is essential but limited, so it must be conserved.

2. What percentage of the world's water is freshwater?
   Answer: About three percent.

3. Where is most freshwater stored?
   Answer: In glaciers and ice caps.

4. What are the causes of increasing water demand?
   Answer: Population growth, urbanisation and climate change.

5. Suggest a suitable title.
   Answer: "Need for Water Conservation".

PASSAGE:
Reading books improves vocabulary, concentration and imagination. It helps students understand different subjects and develop critical thinking. Unlike short videos, books require sustained attention and encourage deep thinking. Reading also reduces stress and improves language skills. Therefore, students should develop the habit of reading newspapers, magazines and books regularly.

   Answer: It improves vocabulary, concentration, imagination, critical thinking and language skills.

2. How is reading different from watching short videos?
   Answer: Reading requires sustained attention and encourages deep thinking.

3. What should students read regularly?
   Answer: Newspapers, magazines and books.

4. What is the tone of the passage?
   Answer: Supportive and persuasive.

5. Suggest a suitable title.
   Answer: "Benefits of Reading".

UNIT 9: PRACTICE QUESTIONS - COMPREHENSION
==================================================

1. What should be done first while answering comprehension?
   Answer: Read the questions and then the passage carefully.

2. What is the main idea?
   Answer: The central point of the passage.

3. What is an inference?
   Answer: A conclusion understood from the passage, though not directly stated.

4. What is tone?
   Answer: The writer's attitude towards the subject.

5. What is the purpose of a passage?
   Answer: The reason for which the passage is written.

6. What type of question has a direct answer in the passage?
   Answer: Factual question.

7. What type of question asks for the central theme?
   Answer: Main idea question.

8. What should a suitable title be?
   Answer: Short, clear and related to the whole passage.

9. What should be avoided while answering comprehension?
   Answer: Personal opinion not supported by the passage.

10. What helps to find the meaning of a difficult word?
    Answer: Context clues in the sentence.

UNIT 10: INTRODUCTION TO TRANSLATION
==================================================

10.1 MEANING
Translation is the process of expressing the meaning of a sentence or passage from one language into another without changing its meaning.

In TNPSC, translation may involve:
1. Tamil to English.
2. English to Tamil.
3. Translation of simple sentences.
4. Translation of proverbs.
5. Translation of official or general expressions.

10.2 BASIC PRINCIPLES
1. Preserve the meaning.
2. Use correct grammar.
3. Use natural sentence structure.
4. Do not translate word by word blindly.
5. Maintain the tone of the original sentence.
6. Use appropriate vocabulary.

==================================================
UNIT 16: INTRODUCTION TO LITERARY WORKS
==================================================

16.1 MEANING
Literary works include poems, short stories, novels, essays, dramas, biographies and autobiographies. In TNPSC General English, questions may be asked about authors, titles, themes, characters and famous lines.

16.2 TYPES OF LITERARY WORKS
1. Poetry - a literary work written in verse.
2. Short story - a brief prose narrative.
3. Novel - a long prose narrative.
4. Drama - a literary work written to be performed.
5. Essay - a short prose composition on a subject.
6. Biography - the life story of a person written by another.
7. Autobiography - the life story of a person written by himself or herself.
8. Travelogue - an account of travel.
9. Memoir - a personal record of experiences.
10. Speech - a formal address delivered to an audience.

UNIT 21: IMPORTANT LITERARY DEVICES
==================================================

21.1 SIMILE
A simile compares two things using "like" or "as".

Examples:
- He is as brave as a lion.
- She sings like a nightingale.
- The child was as quiet as a mouse.
- The road was as smooth as glass.
- He fought like a tiger.

21.2 METAPHOR
A metaphor directly compares two things without using "like" or "as".

Examples:
- He is a lion in battle.
- Time is money.
- The world is a stage.
- She is the light of my life.
- Life is a journey.

21.3 PERSONIFICATION
Personification gives human qualities to non-human things.

Examples:
- The wind whispered through the trees.
- The sun smiled brightly.
- The flowers danced in the breeze.
- The river sang a song.
- Death laid his icy hand on kings.

21.4 ALLITERATION
Alliteration is the repetition of the same initial sound in nearby words.

Examples:
- She sells sea shells.
- Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers.
- The fair breeze blew, the white foam flew.

21.5 HYPERBOLE
Hyperbole is an exaggeration.

Examples:
- I have told you a million times.
- He is as tall as a mountain.
- I am so hungry that I could eat a horse.
- The bag weighed a ton.
- She cried a river of tears.
