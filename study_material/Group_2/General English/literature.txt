Unit 1: Introduction to Literature
Literature refers to written works of artistic value.

Major forms: Poetry, Prose, Drama, Novel.

Major periods: Classical, Medieval, Renaissance, Modern.

Functions: Entertain, educate, inspire.

Genres: Fiction, Non-fiction, Poetry, Drama.

Unit 2: British Authors
William Shakespeare – Plays and sonnets.

Charles Dickens – Novels.

Jane Austen – Novels.

George Orwell – Novels and essays.

William Wordsworth – Poetry.

John Keats – Poetry.

Lord Byron – Poetry.

Percy Bysshe Shelley – Poetry.

Unit 5: Poetry
Poetry: Expresses ideas and emotions in verse.

Forms: Sonnet, Ode, Elegy, Epic, Lyric.

Poetic devices: Rhyme, Rhythm, Meter, Imagery.

Unit 6: Prose
Prose: Ordinary written or spoken language.

Forms: Essay, Short story, Novel, Biography.

Famous prose writers: Bacon, Lamb, Orwell.

Unit 7: Figures of Speech
Simile: Comparison using 'like' or 'as'.

Metaphor: Direct comparison.

Personification: Giving human qualities to non-human things.

Hyperbole: Exaggeration.

Alliteration: Repetition of initial sounds.

Unit 8: Literary Terms
Theme: Central idea.

Plot: Sequence of events.

Character: Person in a story.

Setting: Time and place.

Point of view: Perspective of narration.

Unit 9: Drama
Drama: Story enacted on stage.

Elements: Plot, character, dialogue, setting.

Famous playwrights: Shakespeare, Shaw, Wilde.

Unit 10: Novel
Novel: Long fictional narrative.

Elements: Plot, character, setting, theme.

Famous novelists: Dickens, Austen, Orwell.

Unit 11: Short Story
Short story: Brief fictional narrative.

Elements: Plot, character, setting, theme.

Famous short story writers: Poe, Chekhov, O. Henry.

Unit 12: Essay
Essay: Short piece of writing on a particular subject.

Types: Descriptive, narrative, expository, persuasive.

Famous essayists: Bacon, Lamb, Orwell.

Unit 13: Biography and Autobiography
Biography: Life story written by another.

Autobiography: Life story written by oneself.

Famous examples: Gandhi's autobiography.

Unit 14: Literary Criticism
Literary criticism: Analysis and evaluation of literature.

Approaches: Formalist, Marxist, Feminist, Psychoanalytic.

Famous critics: Aristotle, Coleridge, Eliot.

Unit 15: Literary Movements
Renaissance: Revival of art and learning.

Romanticism: Emotion and nature.

Realism: Everyday life.

Modernism: Break from tradition.

Unit 16: Literary Devices
Metaphor, Simile, Personification, Hyperbole.

Alliteration, Assonance, Onomatopoeia.

Irony, Satire, Parody.

Unit 17: Poetic Forms
Sonnet: 14 lines.

Ode: Praise poem.

Elegy: Lament for the dead.

Epic: Long narrative poem.

Lyric: Personal emotion.

Unit 18: Prose Forms
Essay, Short story, Novel, Biography.

Famous prose writers: Bacon, Lamb, Orwell.

Unit 19: Literary Terms – Advanced
Allegory: Symbolic story.

Allusion: Reference to another work.

Anaphora: Repetition at beginning.

Antithesis: Contrast.

Apostrophe: Direct address.
