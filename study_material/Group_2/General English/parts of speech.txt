
==================================================
UNIT 1: INTRODUCTION TO PARTS OF SPEECH
==================================================

1.1 MEANING OF PARTS OF SPEECH
Parts of speech are the categories into which words are classified according to their function or use in a sentence. The same English word may belong to different parts of speech depending on how it is used in a sentence.

Example:
- "Water" is a noun in "Drink water."
- "Water" is a verb in "Please water the plants."

1.2 THE EIGHT PARTS OF SPEECH
1. Noun - names a person, place, thing, animal or idea.
   Examples: teacher, Chennai, book, dog, honesty.
2. Pronoun - replaces a noun.
   Examples: he, she, it, they, who, someone.
3. Verb - expresses an action, occurrence or state of being.
   Examples: run, write, is, become, seem.
4. Adjective - describes or modifies a noun or pronoun.
   Examples: beautiful, tall, five, Indian, this.
5. Adverb - modifies a verb, adjective, another adverb or a whole sentence.
   Examples: quickly, very, well, often, here.
6. Preposition - shows the relationship between a noun/pronoun and another word.
   Examples: in, on, at, by, with, between.
7. Conjunction - joins words, phrases or clauses.
   Examples: and, but, or, because, although.
8. Interjection - expresses sudden feeling or emotion.
   Examples: Oh!, Alas!, Hurrah!, Bravo!, Wow!

Memory trick:
N - Noun
P - Pronoun
V - Verb
A - Adjective
A - Adverb
P - Preposition
C - Conjunction
I - Interjection

1.3 HOW TO IDENTIFY PARTS OF SPEECH
Do not identify a word only by its spelling. Always see its work in the sentence.

Examples:
- "She gave a quick answer." - "quick" is an adjective because it describes "answer".
- "She answered quickly." - "quickly" is an adverb because it describes "answered".
- "He is a fast runner." - "fast" is an adjective.
- "He runs fast." - "fast" is an adverb.
- "I like this book." - "this" is a demonstrative adjective.
- "This is my book." - "this" is a demonstrative pronoun.

1.4 BASIC SENTENCE PATTERN
A simple English sentence usually has:
Subject + Verb + Object
Example: "The boy reads a book."
- The boy = subject (noun phrase)
- reads = verb
- a book = object (noun phrase)

UNIT 2: NOUN
==================================================

2.1 DEFINITION
A noun is the name of a person, place, thing, animal, quality, action or idea.

Examples:
- Person: Ravi, doctor, teacher, girl
- Place: Chennai, India, school, hospital
- Thing: pen, book, car, mobile
- Animal: dog, lion, elephant
- Idea/quality: honesty, bravery, happiness, freedom
- Action: laughter, movement, decision

2.2 KINDS OF NOUNS
(a) Proper Noun
A proper noun is the special name of a particular person, place, animal or thing. It always begins with a capital letter.
Examples: Tamil Nadu, Chennai, Ravi, India, Monday, Ganga, Taj Mahal.

(b) Common Noun
A common noun is the general name of a person, place, animal or thing.
Examples: boy, city, country, river, school, animal.

Proper noun vs common noun:
- Chennai is a proper noun; city is a common noun.
- India is a proper noun; country is a common noun.
- Ganga is a proper noun; river is a common noun.

(c) Collective Noun
A collective noun is the name of a group of persons, animals or things taken as one unit.
Examples: team, class, army, jury, committee, crowd, flock, herd, bunch, fleet.
Examples in sentences:
- The team has won the match.
- A herd of cattle is grazing.
- The jury has given its decision.

(d) Material Noun
A material noun is the name of a material or substance from which things are made.
Examples: gold, silver, iron, cotton, wood, water, milk, rice, oil.
Example: "This ring is made of gold."

(e) Abstract Noun
An abstract noun is the name of a quality, state, action or idea that cannot be seen or touched.
Examples: honesty, kindness, bravery, childhood, poverty, love, freedom, anger, advice.
Common abstract noun endings:
- -ness: kindness, happiness, darkness
- -ity: ability, cruelty, purity
- -ty: honesty, beauty, safety
- -th: truth, strength, growth
- -ment: movement, agreement, development
- -tion: education, action, decision
- -hood: childhood, brotherhood, motherhood
- -ship: friendship, leadership, citizenship
- -ance/-ence: importance, difference, silence

(f) Concrete Noun
A concrete noun is the name of something that can be seen, touched, heard, smelt or tasted.
Examples: book, tree, dog, water, sugar, music.

2.3 COUNTABLE AND UNCOUNTABLE NOUNS
(a) Countable nouns can be counted. They have singular and plural forms.
Examples: book/books, boy/boys, chair/chairs, idea/ideas.

(b) Uncountable nouns cannot be counted directly. They usually have no plural form and take a singular verb.
Examples: water, milk, sugar, rice, information, advice, furniture, luggage, news, homework, equipment, scenery, machinery, poetry, jewellery, traffic, money.

Important: "Furniture", "luggage", "information", "advice", "news", "homework", "machinery" and "equipment" are uncountable in English.

Correct usage:
- Correct: "She gave me some advice."
- Incorrect: "She gave me some advices."
- Correct: "The news is good."
- Incorrect: "The news are good."
- Correct: "The furniture is new."
- Incorrect: "The furnitures are new."

To show quantity of uncountable nouns, use expressions such as:
- a piece of advice
- a piece of information
- an item of furniture
- a loaf of bread
- a bottle of water
- a kilo of rice

2.4 SINGULAR AND PLURAL NOUNS
General rules:
1. Most nouns add -s.
   book - books, pen - pens, girl - girls.
2. Nouns ending in -s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x, -z add -es.
   bus - buses, class - classes, dish - dishes, watch - watches, box - boxes.
3. Nouns ending in consonant + y change y into i and add -es.
   baby - babies, city - cities, story - stories.
   But vowel + y adds only -s: boy - boys, day - days, key - keys.
4. Some nouns ending in -f or -fe change into -ves.
   leaf - leaves, knife - knives, wife - wives, life - lives, wolf - wolves.
   Exceptions: roof - roofs, chief - chiefs, proof - proofs, belief - beliefs, cliff - cliffs.
5. Nouns ending in -o:
   Add -es in some words: tomato - tomatoes, potato - potatoes, hero - heroes, echo - echoes.
   Add only -s in many words: photo - photos, piano - pianos, radio - radios, video - videos, zoo - zoos.
6. Irregular plurals:
   man - men, woman - women, child - children, foot - feet, tooth - teeth, mouse - mice, goose - geese, ox - oxen, person - people.
7. Some nouns have the same singular and plural form:
   sheep, deer, fish, series, species, aircraft, means.
8. Some nouns are always plural:
   scissors, trousers, spectacles, jeans, shorts, pajamas, belongings, surroundings, thanks, congratulations.
   Example: "My trousers are new."

2.5 GENDER OF NOUNS
1. Masculine gender: man, boy, father, brother, king, lion.
2. Feminine gender: woman, girl, mother, sister, queen, lioness.
3. Common gender: teacher, doctor, student, friend, child, parent.
4. Neuter gender: book, table, stone, car, tree.

Common gender pairs:
- boy - girl
- man - woman
- father - mother
- brother - sister
- son - daughter
- husband - wife
- uncle - aunt
- nephew - niece
- king - queen
- hero - heroine
- lion - lioness
- tiger - tigress
- actor - actress
- waiter - waitress
- prince - princess
- wizard - witch

2.6 CASE OF NOUNS
1. Nominative case: the noun is the subject.
   Example: "Ravi plays cricket."
2. Objective/Accusative case: the noun receives the action.
   Example: "Ravi plays cricket."
3. Possessive case: the noun shows possession.
   Example: "This is Ravi's book."

Possessive case rules:
1. Singular noun: add apostrophe + s.
   Ravi's pen, the girl's bag, India's capital.
2. Plural noun ending in -s: add only apostrophe.
   boys' hostel, teachers' meeting, students' union.
3. Irregular plural not ending in -s: add apostrophe + s.
   children's park, men's room, women's college.
4. For names ending in -s, usually add apostrophe + s.
   James's book, Charles's car.

2.7 IMPORTANT NOUN ERRORS FOR TNPSC
1. "Cattle" takes a plural verb.
   Correct: "The cattle are grazing."
2. "Police" takes a plural verb.
   Correct: "The police have arrested the thief."
3. "People" takes a plural verb.
   Correct: "Many people were present."
4. "Scenery" is uncountable.
   Correct: "The scenery is beautiful."
5. "Hair" is usually uncountable.
   Correct: "Her hair is long."
6. "Bread" is uncountable.
   Correct: "I bought two loaves of bread."
7. "Paper" as material is uncountable; "a paper" means a newspaper or document.
   Correct: "Please give me some paper."
   Correct: "I read a paper yesterday."

UNIT 3: PRONOUN
==================================================

3.1 DEFINITION
A pronoun is a word used in place of a noun to avoid repetition.

Example:
- "Ravi is a doctor. He works in Chennai."
Here "he" replaces "Ravi".

3.2 KINDS OF PRONOUNS
(a) Personal Pronouns
Personal pronouns refer to persons or things.

Subject pronouns: I, we, you, he, she, it, they.
Object pronouns: me, us, you, him, her, it, them.
Possessive adjectives: my, our, your, his, her, its, their.
Possessive pronouns: mine, ours, yours, his, hers, its, theirs.
Reflexive pronouns: myself, ourselves, yourself, himself, herself, itself, themselves.

Examples:
- I am a student.
- She gave me a pen.
- This book is mine.
- He hurt himself.

Important rule:
After prepositions, use object pronouns.
- Correct: "Between you and me."
- Incorrect: "Between you and I."
- Correct: "He gave the book to her."
- Incorrect: "He gave the book to she."

(b) Demonstrative Pronouns
Demonstrative pronouns point out persons or things.
Examples: this, that, these, those.
- This is my pen. (near, singular)
- That is your bag. (far, singular)
- These are my books. (near, plural)
- Those are your books. (far, plural)

(c) Interrogative Pronouns
Interrogative pronouns are used to ask questions.
Examples: who, whom, whose, which, what.
- Who came here?
- Whom did you meet?
- Whose book is this?
- Which colour do you like?
- What is your name?

Who vs whom:
- "Who" is used as subject.
- "Whom" is used as object.
Example:
- Who wrote this letter?
- Whom did you invite?

(d) Relative Pronouns
Relative pronouns connect a clause to a noun and relate it to the noun.
Examples: who, whom, whose, which, that.
- The man who came here is my uncle.
- The book which I bought is interesting.
- The girl whose father is a doctor is my friend.
- The pen that you gave me is lost.

Important uses:
- "Who" is used for persons.
- "Which" is used for animals and things.
- "That" can be used for persons, animals and things.
- "Whose" shows possession and can be used for persons, animals and things.

(e) Indefinite Pronouns
Indefinite pronouns refer to persons or things in a general way.
Examples: someone, somebody, something, anyone, anybody, anything, everyone, everybody, everything, no one, nobody, nothing, one, each, either, neither, all, some, few, many, several, both.

Important rule:
Most indefinite pronouns take a singular verb.
- Everyone is present.
- Somebody has taken my pen.
- Each of the students has submitted the form.
- Neither of the answers is correct.
- Either of the two roads leads to the station.

But "both", "few", "many" and "several" take a plural verb.
- Both of them are intelligent.
- Many were injured in the accident.
- Few students were absent.

(f) Reflexive Pronouns
Reflexive pronouns are used when the subject and object are the same person or thing.
Examples: myself, ourselves, yourself, himself, herself, itself, themselves.
- I taught myself English.
- She introduced herself.
- They enjoyed themselves.

Important: Do not use reflexive pronouns unnecessarily after verbs such as "enjoy", "introduce", "avail", "absent" when the subject is already clear in ordinary usage.
- Correct: "We enjoyed the function."
- Also correct: "We enjoyed ourselves at the party."

(g) Emphatic Pronouns
Emphatic pronouns emphasise a noun or pronoun. They have the same form as reflexive pronouns.
- I myself saw the accident.
- She herself cooked the food.
- The Prime Minister himself attended the meeting.

Difference:
- Reflexive: "He hurt himself." - "himself" is the object.
- Emphatic: "He himself did the work." - "himself" only emphasises "he".

(h) Distributive Pronouns
Distributive pronouns refer to persons or things one at a time.
Examples: each, either, neither.
- Each of the boys was given a prize.
- Either of you can go.
- Neither of them is guilty.

(i) Reciprocal Pronouns
Reciprocal pronouns show a mutual relationship.
Examples: each other, one another.
- The two friends helped each other.
- The members of the team supported one another.

Traditional rule:
- "Each other" is used for two persons or things.
- "One another" is used for more than two.
Example:
- The two sisters love each other.
- All the students helped one another.

3.3 IMPORTANT PRONOUN ERRORS
1. "One" should be followed by "one's", not "his" or "her", in formal English.
   Correct: "One should do one's duty."
2. "Everyone", "everybody", "someone", "somebody", "anyone", "anybody", "nobody" and "no one" take singular verbs.
   Correct: "Everybody was happy."
3. "Who" is subject; "whom" is object.
   Correct: "Who is calling me?"
   Correct: "To whom did you speak?"
4. Use "its" for possession; "it's" means "it is".
   Correct: "The dog wagged its tail."
   Correct: "It's raining."
5. Do not use both a noun and a pronoun as the same subject.
   Incorrect: "Ravi he came here."
   Correct: "Ravi came here."

UNIT 4: VERB
==================================================

4.1 DEFINITION
A verb is a word that expresses an action, an occurrence or a state of being.

Examples:
- Action: run, write, eat, play, read.
- State: is, am, are, was, were, seem, appear, become.
- Occurrence: happen, occur, die, grow.

Examples in sentences:
- Birds fly in the sky.
- She is a teacher.
- The meeting will happen tomorrow.
- He became a doctor.

4.2 KINDS OF VERBS
(a) Main Verbs / Principal Verbs
Main verbs express the main action or state.
Examples: write, eat, go, sing, read.
- She writes a letter.
- They play football.

(b) Helping Verbs / Auxiliary Verbs
Helping verbs help the main verb to form tenses, questions, negatives and passive voice.
Examples: be, am, is, are, was, were, have, has, had, do, does, did, will, would, shall, should, can, could, may, might, must.

Examples:
- She is writing a letter.
- They have finished the work.
- Do you know him?
- He can swim.

(c) Transitive Verbs
A transitive verb needs an object to complete its meaning.
Examples:
- She wrote a letter.
- He bought a car.
- They built a house.

(d) Intransitive Verbs
An intransitive verb does not need an object.
Examples:
- Birds fly.
- The baby slept.
- He laughed loudly.
- The sun rises in the east.

Some verbs can be both transitive and intransitive:
- "She sings a song." - transitive.
- "She sings well." - intransitive.

(e) Linking Verbs
Linking verbs connect the subject with a word that describes or identifies it.
Common linking verbs: is, am, are, was, were, become, seem, appear, look, feel, taste, smell, sound.
Examples:
- She is intelligent.
- The milk smells bad.
- He became angry.
- The flower looks beautiful.

After linking verbs, use an adjective, not an adverb.
- Correct: "She looks beautiful."
- Incorrect: "She looks beautifully."
- Correct: "The food tastes good."
- Incorrect: "The food tastes well."

(f) Regular and Irregular Verbs
Regular verbs form their past tense and past participle by adding -ed or -d.
Examples:
- work - worked - worked
- play - played - played
- walk - walked - walked

Irregular verbs change their form irregularly.
Examples:
- go - went - gone
- eat - ate - eaten
- see - saw - seen
- write - wrote - written
- drink - drank - drunk
- sing - sang - sung
- take - took - taken
- give - gave - given
- buy - bought - bought
- bring - brought - brought
- teach - taught - taught
- catch - caught - caught
- begin - began - begun
- break - broke - broken
- choose - chose - chosen
- speak - spoke - spoken

4.3 FINITE AND NON-FINITE VERBS
(a) Finite Verb
A finite verb changes according to the subject, number, tense or person.
Examples:
- He writes a letter.
- They write letters.
- She wrote a letter.

(b) Non-finite Verbs
Non-finite verbs do not change according to the subject or tense.
There are three types:

1. Infinitive: to + base verb.
   Examples: to read, to write, to go.
   - He wants to learn English.
   - To err is human.

2. Gerund: verb + ing used as a noun.
   Examples: reading, writing, swimming.
   - Reading is a good habit.
   - Swimming is good for health.
   - I enjoy reading novels.

3. Participle: present participle (-ing) or past participle (-ed/-en form).
   - The crying child needs help.
   - The broken window must be repaired.
   - Seeing the police, the thief ran away.

4.4 MODAL AUXILIARY VERBS
Modals express ability, possibility, permission, obligation, advice, request and necessity.
Common modals: can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to, need, dare.

1. Can - ability or permission.
   - She can speak Tamil.
   - Can I use your phone?

2. Could - past ability, polite request or possibility.
   - When he was young, he could run fast.
   - Could you help me?

3. May - permission or possibility.
   - May I come in?
   - It may rain today.

4. Might - slight possibility.
   - He might come late.

5. Must - strong necessity or obligation.
   - You must obey the law.
   - We must wear helmets.

6. Should - advice, duty or expectation.
   - You should study regularly.
   - Students should respect teachers.

7. Will - future action, promise or determination.
   - I will help you.

8. Would - polite request, past habit or imagined situation.
   - Would you please open the window?
   - He would go for a walk every morning.

9. Ought to - moral duty.
   - We ought to help the poor.

Important modal rules:
- After a modal verb, use the base form of the verb.
  Correct: "He can swim."
  Incorrect: "He can swims."
  Correct: "You must go."
  Incorrect: "You must to go."
- "Must" has no past form; use "had to".
  Correct: "Yesterday, I had to go to Chennai."

4.5 SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT
The verb must agree with its subject in number and person.

Important rules:
1. Singular subject takes a singular verb.
   - The boy plays cricket.
2. Plural subject takes a plural verb.
   - The boys play cricket.
3. Two subjects joined by "and" usually take a plural verb.
   - Ravi and Kumar are friends.
4. If two nouns joined by "and" refer to one person or one idea, use a singular verb.
   - Bread and butter is my favourite breakfast.
   - The poet and philosopher has come. (one person)
5. When "each", "every", "either", "neither", "everyone", "everybody", "someone", "somebody", "anyone", "anybody", "no one" and "nobody" is the subject, use a singular verb.
   - Each student has a book.
   - Every man and woman was present.
   - Neither of the boys was present.
6. When two subjects are joined by "or", "nor", "either...or" or "neither...nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject.
   - Either Ravi or his friends have done it.
   - Neither the teacher nor the students were present.
7. Words such as "along with", "as well as", "together with", "in addition to" do not change the number of the subject.
   - The teacher, as well as the students, was present.
   - Ravi, along with his friends, has gone to Chennai.
8. Collective nouns usually take a singular verb when the group acts as one unit.
   - The committee has decided.
   - The team is playing well.
9. "A number of" takes a plural verb; "the number of" takes a singular verb.
   - A number of students were absent.
   - The number of students is small.
10. "One of the + plural noun" takes a singular verb.
   - One of the boys is absent.
   - One of my friends has gone abroad.
11. Distances, amounts of money, time periods and weights are usually treated as singular units.
   - Ten kilometres is a long distance.
   - Five hundred rupees is enough.
   - Two hours is sufficient time.

UNIT 5: ADJECTIVE
==================================================

5.1 DEFINITION
An adjective is a word that describes, qualifies or modifies a noun or pronoun. It gives more information about its quality, quantity, number, size, colour, shape or origin.

Examples:
- a beautiful flower
- an intelligent boy
- five books
- Indian culture
- this pen
- whose bag

5.2 KINDS OF ADJECTIVES
(a) Adjective of Quality / Descriptive Adjective
It describes the quality of a noun or pronoun.
Examples: good, bad, big, small, tall, short, brave, kind, beautiful, intelligent.
- She is a kind woman.
- Chennai is a big city.

(b) Adjective of Quantity
It shows how much of a thing there is. It is usually used with uncountable nouns.
Examples: some, much, little, enough, all, no, any.
- I have some money.
- There is little water in the bottle.
- He has enough patience.

(c) Adjective of Number / Numeral Adjective
It shows how many persons or things there are. It is used with countable nouns.
Examples: one, two, three, first, second, many, few, several, all, some, any, no.
- I have three pens.
- Many students were present.
- This is my first visit to Delhi.

Types:
1. Definite numeral adjectives: one, two, first, second.
2. Indefinite numeral adjectives: some, many, few, all, several.
3. Distributive numeral adjectives: each, every, either, neither.

(d) Demonstrative Adjective
It points out which person or thing is meant.
Examples: this, that, these, those.
- This book is interesting.
- Those boys are playing.

(e) Interrogative Adjective
It is used with a noun to ask a question.
Examples: which, what, whose.
- Which book do you want?
- What colour do you like?
- Whose bag is this?

(f) Possessive Adjective
It shows possession.
Examples: my, our, your, his, her, its, their.
- This is my book.
- Her dress is new.
- Their house is big.

(g) Proper Adjective
A proper adjective is formed from a proper noun.
Examples: Indian, Tamil, Chinese, European, Shakespearean.
- Indian culture is ancient.
- He speaks Tamil.

(h) Emphasising Adjective
It emphasises a noun.
Examples: own, very.
- I saw it with my own eyes.
- He is my very best friend.

(i) Exclamatory Adjective
It expresses strong feeling and is used with a noun.
Example: what.
- What a beautiful sight!
- What an idea!

5.3 DEGREES OF COMPARISON
Adjectives have three degrees:
1. Positive degree - describes one person or thing.
   Example: Ram is tall.
2. Comparative degree - compares two persons or things.
   Example: Ram is taller than Kumar.
3. Superlative degree - compares one person or thing with many others.
   Example: Ram is the tallest boy in the class.

Formation of comparative and superlative:
1. One-syllable adjectives: add -er/-est.
   tall - taller - tallest
   small - smaller - smallest
   old - older - oldest

2. Adjectives ending in -e: add -r/-st.
   large - larger - largest
   brave - braver - bravest
   wise - wiser - wisest

3. Adjectives ending in consonant + y: change y into i and add -er/-est.
   happy - happier - happiest
   easy - easier - easiest
   heavy - heavier - heaviest

4. One-syllable adjectives ending in one vowel + one consonant: double the final consonant.
   big - bigger - biggest
   hot - hotter - hottest
   thin - thinner - thinnest

5. Adjectives of three or more syllables: use "more" and "most".
   beautiful - more beautiful - most beautiful
   intelligent - more intelligent - most intelligent
   important - more important - most important

6. Some adjectives of two syllables can take either form:
   clever - cleverer/more clever - cleverest/most clever
   simple - simpler/more simple - simplest/most simple

Irregular comparison:
- good - better - best
- bad - worse - worst
- little - less - least
- much/many - more - most
- far - farther/further - farthest/furthest
- old - older/elder - oldest/eldest
- late - later/latter - latest/last

Important differences:
- "Elder" and "eldest" are used only for members of the same family.
  Correct: "My elder brother is a doctor."
- "Older" and "oldest" can be used for persons and things.
  Correct: "This is the oldest temple in the town."
- "Farther" refers to physical distance; "further" means additional.
  Correct: "Chennai is farther than Coimbatore."
  Correct: "For further information, contact the office."
- "Later" refers to time; "latter" refers to the second of two things already mentioned.
  Correct: "I will meet you later."
  Correct: "Of tea and coffee, I prefer the latter."

5.4 POSITION OF ADJECTIVES
1. Usually an adjective comes before the noun.
   - a red car
   - an honest officer
2. After linking verbs, the adjective comes after the verb.
   - The sky is blue.
   - She is happy.
3. Some adjectives are used only after the verb:
   afraid, asleep, alive, alone, ashamed, awake, well.
   Correct: "The child is asleep."
   Incorrect: "The asleep child..."

5.5 ORDER OF ADJECTIVES
When more than one adjective is used before a noun, the usual order is:
Opinion + Size + Age + Shape + Colour + Origin + Material + Purpose + Noun.

Example:
- a beautiful small old round brown Indian wooden dining table.

Common examples:
- a big black bag
- a beautiful old Tamil temple
- a small red plastic box

5.6 IMPORTANT ADJECTIVE ERRORS
1. Use "fewer" for countable nouns and "less" for uncountable nouns.
   Correct: "There are fewer students today."
   Correct: "There is less water in the tank."
2. Use "many" for countable nouns and "much" for uncountable nouns.
   Correct: "Many books were sold."
   Correct: "Much money was spent."
3. Use "elder" for family members; do not use "than" with "elder".
   Correct: "He is my elder brother."
   Correct: "He is older than I am."
4. Comparative degree is followed by "than".
   Correct: "She is taller than her sister."
5. Superlative degree is usually preceded by "the".
   Correct: "He is the best student."
6. Do not use double comparatives or double superlatives.
   Incorrect: "more better"
   Correct: "better"
   Incorrect: "most best"
   Correct: "best"

==================================================
UNIT 6: ADVERB
==================================================

6.1 DEFINITION
An adverb is a word that modifies a verb, an adjective, another adverb, a phrase or a whole sentence. It tells how, when, where, how often, how much or to what degree something happens.

Examples:
- He runs quickly. (modifies verb)
- She is very intelligent. (modifies adjective)
- He speaks too slowly. (modifies adverb)
- Unfortunately, he failed. (modifies whole sentence)

6.2 KINDS OF ADVERBS
(a) Adverb of Manner
It tells how an action is done.
Examples: quickly, slowly, carefully, bravely, well, badly, happily.
- She sings beautifully.
- He walked slowly.

(b) Adverb of Time
It tells when an action happens.
Examples: now, then, today, tomorrow, yesterday, soon, early, late, already, still.
- I will come tomorrow.
- He has already finished the work.

(c) Adverb of Place
It tells where an action happens.
Examples: here, there, everywhere, outside, inside, above, below, near, far.
- Come here.
- The children are playing outside.

(d) Adverb of Frequency
It tells how often an action happens.
Examples: always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, seldom, never, daily, weekly.
- He always speaks the truth.
- I rarely watch television.

(e) Adverb of Degree / Quantity
It tells to what degree or extent something happens.
Examples: very, quite, too, enough, almost, nearly, rather, extremely, completely.
- She is very intelligent.
- The water is too hot.
- He is quite ready.

(f) Adverb of Reason / Cause
It tells why an action happens.
Examples: therefore, hence, consequently, because.
- He was ill; therefore, he did not come.
- Hence, the result was delayed.

(g) Interrogative Adverb
It is used to ask questions.
Examples: how, when, where, why.
- How did you come?
- When will you return?
- Where do you live?
- Why are you late?

(h) Relative Adverb
It introduces a relative clause.
Examples: when, where, why.
- I remember the day when we met.
- This is the place where he was born.
- Tell me the reason why you came late.

(i) Sentence Adverb
It modifies the whole sentence and often expresses the speaker's attitude.
Examples: fortunately, unfortunately, clearly, certainly, probably, perhaps.
- Fortunately, no one was injured.
- Probably, he will come tomorrow.

6.3 FORMATION OF ADVERBS
1. Many adverbs are formed by adding -ly to adjectives.
   quick - quickly
   careful - carefully
   brave - bravely
   sad - sadly

2. Adjectives ending in -y change y into i before -ly.
   happy - happily
   easy - easily
   angry - angrily

3. Adjectives ending in -le drop e and add -y.
   simple - simply
   gentle - gently
   possible - possibly

4. Adjectives ending in -ll add only -y.
   full - fully
   dull - dully

5. Some adverbs have the same form as adjectives:
   fast, hard, late, early, near, high, low, deep, straight, enough.
   - He is a fast runner. (adjective)
   - He runs fast. (adverb)
   - It is hard work. (adjective)
   - He works hard. (adverb)

6. Some adverbs have two forms with different meanings:
   hard - hardly
   late - lately
   near - nearly
   high - highly
   free - freely
   hard = with effort; hardly = almost not.
   - He works hard.
   - He hardly works.
   late = after the usual time; lately = recently.
   - He came late.
   - Have you seen him lately?

6.4 COMPARISON OF ADVERBS
Like adjectives, adverbs also have three degrees.

Positive - Comparative - Superlative
- fast - faster - fastest
- hard - harder - hardest
- early - earlier - earliest
- well - better - best
- badly - worse - worst
- little - less - least
- much - more - most

Examples:
- Ravi runs fast.
- Kumar runs faster than Ravi.
- Anil runs the fastest of all.

Adverbs ending in -ly generally use "more" and "most".
- quickly - more quickly - most quickly
- carefully - more carefully - most carefully
- beautifully - more beautifully - most beautifully

6.5 POSITION OF ADVERBS
1. Adverbs of frequency usually come before the main verb but after the verb "be".
   - He always comes late.
   - She is always punctual.
2. Adverbs of manner usually come after the verb or object.
   - He speaks English fluently.
   - She completed the work carefully.
3. Adverbs of time usually come at the end of a sentence.
   - I met him yesterday.
4. Adverbs of place usually come after the verb or object.
   - The children are playing outside.
5. "Only" should be placed close to the word it modifies.
   - Only Ravi came. (no one else came)
   - Ravi only came. (he did not do anything else)
   - Ravi came only yesterday. (not earlier)

6.6 IMPORTANT ADVERB ERRORS
1. Do not use two negative words in the same clause.
   Incorrect: "I don't know nothing."
   Correct: "I don't know anything."
   Correct: "I know nothing."
2. "Hardly" and "scarcely" are already negative in meaning.
   Incorrect: "I can't hardly understand."
   Correct: "I can hardly understand."
3. Use "very" with positive adjectives/adverbs and "much" with comparative forms.
   Correct: "She is very intelligent."
   Correct: "She is much better today."
4. "Too" means "more than necessary" and usually gives a negative meaning.
   - The tea is too hot to drink.
5. "Enough" is placed after an adjective or adverb.
   Correct: "He is strong enough."
   Incorrect: "He is enough strong."

UNIT 7: PREPOSITION
==================================================

7.1 DEFINITION
A preposition is a word placed before a noun or pronoun to show its relationship with another word in the sentence. It may show place, time, direction, cause, manner, possession or purpose.

Examples:
- The book is on the table.
- He came at 5 p.m.
- She is good at English.
- The gift is for you.

7.2 KINDS OF PREPOSITIONS
(a) Simple Prepositions
Examples: at, by, for, from, in, of, off, on, out, through, till, to, up, with.
- He lives in Chennai.
- She came by bus.

(b) Compound Prepositions
Examples: according to, because of, in front of, instead of, in spite of, on account of, with reference to.
- He was absent because of illness.
- In spite of rain, we went out.

(c) Phrase Prepositions
Examples: in order to, as well as, along with, away from, by means of, with a view to.
- He works hard in order to succeed.
- She came along with her mother.

(d) Participial Prepositions
Examples: concerning, considering, regarding, during, pending.
- Regarding your request, we shall reply soon.
- During the holidays, I visited my village.

7.3 PREPOSITIONS OF TIME
1. "At" is used for exact time, festivals and points of time.
   - at 6 o'clock, at noon, at night, at midnight, at dawn, at Christmas, at Pongal.

2. "On" is used for days, dates and particular days.
   - on Monday, on 15 August, on Sunday morning, on my birthday.

3. "In" is used for months, years, seasons, centuries and parts of the day.
   - in April, in 2026, in summer, in the morning, in the twentieth century.

Exception:
- "at night" but "in the morning", "in the afternoon", "in the evening".

4. "Since" is used with a point of time and usually with perfect tenses.
   - I have lived here since 2010.
   - He has been ill since Monday.

5. "For" is used with a period of time.
   - I have lived here for ten years.
   - He waited for two hours.

6. "From" is used with a starting point of time.
   - The class starts from Monday.
   - He worked from morning to evening.

7.4 PREPOSITIONS OF PLACE
1. "At" is used for a specific point or small place.
   - at the bus stop, at the door, at 10 Anna Salai.

2. "In" is used inside an enclosed space or a large area.
   - in the room, in Chennai, in India, in the box.

3. "On" is used for a surface.
   - on the table, on the wall, on the floor.

4. "Between" is used for two persons or things.
   - The bank is between the school and the hospital.

5. "Among" is used for more than two persons or things.
   - The sweets were distributed among the children.

6. "Above" means higher than, without touching.
   - The fan is above the table.

7. "Over" means directly above or covering.
   - The bridge is over the river.
   - He put a blanket over the child.

8. "Below" means lower than.
   - The temperature is below zero.

9. "Under" means directly below.
   - The cat is under the table.

10. "Beside" means by the side of; "besides" means in addition to.
   - He sat beside me.
   - Besides English, she knows French.

7.5 PREPOSITIONS OF DIRECTION AND MOVEMENT
1. "To" shows movement towards a place.
   - He went to Chennai.
2. "Into" shows movement from outside to inside.
   - He walked into the room.
3. "Onto" shows movement onto a surface.
   - The cat jumped onto the table.
4. "Towards" shows direction.
   - He walked towards the station.
5. "Through" means from one side to the other.
   - The train passed through the tunnel.
6. "Across" means from one side to the other of a surface or area.
   - He swam across the river.
7. "Along" means following a line or path.
   - We walked along the beach.
8. "Around" means on all sides or in a circular movement.
   - They sat around the fire.

7.6 IMPORTANT PREPOSITION COMBINATIONS
1. Good at: "She is good at mathematics."
2. Angry with a person: "He is angry with me."
3. Angry at a thing: "He is angry at the delay."
4. Married to: "She is married to a doctor."
5. Consist of: "Water consists of hydrogen and oxygen."
6. Depend on: "Success depends on hard work."
7. Insist on: "He insisted on going."
8. Interested in: "I am interested in music."
9. Fond of: "He is fond of sweets."
10. Afraid of: "She is afraid of dogs."
11. Proud of: "We are proud of our country."
12. Tired of: "I am tired of waiting."
13. Suffer from: "He suffers from fever."
14. Recover from: "He has recovered from illness."
15. Complain about: "He complained about the noise."
16. Agree with a person: "I agree with you."
17. Agree to a proposal: "He agreed to the plan."
18. Listen to: "Listen to me carefully."
19. Wait for: "Please wait for me."
20. Believe in: "I believe in hard work."
21. Compare with: used to show similarities or differences between things of the same class.
   - Compare your answer with the model answer.
22. Compare to: used to show resemblance between things of different classes.
   - Life is compared to a journey.
23. Differ from: "My opinion differs from yours."
24. Prefer to: "I prefer tea to coffee."
25. Superior to / inferior to: "This cloth is superior to that."
26. Senior to / junior to: "He is senior to me."
27. Preferable to: "Health is preferable to wealth."
28. Different from: "This book is different from that one."
29. Deal in: to trade in goods. "He deals in textiles."
30. Deal with: to handle a person, problem or subject. "This book deals with Indian history."

7.7 IMPORTANT PREPOSITION ERRORS
1. Do not use "to" after "home", "abroad", "here" and "there" with verbs of motion.
   Correct: "He went home."
   Incorrect: "He went to home."
2. "Discuss" does not take "about".
   Correct: "We discussed the problem."
   Incorrect: "We discussed about the problem."
3. "Enter" does not take "into" when referring to a room, building or hall.
   Correct: "He entered the room."
4. "Reach" does not take "to".
   Correct: "He reached Chennai."
   Incorrect: "He reached to Chennai."
5. "Order" does not take "for".
   Correct: "The judge ordered him to pay the fine."
6. "Attack" does not take "on".
   Correct: "The army attacked the enemy."
7. "Married" is followed by "to", not "with".
   Correct: "She is married to Ravi."
8. "Comprises" does not take "of".
   Correct: "The team comprises ten players."
   Correct: "The team consists of ten players."

UNIT 8: CONJUNCTION
==================================================

8.1 DEFINITION
A conjunction is a word that joins words, phrases, clauses or sentences.

Examples:
- Ravi and Kumar are friends. (joins words)
- She is poor but honest. (joins adjectives)
- I came because it was raining. (joins clauses)
- He worked hard, so he passed. (joins sentences)

8.2 KINDS OF CONJUNCTIONS
(a) Coordinating Conjunctions
Coordinating conjunctions join words, phrases or clauses of equal grammatical rank.
The four main types are:

1. Cumulative or Copulative - adds one statement to another.
   Examples: and, also, both...and, not only...but also.
   - He is intelligent and hardworking.
   - Not only Ravi but also Kumar came.

2. Adversative - expresses contrast.
   Examples: but, yet, still, however, whereas.
   - He is poor but honest.
   - She was ill, yet she attended the class.

3. Alternative - expresses choice.
   Examples: or, either...or, neither...nor, otherwise, else.
   - You can take tea or coffee.
   - Either you apologise or leave the room.
   - Neither Ravi nor Kumar was present.

4. Illative - expresses inference or result.
   Examples: for, so, therefore, hence, consequently.
   - He was hungry, so he ate food.
   - It was raining; therefore, the match was cancelled.

(b) Subordinating Conjunctions
Subordinating conjunctions join a subordinate clause to a main clause.

1. Time: when, while, before, after, until, till, since, as soon as.
   - Wait until I return.
   - As soon as he came, we started.

2. Cause or reason: because, since, as.
   - He stayed at home because he was ill.

3. Purpose: so that, in order that, lest.
   - Work hard so that you may pass.
   - Walk carefully lest you should fall.

4. Result or consequence: so...that, such...that.
   - He was so tired that he could not walk.
   - It was such a good film that everyone liked it.

5. Condition: if, unless, provided, in case.
   - If you work hard, you will pass.
   - Unless you apologise, he will not forgive you.

6. Concession or contrast: though, although, even though, while.
   - Though he is poor, he is honest.
   - Although it rained, we went out.

7. Comparison: than, as...as, so...as.
   - She is taller than her sister.
   - He is as brave as a lion.

8. Manner: as, as if, as though.
   - Do as I say.
   - He behaves as if he were a king.

(c) Correlative Conjunctions
Correlative conjunctions are used in pairs.
Examples:
- both...and: "Both Ravi and Kumar came."
- either...or: "Either he or she must go."
- neither...nor: "Neither he nor she came."
- not only...but also: "He is not only intelligent but also hardworking."
- whether...or: "I do not know whether he will come or not."
- no sooner...than: "No sooner had he arrived than it started raining."
- hardly...when: "Hardly had he left when the phone rang."
- scarcely...when: "Scarcely had she entered when the bell rang."
- such...that: "It was such a difficult question that nobody could answer it."
- so...that: "He was so weak that he could not stand."

Important rule:
- "No sooner" is followed by "than", not "when".
  Correct: "No sooner had he come than the rain started."
- "Hardly" and "scarcely" are followed by "when", not "than".
  Correct: "Hardly had he come when the rain started."
- "Lest" is followed by "should".
  Correct: "Work hard lest you should fail."

8.3 IMPORTANT CONJUNCTION ERRORS
1. Do not use "though" and "but" together.
   Incorrect: "Though he is poor, but he is honest."
   Correct: "Though he is poor, he is honest."
2. Do not use "because" and "so" together.
   Incorrect: "Because he was ill, so he did not come."
   Correct: "Because he was ill, he did not come."
3. Do not use "both" with "as well as".
   Incorrect: "Both Ravi as well as Kumar came."
   Correct: "Both Ravi and Kumar came."
4. "Neither" is followed by "nor", not "or".
   Correct: "Neither Ravi nor Kumar came."
5. "Either" is followed by "or".
   Correct: "Either Ravi or Kumar will come."
6. "Not only" is followed by "but also".
   Correct: "He is not only rich but also generous."

UNIT 9: INTERJECTION
==================================================

9.1 DEFINITION
An interjection is a word that expresses a sudden feeling or emotion such as joy, sorrow, surprise, pain, approval or disgust. It is usually followed by an exclamation mark.

Examples:
- Hurrah! We won the match.
- Alas! He failed.
- Oh! What a beautiful sight!
- Bravo! Well done.
- Wow! That is amazing.
- Hush! The baby is sleeping.

9.2 INTERJECTIONS AND THEIR FEELINGS
1. Joy: Hurrah!, Wow!, Yippee!
2. Sorrow: Alas!, Oh!, Ah!
3. Surprise: Oh!, What!, Hey!, Wow!
4. Pain: Oh!, Ouch!, Ah!
5. Approval: Bravo!, Well done!
6. Attention: Listen!, Hark!, Look!
7. Silence: Hush!, Silence!
8. Disgust: Fie!, Ugh!, Pooh!
9. Greeting: Hello!, Hi!
10. Calling attention: Hey!, Hello!

Examples:
- Hurrah! We have won the trophy.
- Alas! The old man lost his son.
- Bravo! You have played very well.
- Ouch! That hurt.
- Hush! Don't make noise.
- Fie! How can you tell a lie?

9.3 PUNCTUATION WITH INTERJECTIONS
A strong emotion is followed by an exclamation mark.
- Wow! What a match!

A mild emotion may be followed by a comma.
- Oh, I see.

UNIT 10: IDENTIFYING PARTS OF SPEECH IN SENTENCES

10.1 SAME WORD IN DIFFERENT PARTS OF SPEECH
1. Book
- Noun: "This is a good book."
- Verb: "Please book two tickets."

2. Water
- Noun: "Drink some water."
- Verb: "Water the plants."

3. Work
- Noun: "I have a lot of work."
- Verb: "I work in a bank."

4. Well
- Noun: "Draw water from the well."
- Adjective: "She is well now."
- Adverb: "She sings well."
- Interjection: "Well! Let us begin."

5. Round
- Adjective: "The earth is round."
- Noun: "They played a round of golf."
- Adverb: "He turned round."
- Preposition: "The earth moves round the sun."
- Verb: "The ship rounded the cape."

6. But
- Conjunction: "He is poor but honest."
- Preposition: "All but Ravi were present."
- Adverb: "He is but a child."

7. Since
- Preposition: "I have known him since 2010."
- Conjunction: "Since he was ill, he stayed at home."
- Adverb: "He left in 2010 and has not returned since."

8. After
- Preposition: "He came after lunch."
- Conjunction: "After he finished his work, he went home."
- Adverb: "He came soon after."

9. Before
- Preposition: "Wash your hands before dinner."
- Conjunction: "Before you go, meet me."
- Adverb: "I have seen this film before."

10. Down
- Adverb: "He sat down."
- Preposition: "He fell down the stairs."
- Adjective: "The down train is late."
- Noun: "He has had a down in business."

10.2 HOW TO FIND THE PART OF SPEECH
Step 1: Find the main verb.
Step 2: Find the subject and object.
Step 3: See what work the given word does.
Step 4: Check whether it names something, replaces something, shows action, describes something, shows relationship, joins something or expresses emotion.

Example:
"Ravi quickly wrote a beautiful letter to his friend."

- Ravi = noun
- quickly = adverb
- wrote = verb
- a = article/determiner
- beautiful = adjective
- letter = noun
- to = preposition
- his = possessive adjective
- friend = noun

UNIT 11: ARTICLES AND DETERMINERS
==================================================

11.1 ARTICLES
Articles are words used before nouns. "A", "an" and "the" are called articles. They are often treated as a special type of adjective or determiner.

1. Indefinite Articles: a, an.
   - "A" is used before a word beginning with a consonant sound.
     Examples: a book, a university, a European, a one-rupee coin.
   - "An" is used before a word beginning with a vowel sound.
     Examples: an apple, an hour, an honest man, an MLA, an umbrella.

Important:
Use the sound, not the spelling.
- "a university" because "university" begins with the /yu/ sound.
- "an hour" because "h" is silent.
- "a European" because "European" begins with the /yu/ sound.
- "an X-ray" because "X" begins with the /e/ sound.

2. Definite Article: the.
"The" is used:
- Before a particular person or thing: "The sun rises in the east."
- Before unique things: the sun, the moon, the earth, the sky.
- Before superlatives: the best, the tallest, the most important.
- Before rivers, seas, oceans, mountain ranges and groups of islands: the Cauvery, the Indian Ocean, the Himalayas, the Andamans.
- Before the names of holy books, newspapers and famous buildings: the Gita, the Hindu, the Taj Mahal.
- Before musical instruments: "She plays the violin."
- Before an adjective used as a plural noun: "The rich should help the poor."

"The" is not generally used:
- Before proper nouns: Chennai, Ravi, India.
- Before meals: breakfast, lunch, dinner.
- Before games: cricket, football, chess.
- Before languages: Tamil, English, Hindi.
- Before abstract nouns used in a general sense: honesty, beauty, freedom.

UNIT 12: COMMON CONFUSING WORDS
==================================================

12.1 ADJECTIVE VS ADVERB
1. Good - adjective; Well - adverb.
   - He is a good singer.
   - He sings well.
2. Hard - adjective/adverb; Hardly - adverb meaning "almost not".
   - It is hard work.
   - He works hard.
   - I hardly know him.
3. Late - adjective/adverb; Lately - adverb meaning "recently".
   - He came late.
   - I have not seen him lately.
4. High - adjective/adverb; Highly - adverb meaning "very much".
   - The bird flew high.
   - He is highly qualified.
5. Near - adjective/adverb/preposition; Nearly - adverb meaning "almost".
   - The school is near my house.
   - It is nearly six o'clock.
6. Free - adjective/adverb; Freely - adverb meaning "without restriction".
   - The entry is free.
   - You may speak freely.

12.2 COMMONLY CONFUSED PAIRS
1. Advice - noun; Advise - verb.
   - He gave me good advice.
   - Please advise me.
2. Practice - noun; Practise - verb.
   - Practice makes perfect.
   - We practise English every day.
3. Effect - noun; Affect - verb.
   - The medicine had a good effect.
   - Smoking affects health.
4. Lose - verb; Loose - adjective.
   - Do not lose your pen.
   - This knot is loose.
5. Principal - chief/head; Principle - rule or belief.
   - The principal spoke to the students.
   - He is a man of principle.
6. Stationary - not moving; Stationery - writing materials.
   - The car was stationary.
   - Buy stationery from that shop.
7. Complement - something that completes; Compliment - praise.
   - The scarf complements her dress.
   - He complimented her on her success.
8. Desert - dry land; Dessert - sweet dish.
   - The Sahara is a desert.
   - We had ice cream for dessert.
9. Quite - fairly; Quiet - silent.
   - The film was quite good.
   - Please keep quiet.
10. Weather - climate condition; Whether - expressing choice.
    - The weather is pleasant.
    - I do not know whether he will come.

UNIT 16: FINAL REVISION

1. Noun names a person, place, thing, quality or idea.
2. Pronoun replaces a noun.
3. Verb shows action, state or occurrence.
4. Adjective describes a noun or pronoun.
5. Adverb modifies a verb, adjective, another adverb or sentence.
6. Preposition shows relationship between a noun/pronoun and another word.
7. Conjunction joins words, phrases or clauses.
8. Interjection expresses sudden emotion.
9. Always identify a word's part of speech from its function in the sentence.
