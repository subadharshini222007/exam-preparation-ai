==================================================
UNIT 1: INTRODUCTION TO SENTENCE SYNTHESIS
==================================================

1.1 MEANING OF SENTENCE SYNTHESIS
Sentence synthesis means combining two or more simple sentences into one sentence without changing the meaning.

The combined sentence may be:
1. A simple sentence.
2. A compound sentence.
3. A complex sentence.

Example:
- Separate sentences: "He was ill. He did not come to school."
- Combined simple sentence: "Being ill, he did not come to school."
- Combined compound sentence: "He was ill, so he did not come to school."
- Combined complex sentence: "As he was ill, he did not come to school."

1.2 IMPORTANCE IN TNPSC
In TNPSC General English, sentence synthesis questions may ask the candidate to:
1. Combine two sentences using a participle.
2. Combine two sentences using an infinitive.
3. Combine two sentences using a gerund.
4. Combine two sentences using a conjunction.
5. Combine two sentences using a relative pronoun.
6. Combine two sentences using an adverb clause.
7. Combine sentences without changing their meaning.

UNIT 2: SYNTHESIS USING PARTICIPLES
==================================================

2.1 PRESENT PARTICIPLE
When two actions happen at the same time, use the present participle (verb + ing) for one action.

Rule:
Convert the first action into a present participle and join it to the second sentence.

Examples:
1. He saw the police. He ran away.
   Combined: "Seeing the police, he ran away."

2. She opened the door. She entered the room.
   Combined: "Opening the door, she entered the room."

3. The boy was walking on the road. He saw a snake.
   Combined: "Walking on the road, the boy saw a snake."

4. He finished his work. He went home.
   Combined: "Finishing his work, he went home."

5. She sang a song. The children listened.
   Combined: "Singing a song, she entered the room."

6. The thief saw the policeman. He fled.
   Combined: "Seeing the policeman, the thief fled."

7. He heard the news. He became happy.
   Combined: "Hearing the news, he became happy."

8. The child cried loudly. The mother came.
   Combined: "Crying loudly, the child called his mother."

9. They saw the teacher. They stood up.
   Combined: "Seeing the teacher, they stood up."

10. He walked quickly. He reached the station.
    Combined: "Walking quickly, he reached the station."

2.2 PAST PARTICIPLE
When one action happens before another action, use the past participle.

Examples:
1. The window was broken. It had to be repaired.
   Combined: "The broken window had to be repaired."

2. The letter was written. It was posted.
   Combined: "The written letter was posted."

3. The work was completed. The workers went home.
   Combined: "The work being completed, the workers went home."

4. The road was damaged. It was repaired immediately.
   Combined: "The damaged road was repaired immediately."

5. The tree was uprooted by the storm. It blocked the road.
   Combined: "The tree uprooted by the storm blocked the road."

6. The book was written by R.K. Narayan. It became famous.
   Combined: "The book written by R.K. Narayan became famous."

7. The house was built last year. It is for sale.
   Combined: "The house built last year is for sale."

8. The medicine was prescribed by the doctor. It must be taken regularly.
   Combined: "The medicine prescribed by the doctor must be taken regularly."

9. The thief was caught by the police. He was taken to court.
   Combined: "The thief caught by the police was taken to court."

10. The scheme was launched by the government. It helped many farmers.
    Combined: "The scheme launched by the government helped many farmers."

2.3 PERFECT PARTICIPLE
When the first action is completed before the second action begins, use "having + past participle".

Examples:
1. He finished the work. He went home.
   Combined: "Having finished the work, he went home."

2. She completed the course. She got a job.
   Combined: "Having completed the course, she got a job."

3. The thief saw the police. He ran away.
   Combined: "Having seen the police, the thief ran away."

4. He lost his purse. He reported the matter to the police.
   Combined: "Having lost his purse, he reported the matter to the police."

5. They won the match. They celebrated.
   Combined: "Having won the match, they celebrated."

6. She passed the examination. Her parents were happy.
   Combined: "Having passed the examination, she made her parents happy."

7. He read the book. He returned it to the library.
   Combined: "Having read the book, he returned it to the library."

8. The students submitted the forms. They left the office.
   Combined: "Having submitted the forms, they left the office."

9. The rain stopped. We continued our journey.
   Combined: "The rain having stopped, we continued our journey."

10. He received the letter. He replied immediately.
    Combined: "Having received the letter, he replied immediately."

UNIT 3: SYNTHESIS USING INFINITIVES
==================================================

3.1 INFINITIVE OF PURPOSE
When one sentence shows the purpose of another action, use "to + base verb".

Examples:
1. He works hard. He wants to pass the examination.
   Combined: "He works hard to pass the examination."

2. She went to Chennai. She wanted to attend an interview.
   Combined: "She went to Chennai to attend an interview."

3. We save money. We want to buy a house.
   Combined: "We save money to buy a house."

4. He gets up early. He wants to go for a walk.
   Combined: "He gets up early to go for a walk."

5. The government launched the scheme. It wanted to help farmers.
   Combined: "The government launched the scheme to help farmers."

6. She reads newspapers. She wants to improve her English.
   Combined: "She reads newspapers to improve her English."

7. They planted trees. They wanted to reduce pollution.
   Combined: "They planted trees to reduce pollution."

8. He went to the bank. He wanted to withdraw money.
   Combined: "He went to the bank to withdraw money."

9. The students assembled in the hall. They wanted to attend the meeting.
   Combined: "The students assembled in the hall to attend the meeting."

10. He exercises daily. He wants to remain healthy.
    Combined: "He exercises daily to remain healthy."

3.2 INFINITIVE AFTER ADJECTIVES
Use "to + base verb" after adjectives such as happy, glad, sorry, eager, anxious, ready, afraid.

Examples:
1. He was happy. He heard the good news.
   Combined: "He was happy to hear the good news."

2. She was eager. She wanted to meet her friend.
   Combined: "She was eager to meet her friend."

3. He was afraid. He did not want to cross the river.
   Combined: "He was afraid to cross the river."

4. We were sorry. We heard about his failure.
   Combined: "We were sorry to hear about his failure."

5. She was ready. She wanted to help them.
   Combined: "She was ready to help them."

6. He was anxious. He wanted to know the result.
   Combined: "He was anxious to know the result."

7. They were glad. They received the prize.
   Combined: "They were glad to receive the prize."

8. The child was curious. It wanted to see the train.
   Combined: "The child was curious to see the train."

9. He was foolish. He accepted the false offer.
   Combined: "He was foolish to accept the false offer."

10. She was kind. She helped the old man.
    Combined: "She was kind to help the old man."

3.3 INFINITIVE AFTER NOUNS
Use "to + base verb" after nouns such as time, desire, decision, opportunity, chance, reason.

Examples:
1. It is time. We should go home.
   Combined: "It is time to go home."

2. He has a desire. He wants to become a doctor.
   Combined: "He has a desire to become a doctor."

3. She took a decision. She would resign.
   Combined: "She took a decision to resign."

4. He got an opportunity. He could study abroad.
   Combined: "He got an opportunity to study abroad."

5. They have no chance. They cannot win the match.
   Combined: "They have no chance to win the match."

6. He has no reason. He should fear the police.
   Combined: "He has no reason to fear the police."

7. She has an ambition. She wants to become an IAS officer.
   Combined: "She has an ambition to become an IAS officer."

8. This is the best way. We can solve the problem.
   Combined: "This is the best way to solve the problem."

9. He had no money. He could not buy a ticket.
   Combined: "He had no money to buy a ticket."

10. We have a plan. We will visit Kerala.
    Combined: "We have a plan to visit Kerala."

UNIT 4: SYNTHESIS USING GERUNDS
==================================================

4.1 GERUND AFTER PREPOSITIONS
When the second sentence begins with a preposition, use a gerund after it.

Examples:
1. He was ill. He did not come to school.
   Combined: "Because of being ill, he did not come to school."

2. He saw the snake. He ran away.
   Combined: "On seeing the snake, he ran away."

3. She heard the news. She began to cry.
   Combined: "On hearing the news, she began to cry."

4. He was praised. He was happy.
   Combined: "He was happy on being praised."

5. He completed the work. He went home.
   Combined: "After completing the work, he went home."

6. She finished cooking. She served the food.
   Combined: "After finishing cooking, she served the food."

7. He was tired. He went to bed.
   Combined: "Because of being tired, he went to bed."

8. They reached the station. The train had left.
   Combined: "On reaching the station, they found that the train had left."

9. He was punished. He did not complain.
   Combined: "He did not complain despite being punished."

10. She won the prize. She was happy.
    Combined: "She was happy on winning the prize."

4.2 GERUND AS SUBJECT
Examples:
1. He collects stamps. It is his hobby.
   Combined: "Collecting stamps is his hobby."

2. She teaches poor children. It gives her satisfaction.
   Combined: "Teaching poor children gives her satisfaction."

3. He reads books. It improves knowledge.
   Combined: "Reading books improves knowledge."

4. People waste water. It is wrong.
   Combined: "Wasting water is wrong."

5. He tells lies. It is a bad habit.
   Combined: "Telling lies is a bad habit."

6. She helps the needy. It is a noble act.
   Combined: "Helping the needy is a noble act."

7. They plant trees. It protects the environment.
   Combined: "Planting trees protects the environment."

8. He gets up early. It is good for health.
   Combined: "Getting up early is good for health."

9. We should keep the city clean. It is our duty.
   Combined: "Keeping the city clean is our duty."

10. He drives rashly. It is dangerous.
    Combined: "Driving rashly is dangerous."

UNIT 5: SYNTHESIS USING CONJUNCTIONS
==================================================

5.1 USING "AND"
Use "and" to join two similar or connected ideas.

Examples:
1. Ravi came home. Kumar came home.
   Combined: "Ravi and Kumar came home."

2. She is intelligent. She is hardworking.
   Combined: "She is intelligent and hardworking."

3. He bought a pen. He bought a book.
   Combined: "He bought a pen and a book."

4. The sun set. The stars appeared.
   Combined: "The sun set and the stars appeared."

5. He opened the door. He entered the room.
   Combined: "He opened the door and entered the room."

6. The teacher came. The students stood up.
   Combined: "The teacher came and the students stood up."

7. She cooked food. She served it.
   Combined: "She cooked food and served it."

8. He completed the work. He submitted it.
   Combined: "He completed the work and submitted it."

9. They visited Madurai. They visited Rameswaram.
   Combined: "They visited Madurai and Rameswaram."

10. The rain stopped. We went out.
    Combined: "The rain stopped and we went out."

5.2 USING "BUT", "YET", "STILL"
Use these conjunctions to show contrast.

Examples:
1. He is poor. He is honest.
   Combined: "He is poor but honest."

2. She was ill. She attended the class.
   Combined: "She was ill, yet she attended the class."

3. He worked hard. He failed.
   Combined: "He worked hard, but he failed."

4. The question was difficult. He answered it correctly.
   Combined: "The question was difficult, but he answered it correctly."

5. He is rich. He is not happy.
   Combined: "He is rich, but he is not happy."

6. She is small. She is very brave.
   Combined: "She is small, yet she is very brave."

7. It was raining. They went out.
   Combined: "It was raining, but they went out."

8. He is old. He is active.
   Combined: "He is old, but he is active."

9. The road was narrow. The lorry passed through it.
   Combined: "The road was narrow, yet the lorry passed through it."

10. He had little money. He helped the poor.
    Combined: "He had little money, but he helped the poor."

5.3 USING "OR", "ELSE", "OTHERWISE"
Use these conjunctions to show choice or consequence.

Examples:
1. You must work hard. You will fail.
   Combined: "Work hard, or you will fail."

2. You should apologise. He will not forgive you.
   Combined: "Apologise, or he will not forgive you."

3. Leave now. You will miss the bus.
   Combined: "Leave now, else you will miss the bus."

4. Study regularly. You will not pass.
   Combined: "Study regularly, otherwise you will not pass."

5. Take an umbrella. You will get wet.
   Combined: "Take an umbrella, or you will get wet."

6. Obey the rules. You will be punished.
   Combined: "Obey the rules, or you will be punished."

7. Hurry up. You will be late.
   Combined: "Hurry up, else you will be late."

8. Save money. You will suffer later.
   Combined: "Save money, or you will suffer later."

9. Wear a helmet. You may be fined.
   Combined: "Wear a helmet, or you may be fined."

10. Do not tell lies. People will not trust you.
    Combined: "Do not tell lies, or people will not trust you."

5.4 USING "SO", "THEREFORE", "HENCE"
Use these conjunctions to show result.

Examples:
1. He was ill. He did not come.
   Combined: "He was ill, so he did not come."

2. It rained heavily. The match was cancelled.
   Combined: "It rained heavily; therefore, the match was cancelled."

3. He worked hard. He passed.
   Combined: "He worked hard, so he passed."

4. The road was blocked. The vehicles could not move.
   Combined: "The road was blocked; hence, the vehicles could not move."

5. She was tired. She went to bed early.
   Combined: "She was tired, so she went to bed early."

6. He was late. He missed the train.
   Combined: "He was late; therefore, he missed the train."

7. The question was easy. Everyone answered it.
   Combined: "The question was easy, so everyone answered it."

8. He was hungry. He ate food.
   Combined: "He was hungry, so he ate food."

9. The power failed. The class was stopped.
   Combined: "The power failed; hence, the class was stopped."

10. He was brave. He saved the child.
    Combined: "He was brave; therefore, he saved the child."

5.5 USING "THOUGH", "ALTHOUGH", "EVEN THOUGH"
Use these subordinating conjunctions to show contrast.

Examples:
1. He is poor. He is honest.
   Combined: "Though he is poor, he is honest."

2. It rained heavily. They went out.
   Combined: "Although it rained heavily, they went out."

3. She was ill. She attended the meeting.
   Combined: "Though she was ill, she attended the meeting."

4. He is old. He is active.
   Combined: "Although he is old, he is active."

5. The question was difficult. He answered it.
   Combined: "Though the question was difficult, he answered it."

6. He had little money. He helped the poor.
   Combined: "Though he had little money, he helped the poor."

7. She was tired. She continued working.
   Combined: "Although she was tired, she continued working."

8. The road was narrow. The lorry passed.
   Combined: "Though the road was narrow, the lorry passed."

9. He failed many times. He did not lose hope.
   Combined: "Though he failed many times, he did not lose hope."

10. It was cold. He did not wear a coat.
    Combined: "Although it was cold, he did not wear a coat."

5.6 USING "BECAUSE", "AS", "SINCE"
Use these subordinating conjunctions to show reason.

Examples:
1. He was ill. He did not come.
   Combined: "Because he was ill, he did not come."

2. It was raining. We stayed indoors.
   Combined: "As it was raining, we stayed indoors."

3. She was tired. She went to bed early.
   Combined: "Since she was tired, she went to bed early."

4. He was hungry. He ate food.
   Combined: "Because he was hungry, he ate food."

5. The road was blocked. The vehicles could not move.
   Combined: "As the road was blocked, the vehicles could not move."

6. He was late. He missed the train.
   Combined: "Since he was late, he missed the train."

7. She was happy. She had won the prize.
   Combined: "She was happy because she had won the prize."

8. The power failed. The class was stopped.
   Combined: "As the power failed, the class was stopped."

9. He was brave. He saved the child.
   Combined: "Because he was brave, he saved the child."

10. They were poor. They could not buy a house.
    Combined: "Since they were poor, they could not buy a house."

5.7 USING "IF", "UNLESS"
Use these conjunctions to show condition.

Examples:
1. Work hard. You will pass.
   Combined: "If you work hard, you will pass."

2. Do not work hard. You will fail.
   Combined: "If you do not work hard, you will fail."

3. Apologise. He will forgive you.
   Combined: "If you apologise, he will forgive you."

4. Do not apologise. He will not forgive you.
   Combined: "Unless you apologise, he will not forgive you."

5. Take an umbrella. You will not get wet.
   Combined: "If you take an umbrella, you will not get wet."

6. Do not take an umbrella. You will get wet.
   Combined: "Unless you take an umbrella, you will get wet."

7. Leave now. You will catch the bus.
   Combined: "If you leave now, you will catch the bus."

8. Do not leave now. You will miss the bus.
   Combined: "Unless you leave now, you will miss the bus."

9. Obey the rules. You will not be punished.
   Combined: "If you obey the rules, you will not be punished."

10. Do not obey the rules. You will be punished.
    Combined: "Unless you obey the rules, you will be punished."

   Combined: "The man who came here is my uncle."

UNIT 10: FULL STOP OR PERIOD
==================================================

10.1 USES
1. At the end of a statement:
   - The sun rises in the east.
   - Chennai is the capital of Tamil Nadu.

2. At the end of an imperative sentence:
   - Close the door.
   - Please submit the form.

3. After abbreviations:
   - Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam
   - Mr. Ravi
   - etc.
   - e.g.

10.2 EXAMPLES
1. "India is a democratic country."
2. "The teacher entered the classroom."
3. "Please switch off the light."
4. "He lives in Coimbatore."
5. "The meeting will begin at 10 a.m."

==================================================
UNIT 11: COMMA
==================================================

11.1 USES
1. To separate items in a list:
   - I bought apples, mangoes, bananas and grapes.

2. After introductory words or phrases:
   - However, he did not come.
   - After the meeting, we went home.

3. Before conjunctions joining two main clauses:
   - He was tired, but he continued working.

4. To separate a noun of address:
   - Ravi, please come here.
   - Students, listen carefully.

5. To separate non-essential information:
   - Ravi, my best friend, lives in Chennai.

6. After a direct speech introduction:
   - He said, "I am busy."

7. To separate question tags:
   - You are coming, aren't you?

8. In dates and addresses:
   - He was born on 15 August 1947.
   - She lives at 12, Gandhi Road, Chennai.

9. To separate thousands in numbers:
   - 1,000
   - 25,000
   - 1,00,000

10. To prevent confusion:
    - "Let us eat, Grandma." means inviting Grandma to eat.
    - "Let us eat Grandma." gives a different meaning.

==================================================
UNIT 12: QUESTION MARK
==================================================

12.1 USES
1. At the end of a direct question:
   - Where do you live?
   - Have you finished the work?
   - Who is your class teacher?

2. After tag questions:
   - You are coming, aren't you?
   - She has finished, hasn't she?

3. After a question within a sentence:
   - He asked, "Can you help me?"

12.2 IMPORTANT RULE
Do not use a question mark after an indirect question.
- Direct: He said, "Where do you live?"
- Indirect: He asked where I lived.

UNIT 13: EXCLAMATION MARK

13.1 USES
1. After interjections:

2. After exclamatory sentences:
   - What a beautiful flower!
   - How intelligent she is!

3. After strong commands:
   - Stop!
   - Fire!
   - Get out!

13.2 EXAMPLES
1. "Wow! That is amazing."
2. "What a wonderful performance!"
3. "Ouch! That hurt."
4. "Hush! The baby is sleeping."
5. "Long live our nation!"

UNIT 14: COLON
==================================================

14.1 USES
1. To introduce a list:

2. To introduce an explanation:
   - He had one aim: to become a doctor.

3. To introduce a quotation:
   - The teacher said: "Hard work never fails."

4. To separate hours and minutes:
   - The class begins at 10:30 a.m.

5. After the salutation in a formal letter:
   - Dear Sir:
   - Dear Madam:

UNIT 15: SEMICOLON
==================================================

15.1 USES
1. To join two closely related main clauses:
   - He was ill; he did not come to school.
   - It was raining; therefore, the match was cancelled.

2. To separate items in a list when the items already contain commas:
   - We visited Chennai, Tamil Nadu; Mumbai, Maharashtra; and Kolkata, West Bengal.

3. Before conjunctive adverbs such as however, therefore, hence, moreover:
   - He worked hard; however, he failed.

==================================================
UNIT 16: APOSTROPHE
==================================================

16.1 USES
1. To show possession:
   - Ravi's book
   - the girl's bag
   - boys' hostel
   - children's park

2. To show omission of letters:
   - don't = do not
   - can't = cannot
   - I'm = I am
   - it's = it is
   - won't = will not

3. To form plurals of letters and numbers:
   - Mind your p's and q's.
   - There are two 5's in this number.

16.2 POSSESSIVE RULES
1. Singular noun: add apostrophe + s.
   - Ravi's pen, the teacher's desk.

2. Plural noun ending in -s: add only apostrophe.
   - boys' hostel, teachers' meeting.

3. Irregular plural not ending in -s: add apostrophe + s.
   - children's park, women's college, men's room.

4. Names ending in -s: usually add apostrophe + s.
   - James's book, Charles's car.

UNIT 17: QUOTATION MARKS
==================================================

17.1 USES
1. To enclose the exact words of a speaker:
   - He said, "I am busy."
   - She asked, "Where do you live?"

2. To enclose titles of short works:
   - "The Road Not Taken"
   - "Where the Mind is Without Fear"

3. To enclose a word used in a special sense:
   - He is a so-called "expert".

17.2 PUNCTUATION WITH QUOTATION MARKS
1. Comma before direct speech:
   - He said, "I am tired."

2. Question mark inside quotation marks if the quoted words are a question:
   - She asked, "Are you ready?"

3. Full stop inside quotation marks in American usage:
   - He said, "I am busy."

UNIT 18: HYPHEN, DASH AND BRACKETS
==================================================

18.1 HYPHEN
A hyphen joins two words or parts of words.

Uses:
1. Compound adjectives:

   - a twenty-year-old boy

2. Compound numbers:
   - twenty-one

   - ninety-nine

3. Prefixes:
   - ex-minister
   - self-confidence
   - co-operation

18.2 DASH
A dash indicates a sudden break, an afterthought or emphasis.

Examples:
- He is coming today - I am sure.
- The result was unexpected - a complete surprise.

18.3 BRACKETS
Brackets are used to add extra information.

Examples:

- He was born in Madurai (Tamil Nadu).
- The scheme (launched in 2023) helps farmers.

UNIT 29: PREFIXES AND SUFFIXES
==================================================

29.1 PREFIXES
A prefix is a letter or group of letters added before a word to change its meaning.

1. Un- means not:
   unhappy, unable, unfair.

2. Dis- means not or opposite:
   dishonest, disagree, disappear.

3. Im-/In- means not:
   impossible, incorrect, incomplete.

4. Re- means again:
   rewrite, return, rebuild.

5. Pre- means before:
   preview, prepay, prehistoric.

6. Mis- means wrongly:
   misunderstand, misuse, mislead.

7. Anti- means against:
   antisocial, antibiotic, anti-war.

8. Inter- means between:
   international, intercity, interact.

9. Sub- means under:
   subway, submarine, subtitle.

10. Super- means above or beyond:
    superhuman, supermarket, supervisor.

29.2 SUFFIXES
A suffix is a letter or group of letters added after a word to change its meaning or grammatical function.

1. -ness forms nouns:
   kindness, happiness, darkness.

2. -ment forms nouns:
   movement, agreement, development.

3. -tion forms nouns:
   education, action, decision.

4. -ship forms nouns:
   friendship, leadership, citizenship.

5. -hood forms nouns:
   childhood, brotherhood, motherhood.

6. -ful means full of:
   beautiful, helpful, careful.

7. -less means without:
   careless, hopeless, fearless.

8. -able means capable of:
   readable, comfortable, acceptable.

9. -ly forms adverbs:
   quickly, slowly, carefully.

10. -ify means to make:
    simplify, purify, clarify.

SENTENCE SYNTHESIS:
1. Use present participle for simultaneous actions.
2. Use perfect participle for an action completed before another.
3. Use infinitive to show purpose.
4. Use gerund after prepositions.
5. Use conjunctions such as and, but, or, so, because, though, if.
6. Use relative pronouns such as who, which, that and whose.
7. Do not change the meaning while combining sentences.

PUNCTUATION:
1. Full stop ends a statement.
2. Comma separates words, phrases and clauses.
3. Question mark ends a direct question.
4. Exclamation mark expresses strong emotion.
5. Colon introduces a list or explanation.
6. Semicolon joins closely related main clauses.
7. Apostrophe shows possession or omission.
8. Quotation marks enclose exact spoken words.
9. Hyphen joins compound words.
10. Capital letters begin sentences and proper nouns.

VOCABULARY:
1. Synonyms are words with similar meanings.
2. Antonyms are words with opposite meanings.
3. One-word substitution replaces a phrase with one word.
4. Idioms have figurative meanings.
5. Phrasal verbs combine verbs with prepositions or adverbs.
6. Homophones have the same sound but different meanings.
7. Prefixes are added before a word.
8. Suffixes are added after a word.
9. Correct spelling is essential in TNPSC.
10. Learn vocabulary through examples and repeated revision.
