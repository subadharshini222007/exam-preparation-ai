TNPSC GROUP 2 – APTITUDE
MENTAL ABILITY – BLOOD RELATION

1. INTRODUCTION

Blood Relation questions test the ability to identify relationships between family members. These questions may describe relationships directly or through statements, and the candidate must determine the correct relationship.

2. BASIC FAMILY RELATIONSHIPS

Father – Male parent
Mother – Female parent
Son – Male child
Daughter – Female child
Brother – Male sibling
Sister – Female sibling
Husband – Male spouse
Wife – Female spouse
Grandfather – Father of a parent
Grandmother – Mother of a parent
Uncle – Brother of a parent
Aunt – Sister of a parent
Nephew – Son of a sibling
Niece – Daughter of a sibling
Cousin – Child of an uncle or aunt

3. IMPORTANT RELATIONSHIP RULES

Rule 1: Father's or mother's son may be the person's brother or the person himself.

Rule 2: Father's or mother's daughter may be the person's sister or the person herself.

Rule 3: Father's brother is uncle.

Rule 4: Mother's sister is aunt.

Rule 5: Brother's son is nephew.

Rule 6: Sister's daughter is niece.

Rule 7: Father's father is paternal grandfather.

Rule 8: Mother's mother is maternal grandmother.

Rule 9: Son's wife is daughter-in-law.

Rule 10: Daughter's husband is son-in-law.

4. SOLVED EXAMPLES

Example 1:
Pointing to a boy, Ravi says, "He is the son of my father." How is the boy related to Ravi?

Answer:
The boy is Ravi's brother if he is not Ravi himself.

Example 2:
A is the mother of B. B is the sister of C. How is A related to C?

Solution:
A is the mother of B.
B and C are siblings.
Therefore, A is the mother of C.

Answer: Mother.

Example 3:
P is the brother of Q. Q is the mother of R. How is P related to R?

Solution:
Q is R's mother.
P is Q's brother.
Therefore, P is R's maternal uncle.

Answer: Maternal uncle.

Example 4:
A is the father of B. B is the father of C. How is A related to C?

Solution:
A is B's father.
B is C's father.
Therefore, A is C's grandfather.

Answer: Grandfather.

Example 5:
Pointing to a woman, Kumar says, "She is the daughter of my mother's only son." Who is the woman to Kumar?

Solution:
Kumar's mother's only son is Kumar himself.
The daughter of Kumar is his daughter.

Answer: Daughter.

5. FAMILY TREE METHOD

Use these symbols to draw a family tree:

Male = M
Female = F
Marriage = —
Parent-child relationship = vertical line
Siblings = same generation

Always identify the gender and generation before deciding the relationship.

6. IMPORTANT EXAM SHORTCUTS

7. Read the statement carefully.

8. Identify the person from whose point of view the relationship is asked.

9. Mark the gender of each person.

10. Draw a small family tree for complicated questions.

11. Do not assume that a person is male or female unless the question provides the information.

12. Read the final question carefully: it may ask how A is related to B, not how B is related to A.

13. PRACTICE QUESTIONS

Q6. A is the son of B. B is the daughter of C. How is C related to A?

Coding and Decoding is a reasoning topic in which letters, numbers, or words are converted into another form according to a particular rule. The candidate must identify the rule and apply it to the given word or number.

2. TYPES OF CODING

3. Letter shifting

4. LETTER SHIFTING

Example 1:
If CAT is coded as DBU, how is DOG coded?

Rule:
C → D (+1)
A → B (+1)
T → U (+1)

Apply the same rule:
D → E
O → P
G → H

Answer: EPH

Example 2:
If BOOK is coded as CPPL, how is PEN coded?

Rule: Shift every letter one position forward.

P → Q
E → F
N → O

Answer: QFO

5. BACKWARD LETTER SHIFTING

Example:
If DBU is the code for CAT, each letter has been shifted one position forward.

To decode DBU:
D → C
B → A
U → T

Answer: CAT

6. REVERSE ALPHABET CODING

In reverse alphabet coding:

A ↔ Z
B ↔ Y
C ↔ X
D ↔ W
E ↔ V
F ↔ U
G ↔ T
H ↔ S
I ↔ R
J ↔ Q
K ↔ P
L ↔ O
M ↔ N

Example:
If CAT is coded using reverse alphabet positions:

C → X
A → Z
T → G

Answer: XZG

7. NUMBER CODING

Example:
If A = 1, B = 2, C = 3, what is the code for BAD?

B = 2
A = 1
D = 4

Answer: 2-1-4

Example:
If the value of a word is the sum of its letter positions, find the value of CAT.

C = 3
A = 1
T = 20

Total = 3 + 1 + 20 = 24

Answer: 24

8. SOLVED EXAMPLES

Example 1:
If SUN is coded as TVO, how is MOON coded?

Rule: Shift each letter one position forward.

M → N

Rule: Shift each letter one position forward.

B → C

R → S
D → E

Answer: CJSE

Example 3:
If APPLE is coded as ELPPA, what is the coding rule?

Rule: Reverse the order of the letters.

Answer: The letters are written in reverse order.

9. IMPORTANT SHORTCUTS

10. Write the alphabet positions when needed.

11. Compare the original and coded words letter by letter.

12. Check whether letters move forward or backward.

13. Check whether the word is reversed.

14. Check whether the code uses letter positions or their sum.

15. Apply the identified rule consistently to every letter.

16. Do not assume the same coding rule for every question.

TNPSC GROUP 2 – APTITUDE
MENTAL ABILITY – VENN DIAGRAM

1. INTRODUCTION

A Venn Diagram is a diagram that uses circles to represent groups or sets. The relationship between the groups is shown by the positions and intersections of the circles.

Venn Diagram questions test the ability to understand similarities, differences, and relationships between categories.

2. BASIC TYPES

Type 1: Completely separate groups

Example:
Dogs and Cars.

Dogs and cars are different categories, so their circles do not overlap.

Type 2: One group inside another

Example:
Roses and Flowers.

All roses are flowers, but not all flowers are roses. The Roses circle is inside the Flowers circle.

Type 3: Partially overlapping groups

Example:
Students who play Cricket and students who play Football.

Some students may play both games, so the circles overlap.

Type 4: Three related groups

Example:
Doctors, Women, and Human Beings.

The relationships depend on the information given in the question. Not every member of one group necessarily belongs to another group.

3. IMPORTANT SYMBOLS

Union (A ∪ B):
Elements belonging to A or B or both.

Intersection (A ∩ B):
Elements common to both A and B.

Subset (A ⊆ B):
Every element of A belongs to B.

Disjoint sets:
Two sets that have no common elements.

4. SOLVED EXAMPLES

Example 1:
Which diagram best represents Roses, Flowers, and Animals?

Solution:
All roses are flowers.
Animals are a separate category in this basic classification question.

Answer:
A small Roses circle inside a larger Flowers circle, with Animals in a separate circle.

Example 2:
Which diagram represents Students, Boys, and Girls?

Solution:
Boys and girls are categories of students when the question defines them as such. The exact diagram depends on whether the question assumes the groups are exhaustive and mutually exclusive.

Answer:
A Students circle containing separate Boys and Girls regions, if every student is classified into exactly one of these two groups.

Example 3:
Which diagram represents Cats, Dogs, and Animals?

Solution:
Cats and dogs are both animals, but cats are not dogs and dogs are not cats.

Answer:
Two separate smaller circles, Cats and Dogs, inside a larger Animals circle.

Example 4:
In a class, 20 students like Cricket, 15 like Football, and 5 like both. How many like at least one of the two games?

Formula:
At least one = Cricket + Football − Both

= 20 + 15 − 5
= 30

Answer: 30 students.

Example 5:
In a group of 40 students, 22 like Mathematics, 18 like Science, and 8 like both. How many like neither subject?

Students liking at least one:
22 + 18 − 8 = 32

Students liking neither:
40 − 32 = 8

Answer: 8 students.

5. IMPORTANT FORMULAS

For two sets A and B:

n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B)

Number belonging to neither set:
Total − n(A ∪ B)

Number belonging only to A:
n(A) − n(A ∩ B)

Number belonging only to B:
n(B) − n(A ∩ B)

6. EXAM SHORTCUTS

7. Identify whether the categories are separate, overlapping, or contained.

8. Read words such as all, some, none, and only.

9. For numerical questions, subtract the common group once.

10. To find neither, subtract the union from the total.

11. Do not assume that two categories overlap unless the question supports it.

12. Draw circles before choosing the answer.

13. PRACTICE QUESTIONS

Q1. Which relationship is correct for Mangoes, Fruits, and Vehicles?
A) All are the same group
B) Mangoes are inside Fruits; Vehicles are separate
C) Fruits are inside Mangoes
D) Vehicles are inside Fruits

Answer: B

Q2. In a class of 50 students, 30 like Mathematics, 25 like Science, and 10 like both. How many like at least one subject?

B) 40
C) 45
D) 55

Answer: C) 45

Q3. In a group of 60 people, 35 like Tea, 25 like Coffee, and 10 like both. How many like neither?
A) 5
B) 10
C) 15
D) 20

Answer: B) 10

Q4. Which relationship best represents Cats, Dogs, and Animals?
A) Cats and Dogs are both inside Animals and do not overlap
B) Animals are inside Cats
C) Dogs are inside Cats
D) All circles are identical

Answer: A

Q5. In a group, 24 play Cricket, 16 play Football, and 6 play both. How many play only Cricket?
A) 6
B) 16
C) 18
D) 24

Answer: C) 18

Q6. In a group of 40 students, 22 like English, 15 like Tamil, and 7 like both. How many like only Tamil?
A) 7
B) 8
C) 15

Answer: B) 8

Q7. Which relationship best represents Squares and Rectangles?
A) All rectangles are squares

8. CONCLUSION

Venn Diagrams help solve classification and set-based questions visually. Understanding containment, overlap, and set formulas is essential for solving numerical problems quickly.

TNPSC GROUP 2 – APTITUDE
MENTAL ABILITY – DIRECTIONS

1. INTRODUCTION

Direction questions test the ability to determine a person's final position or direction after moving through a series of routes.

2. FOUR MAIN DIRECTIONS

North – Up
South – Down
East – Right
West – Left

3. INTERMEDIATE DIRECTIONS

North-East (NE)
North-West (NW)
South-East (SE)
South-West (SW)

4. RIGHT AND LEFT TURNS

When facing North:
Right = East
Left = West

When facing South:
Right = West
Left = East

When facing East:
Right = South
Left = North

When facing West:
Right = North
Left = South

5. SOLVED EXAMPLES

Example 1:
Ravi walks 5 km North and then 3 km East. In which direction is he from the starting point?

Solution:
He is North and East of the starting point.

Answer: North-East.

Example 2:
A person walks 6 km East and then 6 km West. How far is the person from the starting point?

Solution:
The movements cancel each other.

Answer: 0 km.

Example 3:
A person faces North and turns right. Which direction is the person facing?

Answer: East.

Example 4:
A person walks 4 km South and then 3 km North. How far and in which direction is the person from the starting point?

Solution:
Net movement = 4 − 3 = 1 km South.

Answer: 1 km South.

Example 5:
A person walks 3 km East and 4 km North. What is the shortest distance from the starting point?

Formula:
Distance = √(3² + 4²)
= √(9 + 16)
= √25
= 5 km

Answer: 5 km.

6. IMPORTANT FORMULAS

If movements are in opposite directions on the same straight line:
Net distance = Difference between the distances.

If movements are in perpendicular directions:
Shortest distance = √(Horizontal distance² + Vertical distance²)

If both movements are in the same direction:
Net distance = Sum of the distances.

7. EXAM SHORTCUTS

8. Draw a small compass.

9. Mark the starting point.

10. Track every turn and movement in order.

11. Do not confuse the direction faced with the direction travelled.

12. Cancel opposite movements when they lie on the same straight line.

13. Use the Pythagorean theorem for perpendicular movements.

14. For left and right turns, first identify the direction the person is facing.

15. PRACTICE QUESTIONS
