TNPSC GROUP 2 – MENTAL ABILITY
TOPIC 2: ANALYTICAL REASONING

1. INTRODUCTION

Analytical Reasoning is the ability to examine information systematically, identify patterns, understand relationships, and solve problems using a structured approach.

It often involves arrangements, comparisons, rankings, relationships, and logical puzzles.

2. IMPORTANT TYPES

A. Ranking and Order

Ranking questions ask candidates to determine the position of a person or object.

Formula:

Total persons = Rank from the left + Rank from the right - 1

Example:

Ravi is 8th from the left and 12th from the right.

Total persons = 8 + 12 - 1
Total persons = 19

Answer: 19 persons.

B. Linear Arrangement

In linear arrangement questions, people or objects are arranged in a straight line according to given conditions.

Example:

Five students A, B, C, D, and E stand in a row.

Conditions:
A stands to the left of B.
C stands to the right of B.
D stands to the left of A.
E stands to the right of C.

One possible arrangement is:

D – A – B – C – E

C. Seating Arrangement

Seating arrangement questions involve placing people around a table or in a row.

Example:

Four students P, Q, R, and S sit in a row.

Conditions:
P sits to the left of Q.
R sits to the right of Q.
S sits to the right of R.

Arrangement:

P – Q – R – S

D. Blood Relations

Blood relation questions identify family relationships.

Important relationships:

Father's father – Grandfather
Mother's mother – Grandmother
Father's brother – Uncle
Mother's sister – Aunt
Brother's son – Nephew
Sister's daughter – Niece
Father's sister – Aunt

Example:

Arun is the son of Kumar. Kumar is the brother of Meena.

Question: How is Meena related to Arun?

Answer: Aunt.

E. Direction Sense

Direction questions test the ability to identify positions and movements.

Basic directions:

North
South
East
West

Opposite directions:

North – South
East – West

Example:

A person walks 5 metres north and then 3 metres east.

The person is now northeast of the starting point.

Shortest distance:

Distance = √(5² + 3²)
Distance = √34 metres

F. Classification

Classification questions ask candidates to identify the item that does not belong to a group.

Example:

Apple, Mango, Banana, Carrot

Answer: Carrot.

Explanation:
Apple, mango, and banana are commonly classified as fruits. Carrot is a root vegetable.

G. Analogy

Analogy questions identify relationships between pairs of words or objects.

Example:

Bird : Nest :: Bee : ?

Answer: Hive.

Explanation:
A nest is a bird's dwelling, and a hive is a bee's dwelling.

3. PROBLEM-SOLVING METHOD

4. Read all the conditions carefully.

5. Identify the people, objects, or positions involved.

6. Write down the conditions.

7. Use a simple diagram or table.

8. Apply each condition systematically.

9. Check whether the final arrangement satisfies every condition.

10. PRACTICE QUESTIONS

Question 1:

A student is 7th from the left and 9th from the right in a row. Find the total number of students.

Options:
A) 14
B) 15
C) 16
D) 17

Answer: B) 15

Explanation:
Total = 7 + 9 - 1 = 15.

Question 2:

A person walks 4 metres north and then 4 metres south. Where is the person relative to the starting point?

Options:
A) North
B) South
C) East
D) At the starting point

Answer: D) At the starting point.

Question 3:

Ravi is the brother of Priya. Priya is the daughter of Kumar. How is Ravi related to Kumar?

Options:
A) Brother
B) Son
C) Uncle
D) Father

Answer: B) Son.

Question 4:

Find the odd one out.

Options:
A) Circle
B) Triangle
C) Square
D) Mango

Answer: D) Mango.

Explanation:
The first three are geometrical shapes.

Question 5:

Complete the analogy.

Doctor : Hospital :: Teacher : ?

Options:
A) Court
B) School
C) Bank
D) Factory

Answer: B) School.

5. EXAM TIPS

6. Draw diagrams for seating and direction questions.

7. Use the ranking formula carefully.

8. Track left and right directions from the correct reference point.

9. Read family relationships from one generation to another.

10. Verify every condition before selecting an answer.

CONCLUSION

Analytical Reasoning develops systematic thinking and problem-solving ability. Practice with arrangements, rankings, directions, and relationships helps improve speed and accuracy in competitive examinations.
