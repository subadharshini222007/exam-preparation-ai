TNPSC GROUP 2 – MENTAL ABILITY
TOPIC 1: LOGICAL REASONING

1. INTRODUCTION

Logical Reasoning is the ability to think clearly, analyze information, identify relationships, and arrive at a valid conclusion. It tests a person's ability to solve problems using logic rather than assumptions.

Logical reasoning questions are commonly based on statements, conclusions, assumptions, arguments, and logical relationships.

2. TYPES OF LOGICAL REASONING

A. Statement and Conclusion

A statement provides certain information. A conclusion is an inference that logically follows from the given statement.

Example:

Statement: All doctors are educated. Ravi is a doctor.

Conclusion: Ravi is educated.

Explanation:
The statement says that every doctor is educated. Since Ravi is a doctor, he must be educated.

Answer: The conclusion follows.

B. Statement and Assumption

An assumption is an idea accepted as true without being directly stated.

Example:

Statement: The government introduced free bus passes for school students.

Assumption: Students need transportation to attend school.

Explanation:
The policy is intended to help students travel to school. Therefore, the assumption is reasonable.

C. Cause and Effect

A cause is an event that produces a result. An effect is the result of an event.

Example:

Statement 1: Heavy rainfall occurred throughout the night.
Statement 2: Several roads were flooded the next morning.

Explanation:
Heavy rainfall can cause flooding.

Answer: Statement 1 is a possible cause of Statement 2.

D. Logical Deduction

Logical deduction uses general rules and specific facts to reach a conclusion.

Example:

All squares are rectangles.
All rectangles are quadrilaterals.

Conclusion: All squares are quadrilaterals.

Answer: The conclusion follows logically.

3. RULES FOR SOLVING LOGICAL REASONING QUESTIONS

4. Read the statement carefully.

5. Identify the facts provided.

6. Do not introduce information that is not given.

7. Distinguish between facts and assumptions.

8. Check whether the conclusion necessarily follows.

9. Eliminate options that contradict the statement.

10. Choose the answer supported by the available information.

11. IMPORTANT EXAMPLES

Example 1:

Statements:
All cats are animals.
All animals need food.

Conclusion:
All cats need food.

Answer: The conclusion follows.

Example 2:

Statements:
Some students are athletes.
All athletes are physically active.

Conclusion:
Some students are physically active.

Answer: The conclusion follows.

Example 3:

Statement:
The school library extended its working hours during examination week.

Possible assumption:
Students need additional time to study.

Answer: The assumption is reasonable.

Example 4:

Statements:
All pens are stationery items.
Some stationery items are expensive.

Conclusion:
All pens are expensive.

Answer: The conclusion does not follow.

Explanation:
The statements do not establish that pens are expensive.

6. EXAM TIPS

7. Focus only on the information provided.

8. Avoid conclusions based on personal knowledge.

9. In syllogism questions, identify universal and particular statements.

10. In cause-and-effect questions, distinguish actual evidence from mere possibility.

11. Practise different question patterns regularly.

CONCLUSION

Logical Reasoning improves the ability to evaluate statements, identify valid conclusions, and make decisions based on facts. Regular practice helps candidates solve TNPSC Mental Ability questions accurately.
