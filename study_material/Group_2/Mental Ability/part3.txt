# LOGICAL REASONING AND ANALYTICAL REASONING

## PART A: LOGICAL REASONING

### 1. Logical Reasoning

Logical Reasoning is the ability to think logically, identify relationships, analyze statements, and arrive at correct conclusions.

### 2. Statements and Conclusions

A statement gives some information. A conclusion is an inference drawn from the given statement.

**Example:**

Statement: All doctors are educated.

Conclusion I: All educated people are doctors.
Conclusion II: All doctors are educated.

Answer: Only Conclusion II follows.

### 3. Syllogism

Syllogism questions involve two or more statements and conclusions. We must determine whether the conclusions logically follow from the statements.

**Example:**

Statements:

* All roses are flowers.
* All flowers are plants.

Conclusion: All roses are plants.

Answer: The conclusion follows.

### 4. Statement and Assumption

An assumption is an unstated idea that is accepted as true when a statement is made.

**Example:**

Statement: The government advises people to save water.

Assumption: Water conservation is important.

Answer: The assumption is valid.

### 5. Assertion and Reason

These questions contain an assertion and a reason. The student must determine whether both are true and whether the reason correctly explains the assertion.

**Example:**

Assertion: Plants need sunlight for photosynthesis.
Reason: Sunlight provides energy for photosynthesis.

Answer: Both are true, and the reason correctly explains the assertion.

### 6. Strong and Weak Arguments

A strong argument is relevant, logical, and supported by facts. A weak argument is irrelevant, illogical, or unsupported.

**Example:**

Question: Should plastic bags be restricted?

Argument I: Plastic pollution harms the environment.
Argument II: Some people like colourful plastic bags.

Answer: Argument I is strong because it provides a relevant reason.

### 7. Cause and Effect

Cause and effect questions identify the relationship between an event and the result produced by it.

**Example:**

Event I: Heavy rainfall occurred.
Event II: Several streets were flooded.

Answer: Heavy rainfall may be the cause, and flooding may be the effect.

### 8. Course of Action

These questions ask us to choose a practical and appropriate action to solve a given problem.

**Example:**

Problem: Road accidents are increasing near a school.

Possible action: Install speed-limit signs and provide traffic monitoring near the school.

Answer: This is an appropriate course of action.

### 9. Logical Deduction

Logical deduction involves reaching a conclusion based on the information provided.

**Example:**

Statements:

* All engineers are graduates.
* Ravi is an engineer.

Conclusion: Ravi is a graduate.

Answer: The conclusion follows logically.

## PART B: ANALYTICAL REASONING

### 1. Analytical Reasoning

Analytical Reasoning involves examining information, identifying patterns, comparing facts, and solving problems systematically.

### 2. Ranking and Order

Ranking questions determine the position of a person or object in a sequence.

**Example:**

Ravi ranks 8th from the top and 12th from the bottom.

Total number of students = 8 + 12 − 1 = 19.

**Formula:**

Total = Rank from top + Rank from bottom − 1

### 3. Seating Arrangement

Seating arrangement questions involve arranging people according to given conditions.

Common types:

* Linear arrangement
* Circular arrangement
* Facing north or south
* Facing inside or outside

**Example:**

A, B, and C sit in a row. A sits to the left of B, and C sits to the right of B.

Arrangement: A – B – C

Tip: Write down the conditions and draw a simple diagram before answering.

### 4. Blood Relations

Blood relation questions identify family relationships using the information provided.

Common relationships:

* Father's son: Brother or self
* Mother's daughter: Sister or self
* Father's brother: Uncle
* Mother's sister: Aunt
* Brother's daughter: Niece
* Sister's son: Nephew

**Example:**

Ravi is the brother of Kumar. Kumar is the father of Priya.

Question: How is Ravi related to Priya?

Answer: Uncle.

### 5. Direction Sense

Direction questions involve determining the direction or distance travelled by a person.

Main directions:

* North
* South
* East
* West

Opposite directions:

* North ↔ South
* East ↔ West

**Example:**

A person walks 5 km north and then 3 km east.

The person is now in the north-east direction relative to the starting point.

Tip: Draw the directions on paper to avoid confusion.

### 6. Number Series

Number series questions require identifying the pattern between numbers.

**Example 1:**

2, 4, 6, 8, 10, ?

Pattern: Add 2.

Answer: 12.

**Example 2:**

3, 6, 12, 24, ?

Pattern: Multiply by 2.

Answer: 48.

Common patterns:

* Addition
* Subtraction
* Multiplication
* Division
* Squares
* Cubes
* Alternating patterns

### 7. Letter Series

Letter series questions involve finding patterns in alphabetical sequences.

Alphabet positions:
A = 1, B = 2, C = 3, ..., Z = 26.

**Example:**

A, C, E, G, ?

Pattern: Skip one letter each time.

Answer: I.

### 8. Coding and Decoding

Coding and decoding questions involve converting words or letters according to a particular rule.

**Example:**

If CAT is coded as DBU, how is DOG coded?

Rule: Each letter is moved one position forward.

D → E
O → P
G → H

Answer: EPH.

### 9. Analogy

Analogy questions identify a relationship between two words, numbers, or objects.

**Example:**

Doctor : Hospital :: Teacher : ?

Answer: School.

Explanation: A doctor commonly works in a hospital, while a teacher commonly works in a school.

### 10. Classification and Odd One Out

These questions require identifying the item that does not belong to the same group.

**Example:**

Apple, Mango, Banana, Carrot

Answer: Carrot.

Explanation: Carrot is commonly classified as a vegetable, while the others are fruits.

### 11. Scheduling and Ordering

These questions require arranging events or people according to given conditions.

**Example:**

Three students A, B, and C attend a test.

* A attends before B.
* B attends before C.

Correct order: A → B → C.

Tip: Identify the fixed conditions first, then arrange the remaining items.

### 12. Analytical Puzzles

Analytical puzzles combine multiple conditions involving people, places, objects, or activities.

Common puzzle types:

* Person and profession
* Person and location
* Days and schedules
* Objects and colours
* Ordering and grouping

**Example:**

Three people—A, B, and C—choose different fruits: apple, mango, and orange.

Conditions:

* A chooses apple.
* B does not choose mango.

Based on these conditions, eliminate impossible combinations to find the correct arrangement.

Tip: Use a table to record the conditions and possible combinations.

## PREPARATION TIPS

1. Understand the question before selecting an answer.
2. Practise different question patterns.
3. Use diagrams for seating arrangements, directions, and blood relations.
4. Identify the rule before solving series questions.
5. Eliminate incorrect options carefully.
6. Practise under a time limit to improve speed and accuracy.
