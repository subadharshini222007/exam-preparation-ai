#  INDIAN FREEDOM STRUGGLE
---

# 🟢 INTRODUCTION TO FREEDOM STRUGGLE

The Indian Freedom Struggle was a long and continuous movement against British colonial rule that lasted for nearly 200 years. It involved political, social, and economic resistance by Indians to achieve independence. The struggle was not a single event but a series of movements led by different leaders, organizations, and ideologies. The ultimate goal was to end British rule and establish a sovereign, democratic India, which was achieved on 15 August 1947.

The freedom struggle can be divided into three major phases: early resistance (1757–1857), the rise of nationalism (1857–1919), and the mass struggle phase (1919–1947).

---

# 🟡 EARLY RESISTANCE AGAINST BRITISH (1757–1857)

The British East India Company established control over India after the Battle of Plassey in 1757. Early resistance movements were mainly local and unorganized, led by regional rulers and tribal groups.

Important resistances include:

* Tipu Sultan of Mysore fought against British expansion
* Marathas resisted British control in western India
* Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi led heroic resistance during 1857 revolt
* Tribal revolts like Santhal rebellion and Munda uprising

---

## 🔴 THE REVOLT OF 1857 (FIRST WAR OF INDEPENDENCE)

The Revolt of 1857 is considered the first major organized uprising against British rule. It started as a sepoy mutiny in Meerut and spread across northern India.

### CAUSES:

* Political annexation policies (Doctrine of Lapse)
* Economic exploitation
* Social and religious interference
* Military grievances

### IMPORTANT LEADERS:

* Rani Lakshmibai
* Nana Sahib
* Mangal Pandey
* Bahadur Shah Zafar

### OUTCOME:

* Revolt was suppressed
* Company rule ended
* British Crown took direct control (1858)

---

# 🟢 RISE OF NATIONALISM (1857–1919)

After 1857, Indians realized the need for organized political struggle. This led to the formation of the Indian National Congress (INC) in 1885.

---

## 🟡 INDIAN NATIONAL CONGRESS (INC)

INC was founded by A.O. Hume in 1885. It became the main platform for the freedom struggle.

### MODERATE PHASE (1885–1905)

Leaders believed in petitions and constitutional reforms.

* Dadabhai Naoroji
* Gopal Krishna Gokhale
* Surendranath Banerjee

### METHODS:

* Petitions
* Meetings
* Reforms through British rule

---

## 🔵 EXTREMIST PHASE (1905–1919)

Extremists believed in direct action and mass movements.

### LEADERS:

* Bal Gangadhar Tilak
* Lala Lajpat Rai
* Bipin Chandra Pal

### METHODS:

* Swadeshi movement
* Boycott of British goods
* National education

---

# 🟣 GANDHIAN ERA (1919–1947)

The arrival of Mahatma Gandhi marked a new phase of mass-based freedom struggle.

---

## 🟡 NON-COOPERATION MOVEMENT (1920–1922)

Launched after Jallianwala Bagh massacre.

### FEATURES:

* Boycott of British goods
* Surrender of titles
* Non-cooperation with British institutions

---

## 🔴 CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE MOVEMENT (1930)

### SALT MARCH:

Gandhi marched to Dandi to break Salt Law.

### FEATURES:

* Violation of salt laws
* Boycott of foreign goods
* Mass participation

---

## ⚫ QUIT INDIA MOVEMENT (1942)

Launched during World War II.

### SLOGAN:

“Do or Die”

### FEATURES:

* Mass protests
* Arrest of leaders
* Underground movements

---

# 🟠 REVOLUTIONARY MOVEMENT

Some Indians believed armed struggle was necessary.

### IMPORTANT REVOLUTIONARIES:

* Bhagat Singh
* Chandrashekhar Azad
* Rajguru & Sukhdev
* Subhas Chandra Bose

---

# 🔵 ROLE OF SUBHAS CHANDRA BOSE

* Founded INA (Indian National Army)
* Slogan: “Give me blood, I will give you freedom”
* Allied with Japan during WWII

---

# 🟣 PARTITION OF INDIA (1947)

India was partitioned into India and Pakistan based on religious lines. This led to mass migration and communal violence.
