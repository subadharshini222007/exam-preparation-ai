
### 1. Introduction to Indian Culture

Indian culture is one of the oldest and most diverse cultures in the world, developed over thousands of years. It is a combination of traditions, beliefs, customs, languages, arts, and lifestyles followed by people across India. Unity in diversity is the core feature of Indian culture, where people of different religions, languages, and traditions coexist peacefully. Indian culture has been influenced by ancient civilizations like the Indus Valley Civilization, Vedic culture, and later interactions with various foreign cultures.

**Quick Revision Points:**

* Oldest continuous culture in the world
* Unity in Diversity
* Influenced by multiple civilizations

---

### 2. Features of Indian Culture

Indian culture is characterized by continuity, adaptability, tolerance, and spirituality. It has maintained its traditions while adapting to changes over time. Respect for elders, strong family values, religious tolerance, and community living are important features.

**Quick Revision Points:**

# INDIAN ART AND ARCHITECTURE

---

## 1. Introduction to Indian Art and Architecture

Indian art and architecture reflect the cultural, religious, and social values of Indian society from ancient to modern times. It evolved through different periods such as the Indus Valley Civilization, Mauryan, Gupta, Medieval, and Modern periods. Art includes painting, sculpture, and crafts, while architecture includes temples, stupas, mosques, forts, and palaces. Religion played a major role in shaping Indian architecture.

**Quick Revision Points:**

- Art = Expression, Architecture = Structure
- Religion-based development
- Evolution across historical periods

---

## 2. Indus Valley Civilization Architecture

The Indus Valley Civilization (Harappa, Mohenjo-Daro) had advanced urban planning with grid layouts, drainage systems, and brick houses. The Great Bath is an important structure showing engineering skills.

**Quick Revision Points:**

- Planned cities
- Drainage system
- Great Bath

---

## 3. Mauryan Architecture

Mauryan period (Ashoka) is known for pillars, stupas, and rock-cut caves. The Ashokan pillars with lion capital are important symbols.

**Quick Revision Points:**

- Ashoka pillars
- Stupas (Sanchi)
- Rock-cut caves

---

## 4. Buddhist Architecture

Buddhist architecture includes stupas, viharas, and chaityas. Stupas are dome-shaped structures containing relics of Buddha. Ajanta and Ellora caves contain Buddhist paintings and sculptures.

**Quick Revision Points:**

- Stupa = relic structure
- Ajanta paintings
- Chaitya halls

---

## 5. Rock-Cut Architecture

Rock-cut architecture involves carving structures out of rocks. Examples include Ajanta, Ellora, and Elephanta caves.

**Quick Revision Points:**

- Carved from rocks
- Religious purpose
- Cave temples

---

## 6. Temple Architecture in India

Indian temples are broadly classified into:

- **Dravidian style (South India)**
- **Nagara style (North India)**
- **Vesara style (Mixed)**

Temples consist of Garbhagriha (sanctum), Mandapa (hall), and Shikhara (tower).

**Quick Revision Points:**

- Dravidian = Pyramid tower
- Nagara = Curved tower
- Vesara = Mixed

---

## 7. Dravidian Temple Architecture

Found mainly in South India (Tamil Nadu). Features include towering Gopurams, large temple complexes, and water tanks. Example: Brihadeeswarar Temple.

**Quick Revision Points:**

- Gopuram
- Temple complexes
- Tamil Nadu temples

---

## 8. Nagara Temple Architecture

Found in North India. Features include curved Shikhara and square base. Example: Khajuraho temples.

**Quick Revision Points:**

- Curved tower
- No large boundary walls

---

## 9. Vesara Architecture

Combination of Dravidian and Nagara styles. Found in Deccan region.

**Quick Revision Points:**

- Mixed style
- Deccan region

---

## 10. Indo-Islamic Architecture

Developed during Delhi Sultanate and Mughal period. Features include domes, arches, minarets, and calligraphy. Example: Taj Mahal.
---

# RELIGION-WISE CULTURE IN INDIA

---

## 1. HINDUISM

### Introduction

Hinduism is one of the oldest religions in the world, often referred to as *Sanatana Dharma*. It has no single founder and evolved over thousands of years. It is deeply connected with Indian culture, traditions, and way of life.

### Sacred Texts

The main sacred texts of Hinduism include:

- Vedas (Rig, Yajur, Sama, Atharva)
- Upanishads (philosophical teachings)
- Bhagavad Gita (spiritual guide)
- Ramayana and Mahabharata (epics)

These texts guide moral values, duties, and spiritual knowledge.

### Core Beliefs

Hinduism is based on key concepts such as:

- Dharma (duty)
- Karma (action and result)
- Moksha (liberation from rebirth)
- Reincarnation (cycle of birth and death)

### Gods and Worship

Hinduism follows both monotheism and polytheism. The main gods are:

- Brahma (creator)
- Vishnu (protector)
- Shiva (destroyer)

Worship includes temple visits, prayers, rituals, and festivals.

### Cultural Practices

- Festivals: Diwali, Holi, Pongal
- Pilgrimage: Varanasi, Rameswaram
- Yoga and meditation
- Caste system (traditional social structure)

### Contribution to Culture

Hinduism influenced:

- Temple architecture
- Classical dances (Bharatanatyam)
- Music and literature
- Philosophy and ethics

**Quick Revision Points:**

- Oldest religion
- No founder
- Dharma, Karma, Moksha
- Vedas + Epics

---

## 2. BUDDHISM

### Introduction

Buddhism was founded by Gautama Buddha in the 6th century BCE. It emerged as a reaction against ritualism and caste system in Hindu society. It focuses on achieving enlightenment through simple living and meditation.

### Life of Buddha

- Born as Siddhartha in Lumbini
- Attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya
- First sermon at Sarnath
- Attained Nirvana at Kushinagar

### Core Teachings

The main teachings of Buddhism are:

- Four Noble Truths
- Eightfold Path
- Middle Path (avoid extremes)

### Key Concepts

- Nirvana (freedom from suffering)
- Ahimsa (non-violence)
- Compassion and kindness

### Sacred Texts

- Tripitaka (Vinaya, Sutta, Abhidhamma)

### Cultural Practices

- Meditation and mindfulness
- Monastic life (monks)
- Stupa worship

### Contribution to Culture

- Spread of education (Nalanda University)
- Art (Ajanta paintings)
- Architecture (Stupas)
- Promotion of equality

**Quick Revision Points:**

- Founder: Buddha
- Four Noble Truths
- Eightfold Path
- Ahimsa

---

## 3. JAINISM

### Introduction

Jainism is an ancient religion founded by Mahavira in the 6th century BCE. It emphasizes strict non-violence and self-discipline. It rejects the authority of the Vedas.

### Tirthankaras

Jainism believes in 24 Tirthankaras. The last one was Mahavira.

### Core Principles

- Ahimsa (non-violence)
- Satya (truth)
- Asteya (non-stealing)
- Brahmacharya (celibacy)
- Aparigraha (non-possession)

### Key Beliefs

- No creator god
- Liberation through self-effort
- Karma influences rebirth

### Sects

- Digambara (sky-clad monks)
- Svetambara (white-clad monks)

### Sacred Texts

- Agamas

### Cultural Practices

- Strict vegetarianism
- Fasting and penance
- Simple living

### Contribution to Culture

- Promotion of non-violence
- Influence on Indian philosophy
- Development of literature and art

**Quick Revision Points:**

- Founder: Mahavira
- 24 Tirthankaras
- Ahimsa (most important)
- No creator god
