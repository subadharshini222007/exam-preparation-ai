## 📌 1. Introduction to Demand

Demand is one of the fundamental concepts in economics that refers to the quantity of a commodity that consumers are willing and able to purchase at various prices during a given period of time. It is not merely a desire for a product, but an effective demand backed by purchasing power.

In the context of competitive examinations like TNPSC Group 1, understanding demand is crucial as it forms the basis for analyzing consumer behavior, price determination, and market equilibrium. Demand reflects human wants, which are unlimited, while resources are limited, leading to the need for rational allocation.

---

## 📌 2. Determinants of Demand

Demand for a commodity is influenced by several factors apart from its price. These determinants play a crucial role in shaping the demand curve.

### 🔹 1. Price of the Commodity

The most important determinant of demand is the price of the commodity itself. Generally, there exists an inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.

### 🔹 2. Income of Consumers

When consumer income increases, demand for normal goods increases, while demand for inferior goods decreases.

### 🔹 3. Prices of Related Goods

* Substitute goods (Tea vs Coffee)
* Complementary goods (Car and Petrol)

### 🔹 4. Tastes and Preferences

Consumer preferences influenced by fashion, trends, and advertising significantly affect demand.

### 🔹 5. Expectations of Future Prices

If consumers expect prices to rise in the future, current demand increases.

### 🔹 6. Population

An increase in population leads to an increase in demand.

---

## 📌 3. Law of Demand

The law of demand states that, **ceteris paribus (other things remaining constant)**, the quantity demanded of a good decreases when its price increases, and vice versa.

This inverse relationship is represented through a downward-sloping demand curve.

### 🔹 Assumptions of Law of Demand

* Income remains constant
* Tastes and preferences remain unchanged
* Prices of related goods are constant

---

## 📌 4. Demand Schedule and Demand Curve

A demand schedule is a tabular representation showing the relationship between price and quantity demanded.

### 🔹 Types of Demand Schedule

#### 1. Individual Demand Schedule

Represents demand of a single consumer.

#### 2. Market Demand Schedule

Represents total demand of all consumers in the market.

---

### 🔹 Demand Curve

The demand curve is a graphical representation of the demand schedule. It slopes downward from left to right, indicating the inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.

---

## 📌 5. Reasons for Downward Sloping Demand Curve

### 🔹 Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility

As consumption increases, additional satisfaction decreases, so consumers buy more only at lower prices.

### 🔹 Income Effect

When price falls, real income increases, leading to higher demand.

### 🔹 Substitution Effect

Consumers substitute cheaper goods for expensive ones.

---

## 📌 6. Exceptions to Law of Demand

In certain cases, the law of demand does not hold true.

### 🔹 Giffen Goods

Inferior goods where demand increases even when price rises.

### 🔹 Veblen Goods

Luxury goods where higher prices make them more desirable.

### 🔹 Speculative Goods

Assets like gold or stocks where expectations influence demand.

---

## 📌 7. Types of Demand

### 🔹 Price Demand

Demand for a commodity at different prices.

### 🔹 Income Demand

Demand changes with income.

### 🔹 Cross Demand

Demand for a commodity in relation to price of another good.

---

## 📌 8. Elasticity of Demand (Introduction)

Elasticity of demand measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in price.

### 🔹 Types

* Price Elasticity
* Income Elasticity
* Cross Elasticity

Elasticity is important for pricing decisions and tax policies.

# 📊 SUPPLY – CORE CONCEPTS

---

## 📌 9. Introduction to Supply

Supply refers to the quantity of a commodity that producers are willing and able to offer for sale at various prices during a given period of time.

Supply depends on production decisions of firms and is influenced by cost, technology, and expectations.

---

## 📌 10. Determinants of Supply

### 🔹 1. Price of the Commodity

Higher price → Higher supply

### 🔹 2. Cost of Production

Higher costs reduce supply.

### 🔹 3. Technology

Improved technology increases supply.

### 🔹 4. Government Policies

Taxes reduce supply, subsidies increase supply.

### 🔹 5. Number of Producers

More producers → More supply.

---

## 📌 11. Law of Supply

The law of supply states that, **other things remaining constant**, quantity supplied increases with increase in price.

---

## 📌 12. Supply Curve

The supply curve is upward sloping, indicating a direct relationship between price and quantity supplied.

---

## 📌 13. Elasticity of Supply

Elasticity of supply measures responsiveness of supply to price changes.

### 🔹 Types

* Elastic supply
* Inelastic supply

---

## 📌 15. Elasticity of Demand (Detailed Concept)

Elasticity of demand refers to the degree of responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in one of its determinants, especially price. It helps economists understand how sensitive consumers are to price changes.

Elasticity is a crucial concept in pricing policy, taxation, and production planning. If demand is highly elastic, even a small change in price leads to a significant change in quantity demanded.

---

## 📌 16. Types of Price Elasticity of Demand

### 🔹 1. Perfectly Elastic Demand

Demand is perfectly elastic when a very small change in price leads to an infinite change in quantity demanded. The demand curve is horizontal.

### 🔹 2. Perfectly Inelastic Demand

Demand is perfectly inelastic when quantity demanded does not change at all despite changes in price. The demand curve is vertical.

### 🔹 3. Relatively Elastic Demand

A small change in price leads to a large change in demand. Usually seen in luxury goods.

### 🔹 4. Relatively Inelastic Demand

Demand changes very little even when price changes. Common in essential goods.

### 🔹 5. Unitary Elastic Demand

Percentage change in quantity demanded is equal to percentage change in price.

---

## 📌 17. Measurement of Elasticity of Demand

Elasticity can be measured using different methods:

### 🔹 1. Percentage Method

Elasticity (Ed) =
Percentage Change in Quantity Demanded / Percentage Change in Price

This is the most widely used method in theoretical analysis.

---

### 🔹 2. Total Expenditure Method

* If total expenditure increases when price falls → Elastic demand
* If total expenditure decreases → Inelastic demand
* If no change → Unitary elasticity

---

### 🔹 3. Point Elasticity

Used to measure elasticity at a specific point on the demand curve.

---

### 🔹 4. Arc Elasticity

Measures elasticity between two points on the demand curve.

---

## 📌 18. Factors Affecting Elasticity of Demand

### 🔹 Nature of Commodity

Necessities have inelastic demand, luxuries have elastic demand.

### 🔹 Availability of Substitutes

More substitutes → More elastic demand.

### 🔹 Income Level

Higher income → More elastic demand.

### 🔹 Time Period

Demand becomes more elastic in the long run.

---

## 📌 19. Income Elasticity of Demand

Income elasticity measures the responsiveness of demand to changes in income.

### 🔹 Types

* Positive (Normal goods)
* Negative (Inferior goods)
* Zero (No change with income)

This helps in understanding consumption patterns as income changes.

---

## 📌 20. Cross Elasticity of Demand

Cross elasticity measures how demand for one good changes when the price of another good changes.

### 🔹 Types

* Positive (Substitutes)
* Negative (Complements)

---

## 📌 21. Demand Forecasting (Advanced Concept)

Demand forecasting refers to predicting future demand based on past data and analysis.

### 🔹 Methods

* Survey method
* Statistical method
* Trend analysis

### 🔹 Importance

* Helps in production planning
* Reduces uncertainty
* Improves decision-making

---

## 📌 22. Consumer Surplus

Consumer surplus is the difference between what a consumer is willing to pay and what they actually pay.

It represents the extra satisfaction or benefit gained by consumers.

---

## 📌 23. Advanced Demand Concepts

### 🔹 Derived Demand

Demand for a good that arises from demand for another good (e.g., labor demand depends on demand for output).

### 🔹 Joint Demand

Demand for goods used together (e.g., car and petrol).

### 🔹 Composite Demand

A good used for multiple purposes (e.g., electricity).

## 📌 31. Meaning of Market Equilibrium

Market equilibrium refers to a situation in which the quantity demanded by consumers is exactly equal to the quantity supplied by producers at a particular price. This price is known as the equilibrium price, and the corresponding quantity is called equilibrium quantity.

At equilibrium, there is no tendency for price to change because the market is in a state of balance. Buyers are willing to purchase exactly the amount that sellers are willing to sell, ensuring stability in the market.

---

## 📌 32. Determination of Equilibrium Price

Equilibrium price is determined by the interaction of demand and supply forces in the market.

When demand equals supply:

👉 Quantity Demanded (Qd) = Quantity Supplied (Qs)

### 🔹 Case 1: Excess Demand (Shortage)

When quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied at a given price:

* There is a shortage in the market
* Consumers compete to buy limited goods
* Price tends to rise

This rise in price continues until equilibrium is restored.

---

### 🔹 Case 2: Excess Supply (Surplus)

When quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded:

* There is surplus stock
* Sellers reduce prices to attract buyers
* Price falls

This fall continues until equilibrium is achieved.

---

## 📌 33. Equilibrium Schedule (Example)

| Price | Demand | Supply |
| ----- | ------ | ------ |
| 10    | 100    | 50     |
| 20    | 80     | 80     |
| 30    | 60     | 100    |

👉 At price = 20 → Demand = Supply = 80
👉 So equilibrium price = 20

---

## 📌 34. Graphical Representation (Theory)

In a demand-supply graph:

* Demand curve slopes downward
* Supply curve slopes upward
* Their intersection point = Equilibrium

This intersection point shows the market-clearing price.

---

## 📌 35. Changes in Equilibrium

Equilibrium is not fixed; it changes when demand or supply shifts.

---

## 📌 36. Shift in Demand

When demand increases:

* Demand curve shifts right
* Price increases
* Quantity increases

When demand decreases:

* Demand curve shifts left
* Price decreases
* Quantity decreases

### 🔹 Causes of Demand Shift

* Increase in income
* Change in taste
* Population growth
* Rise in price of substitutes

---

## 📌 37. Shift in Supply

When supply increases:

* Supply curve shifts right
* Price decreases
* Quantity increases

When supply decreases:

* Supply curve shifts left
* Price increases
* Quantity decreases

### 🔹 Causes of Supply Shift

* Technological improvement
* Reduction in cost
* Government subsidies

---

## 📌 38. Simultaneous Changes in Demand and Supply

In real-world markets, both demand and supply may change simultaneously.

### 🔹 Case 1: Demand ↑ and Supply ↑

* Quantity increases
* Price may increase or decrease depending on magnitude

### 🔹 Case 2: Demand ↓ and Supply ↓

* Quantity decreases
* Price uncertain

This concept is important for analytical questions in TNPSC exams.

---

## 📌 39. Price Mechanism (Invisible Hand Concept)

Price mechanism refers to the system where prices adjust automatically due to demand and supply forces.

This concept was introduced by **Adam Smith**, who described it as the “invisible hand” guiding the economy.

### 🔹 Importance

* Allocates resources efficiently
* Balances demand and supply
* Promotes economic efficiency

---

## 📌 40. Role of Time in Equilibrium

Time plays a crucial role in determining equilibrium.

### 🔹 Market Period

Very short period → supply is fixed → price fluctuates based on demand

### 🔹 Short Run

Supply can adjust slightly

### 🔹 Long Run

Full adjustment of supply → stable equilibrium

---

## 📌 41. Cobweb Model (Advanced Concept)

Cobweb theory explains price fluctuations in markets where production takes time (like agriculture).

### 🔹 Explanation

* Farmers decide production based on previous prices
* This leads to cycles of high and low prices

### 🔹 Types

* Convergent cobweb
* Divergent cobweb
* Continuous fluctuation

This is an important analytical concept for higher-level exams.

---

## 📌 42. Government Intervention in Price

Sometimes the government intervenes to control prices.

---

### 🔹 Price Ceiling (Maximum Price)

* Set below equilibrium price
* Leads to shortage

Example: Essential commodities control

---

### 🔹 Price Floor (Minimum Price)

* Set above equilibrium price
* Leads to surplus

Example: Minimum Support Price (MSP)

---

## 📌 43. Rationing System

When price ceiling causes shortage, government may introduce rationing.

### 🔹 Purpose

* Ensure fair distribution
* Prevent black marketing

---

## 📌 44. Buffer Stock Policy

Government maintains stock of essential goods.

### 🔹 Objectives

* Stabilize prices
* Prevent scarcity

---

## 📌 45. Market Equilibrium in Real Life (India Context)

In India:

* Agricultural markets often face price fluctuations
* MSP ensures farmers get minimum price
* Government intervenes to stabilize food prices

---

## 📌 46. Disequilibrium

Disequilibrium occurs when demand ≠ supply.

### 🔹 Types

* Shortage
* Surplus

Market forces naturally push towards equilibrium.

---

## 📌 47. Importance of Equilibrium

* Ensures efficient allocation
* Stabilizes market
* Helps in decision making
