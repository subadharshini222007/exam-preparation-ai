
1. Synonyms

A synonym is a word that has the same or nearly the same meaning as another word in the same language. In TNPSC exams, synonym questions test whether a candidate can identify a word closest in meaning to a given word, often among options that include near-misses. Building a strong synonym base helps in both the vocabulary section and in improving comprehension speed during the exam.
Abundant — existing in large quantities; plentiful. Example: The region has an abundant supply of natural resources.
Candid — truthful and straightforward; frank. Example: She gave a candid opinion about the new policy.
Diligent — showing care and effort in one's work or duties. Example: He is a diligent student who never misses his studies.
Eloquent — fluent and persuasive in speaking or writing. Example: The minister delivered an eloquent speech on rural development.
Frugal — sparing or economical with money or food. Example: Being frugal helped the family save for their daughter's education.
Genuine — truly what it is said to be; authentic. Example: The officer verified that the documents were genuine.
Hostile — unfriendly; antagonistic. Example: The two neighbouring villages had a hostile relationship over land disputes.
Immense — extremely large or great. Example: The project required an immense amount of planning.
Meticulous — showing great attention to detail; very careful. Example: The auditor was meticulous while checking the accounts.
Novice — a person new to a field or activity; beginner. Example: As a novice teacher, she sought guidance from senior staff.

2. Antonyms
An antonym is a word that means the opposite of another word. Antonym-based questions in TNPSC exams check a candidate's grasp over contrasting shades of meaning, which is useful not only for direct questions but also for understanding passages where opposite ideas are compared.
Abundant × Scarce — Scarce means insufficient in supply; rare. Example: Clean drinking water is scarce in that drought-hit district.
Candid × Evasive — Evasive means tending to avoid commitment or directness. Example: His evasive answers raised suspicion among the committee members.
Diligent × Lazy — Lazy means unwilling to work or use energy. Example: A lazy approach to preparation rarely yields good exam results.
Genuine × Fake — Fake means not genuine; a forgery or imitation. Example: The police seized a large stock of fake currency notes.
Hostile × Amicable — Amicable means characterized by friendliness and absence of discord. Example: The dispute was settled in an amicable manner.
Immense × Minute — Minute means extremely small. Example: Even a minute error in the calculation can change the result.
Optimistic × Pessimistic — Pessimistic means expecting the worst outcome. Example: Despite the setback, he remained optimistic rather than pessimistic about the project.
Transparent × Opaque — Opaque means not able to be seen through; unclear. Example: The new glass used in the building is opaque for privacy.
