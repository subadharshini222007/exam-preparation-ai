# 📘 POLITY – CONSTITUTION OF INDIA 

---

## 1. Historical Background of the Indian Constitution

The Constitution of India did not emerge suddenly; it is the result of a long historical evolution shaped by colonial administration, freedom struggle, and nationalist aspirations. The foundation was laid during British rule through various Acts such as the Regulating Act of 1773, Pitt’s India Act of 1784, Charter Acts, and most importantly the Government of India Act, 1935, which provided the structural framework for federal governance, provincial autonomy, and administrative organization. The demand for a Constituent Assembly was first officially raised by M.N. Roy in 1934, and later supported by the Indian National Congress. The Constituent Assembly was formed in 1946 under the Cabinet Mission Plan. After India gained independence through the Indian Independence Act of 1947, the Assembly became sovereign and undertook the task of drafting the Constitution. The Constitution was adopted on November 26, 1949, and came into force on January 26, 1950, marking the birth of the Republic of India. It reflects the ideals of justice, liberty, equality, and fraternity.

---

## 2. Making of the Constitution

The drafting of the Constitution was a meticulous and comprehensive process involving debates, discussions, and deliberations. The Constituent Assembly consisted of 299 members representing various regions and communities. The Drafting Committee was chaired by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, who is regarded as the chief architect of the Constitution. Other important members included Alladi Krishnaswamy Iyer, K.M. Munshi, and Gopalaswami Ayyangar. The drafting process took nearly 2 years, 11 months, and 18 days, during which the Assembly held 11 sessions and debated every provision in detail. The Constitution is one of the lengthiest written constitutions in the world, originally containing 395 Articles and 8 Schedules. It incorporated features from various constitutions across the world, adapting them to suit Indian conditions. The final document represents a balance between rigidity and flexibility, tradition and modernity, and individual rights and social justice.

---

## 3. Salient Features of the Constitution

The Constitution of India is unique in its structure and content. It is the longest written constitution in the world, ensuring detailed provisions for governance. It establishes India as a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic. It provides for a parliamentary form of government, where the executive is responsible to the legislature. It introduces a federal system with a strong central government. The Constitution guarantees Fundamental Rights to citizens while also laying down Directive Principles of State Policy to guide governance. It includes provisions for an independent judiciary, single citizenship, universal adult franchise, and emergency provisions. The Constitution also provides for flexibility through amendments under Article 368. These features ensure stability, unity, and democratic functioning of the country.

---

## 4. Preamble of the Constitution

The Preamble is the introductory statement of the Constitution, reflecting its philosophy and objectives. It begins with the words “We, the People of India,” emphasizing that the Constitution derives its authority from the people. It declares India to be a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic. It aims to secure justice (social, economic, political), liberty (of thought, expression, belief, faith, and worship), equality (of status and opportunity), and fraternity (ensuring dignity and unity). The Preamble is not enforceable by courts, but it serves as a guiding principle for interpretation. The Supreme Court has held that the Preamble is part of the Constitution and reflects its basic structure.

---

## 5. Union and its Territory

The Constitution defines India as a “Union of States,” emphasizing the indestructible nature of the Union. Articles 1 to 4 deal with the territory of India, including states, union territories, and acquired territories. Parliament has the power to reorganize states, alter boundaries, and create new states. This flexibility has allowed India to adapt to regional demands and administrative needs over time.

---

## 6. Citizenship

Citizenship provisions are covered under Articles 5 to 11. The Constitution initially provided citizenship based on domicile, birth, descent, and migration. Parliament has the authority to regulate citizenship through laws such as the Citizenship Act, 1955. Citizenship ensures political rights and participation in governance.

---

## 7. Fundamental Rights

Fundamental Rights are essential for the development of individuals and democracy. They are enshrined in Part III (Articles 12–35). These rights include Right to Equality, Right to Freedom, Right against Exploitation, Right to Freedom of Religion, Cultural and Educational Rights, and Right to Constitutional Remedies. These rights are justiciable, meaning they can be enforced by courts. The Supreme Court acts as the guardian of Fundamental Rights. However, these rights are not absolute and can be reasonably restricted in the interest of public order, morality, and national security.

---

## 8. Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)

Directive Principles are contained in Part IV (Articles 36–51). They are non-justiciable guidelines for the government to establish a welfare state. These principles aim to promote social and economic democracy. They include provisions for equal pay, free education, public health, and rural development. Though not enforceable, they are fundamental in governance.

---

## 9. Fundamental Duties

Fundamental Duties were added by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976. These duties are moral obligations of citizens to promote harmony, protect the environment, respect the Constitution, and uphold national unity. Though not enforceable by law, they play a crucial role in strengthening democracy.

---

## 10. Structure of Government

The Constitution establishes three organs of government: Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary. The Legislature makes laws, the Executive implements them, and the Judiciary interprets them. This separation ensures checks and balances and prevents misuse of power.

---

## 11. Federal System with Unitary Bias

India follows a federal structure with a strong central government. Powers are divided between the Union and the States through Union List, State List, and Concurrent List. However, during emergencies, the system becomes unitary.

---

## 12. Parliamentary System

India follows the parliamentary form of government where the Prime Minister is the head of the government and the President is the nominal head. The executive is responsible to the legislature, ensuring accountability.

---

## 13. Independent Judiciary

The judiciary in India is independent and plays a vital role in protecting the Constitution. The Supreme Court is the highest court, followed by High Courts and subordinate courts. Judicial review ensures that laws and actions of the government are constitutional.

---

## 14. Amendment of the Constitution

The Constitution provides a flexible amendment process under Article 368. Amendments can be made by Parliament with special majority and, in some cases, ratification by states.

---

## 15. Emergency Provisions

Emergency provisions enable the government to deal with extraordinary situations such as war, external aggression, or internal disturbances. During emergencies, the central government gains more power.

---

## 16. Basic Structure Doctrine

The Basic Structure Doctrine, evolved by the Supreme Court, ensures that certain features of the Constitution cannot be altered by amendments. These include democracy, rule of law, and judicial independence.

1.PRESIDENT OF INDIA

The President of India is the constitutional head of the country and the first citizen of India. The office of the President is established under Article 52 of the Constitution. Though the real executive power lies with the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister, all executive actions are carried out in the name of the President. The President acts as the nominal executive but plays a crucial role in maintaining the constitutional framework. The President is elected indirectly by an electoral college consisting of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of State Legislative Assemblies. The election is conducted using proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote system. The President holds office for a term of five years and is eligible for re-election.

Powers of the President

The powers of the President can be classified into executive, legislative, judicial, diplomatic, military, and emergency powers. Executive powers include appointing the Prime Minister, Council of Ministers, Governors, Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts, and other high officials. Legislative powers include summoning and proroguing Parliament, dissolving the Lok Sabha, and giving assent to bills. The President can also issue ordinances when Parliament is not in session. Judicial powers include granting pardons, reprieves, and commutations. As the Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces, the President has military powers. Diplomatic powers include representing India internationally.

Emergency Powers

The President has the authority to declare National Emergency, State Emergency (President’s Rule), and Financial Emergency under specific conditions. During emergencies, the central government gains significant powers, and the federal structure becomes more unitary.

Role and Importance

Though largely ceremonial, the President ensures constitutional stability, acts as a guardian of democracy, and plays a balancing role during political uncertainty.


2.PARLIAMENT OF INDIA

The Parliament of India is the supreme legislative body of the country and represents the will of the people. It consists of the President and two Houses: the Lok Sabha (House of the People) and the Rajya Sabha (Council of States). The Lok Sabha is the lower house and represents the people directly, while the Rajya Sabha represents the states. The Parliament performs functions such as law-making, controlling the executive, financial control, and constitutional amendment.

Lok Sabha

The Lok Sabha consists of directly elected representatives of the people. The maximum strength is 552 members. Members serve for a term of five years unless dissolved earlier. The Lok Sabha has more powers compared to the Rajya Sabha, especially in financial matters.

Rajya Sabha

The Rajya Sabha is the upper house and represents the states. It is a permanent body and is not subject to dissolution. One-third of its members retire every two years. The maximum strength is 250 members.

Functions of Parliament

Parliament makes laws, controls the executive through questions and debates, approves budgets, and ensures accountability. It also has the power to amend the Constitution.

3.PRIME MINISTER OF INDIA

The Prime Minister is the real executive head of the Indian government and the most powerful political authority in the country. The Prime Minister is appointed by the President but must have the support of the majority in the Lok Sabha. The Prime Minister is the leader of the Council of Ministers and plays a central role in governance and policy-making.

Powers and Functions

The Prime Minister selects and allocates portfolios to ministers, presides over cabinet meetings, and coordinates the functioning of various ministries. The Prime Minister acts as a link between the President and the Council of Ministers. He/she advises the President on important matters, including the dissolution of the Lok Sabha. The Prime Minister also represents India in international forums.

Role in Governance

The Prime Minister is responsible for decision-making, implementation of policies, and maintaining political stability. The office ensures effective leadership and smooth functioning of the government.

4.SUPREME COURT OF INDIA

The Supreme Court of India is the highest judicial authority in the country and acts as the guardian of the Constitution. It was established under Article 124 of the Constitution. The Supreme Court consists of the Chief Justice of India and other judges appointed by the President.

Jurisdiction of Supreme Court

The Supreme Court has original, appellate, and advisory jurisdiction. Original jurisdiction deals with disputes between the Centre and States. Appellate jurisdiction includes hearing appeals from lower courts. Advisory jurisdiction allows the President to seek the Court’s opinion.

Judicial Review and Activism

The Supreme Court has the power of judicial review, which allows it to examine the constitutionality of laws and government actions. It also plays an active role in protecting Fundamental Rights through Public Interest Litigation (PIL).

Importance

The Supreme Court ensures rule of law, protects citizens’ rights, and maintains the supremacy of the Constitution. It is a key pillar of Indian democracy.


5.CHIEF MINISTER OF A STATE

The Chief Minister is the real executive head of a state government in India. The Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor, but must have majority support in the State Legislative Assembly. The Chief Minister leads the Council of Ministers and is responsible for the administration of the state.

Powers and Functions

The Chief Minister selects ministers and allocates portfolios. He/she presides over cabinet meetings and coordinates the functioning of various departments. The Chief Minister advises the Governor on important matters and plays a key role in policy-making and implementation at the state level. The Chief Minister also represents the state in interactions with the central government.

Importance in State Governance

The Chief Minister is the central figure in state politics and administration. The effectiveness of state governance largely depends on the leadership of the Chief Minister.


6.HIGH COURT IN INDIA

The High Court is the highest judicial body at the state level and is subordinate only to the Supreme Court of India. Every state or group of states has a High Court. The High Courts are established under Article 214 of the Constitution.

Jurisdiction of High Court

The High Court has original, appellate, and supervisory jurisdiction. It can hear cases related to civil and criminal matters and appeals from lower courts. High Courts also have the power of judicial review to ensure that laws and executive actions are constitutional.

Powers and Functions

High Courts issue writs under Article 226 for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights and other legal rights. They supervise lower courts and ensure proper functioning of the judiciary within the state.

Importance

High Courts play a crucial role in delivering justice at the state level and act as a protector of constitutional rights.

7.GOVERNOR OF A STATE

The Governor is the constitutional head of a state in India and acts as the representative of the President at the state level. The office of the Governor is defined under Article 153 of the Constitution. Although the Governor is the nominal executive head of the state, real executive powers are exercised by the Chief Minister and the Council of Ministers. The Governor is appointed by the President of India and holds office during the pleasure of the President, usually for a term of five years.

Powers of Governor

The Governor has executive, legislative, judicial, and discretionary powers. Executive powers include appointing the Chief Minister and other ministers, Advocate General, and State Election Commissioner. Legislative powers include summoning and proroguing the State Legislative Assembly and giving assent to bills. The Governor can reserve certain bills for the consideration of the President. Judicial powers include the power to grant pardons in cases under state law. Discretionary powers allow the Governor to act independently in special situations, such as recommending President’s Rule.

Role in State Administration

The Governor acts as a link between the state and the central government. In normal times, the Governor acts on the advice of the Council of Ministers, but in exceptional situations, discretionary powers become important.
